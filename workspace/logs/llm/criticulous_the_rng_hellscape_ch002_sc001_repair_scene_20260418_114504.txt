PROSE:
Artie, now safe from Vane behind the shimmering green barrier of the safe zone, began browsing the crowded market stalls with Fizz floating cheerfully by his shoulder. The transition from the low-resolution pixelated trees of the Tutorial Forest to the bustling, high-fidelity cobblestone streets of Oakhaven Market was jarring. The air here smelled heavily of roasted mutton, woodsmoke, and the sharp tang of hot iron. 

Artie leaned against a wooden post, his chest heaving, his pale skin slick with cold sweat. He ran a trembling hand through his messy brown hair, trying to slow his racing heart. His skinny-fat build was entirely unsuited for sprinting for his life, let alone dodging a sword-wielding maniac. 

"You did great!" Fizz chirped. The tiny system entity hovered at his eye level, her neon pink hair glowing softly in the shadows of the market awning. She did a little mid-air loop, her translucent skin leaving a faint trail of magenta light. "Evasion is a perfectly valid early-game strategy! Plus, you made it to the hub city without dying. Statistically, thirty percent of new recruits get eaten by the tutorial wolves."

"I have one hit point, Fizz," Artie wheezed, staring at the glowing blue text that stubbornly hovered in the top left corner of his vision.

[HP: 1/1]

"Just one," he muttered, his voice cracking. "A stiff breeze could kill me. A bad case of heartburn could kill me. If I trip on these cobblestones and scrape my knee, my heart will instantly stop."

"Well, technically, environmental damage is calculated differently," Fizz said, tapping her chin with a tiny finger. Her digital blue eyes sparkled with unhelpful enthusiasm. "A scraped knee is only point-two damage. But since the system rounds up to the nearest whole number... yes, you would die instantly."

Artie squeezed his eyes shut. "I need armor. I need a bubble. I need a medieval tank to crawl inside of."

He pushed off the wooden post, his Employee ID Badge swinging uselessly on its neck lanyard. The plastic card felt like a cruel joke from a past life. Beside it, permanently affixed to his lapel, the cursed Participation Trophy gleamed with an obnoxious, mocking gold sheen. 

The market was a chaotic sprawl of canvas tents, wooden carts, and permanent stone storefronts. Players and NPCs mingled in the thoroughfare, haggling over glowing potions, bundles of herbs, and weapons that looked far too large to be swung by human arms. Artie ignored the potion sellers and the food vendors, his eyes locked on the unmistakable glint of polished metal near the center of the plaza. 

He approached a massive stall draped in red velvet, where suits of armor stood like headless sentinels. The merchant, a burly man with a braided beard and arms the size of tree trunks, was currently polishing a massive tower shield.

"Welcome to Ironclad Defenses!" the merchant boomed, his voice echoing over the din of the market. "You look like a man who values his internal organs staying internal! What can I get you? Plate? Chain? We've got a fresh shipment of mithril-weave tunics in the back!"

"I need the thickest, heaviest, most impenetrable thing you have," Artie said, stepping up to the counter. He pointed at a massive, overlapping suit of steel plate armor that looked like it belonged on a rhinoceros. "That. I want that."

He reached out to touch the cold metal. The moment his finger brushed the steel, a translucent blue window snapped into existence.

[Item: Juggernaut's Fortress Plate]
[Defense: +150]
[Movement Speed: -40%]
[Level Requirement: 25]
[Price: 15,000 Gold]

Artie stared at the floating text. "Level twenty-five?"

"System locks, buddy," the merchant grunted, not looking up from his shield. "Can't have a level one novice walking around in endgame gear. The system would tear your muscles off the bone just trying to lift the pauldron. You gotta earn the right to wear the heavy stuff."

"But I need it now," Artie said, panic edging into his voice. "If I don't have it, I won't survive long enough to reach level two!"

"Not my problem," the merchant said, finally looking up. He took in Artie's cheap, ill-fitting office suit, the plastic ID badge, and the glowing trophy. He snorted. "Look, pal. You want starter gear? Check the bargain bin at the end of the counter. Don't waste my time with the display models."

Artie swallowed his pride and shuffled down to the end of the stall. A large wooden crate sat on the ground, filled with mismatched leather bracers, rusted chainmail coifs, and dented helmets. It smelled like old sweat and wet dog. 

He dug through the pile, pulling out a stiff, cracked leather vest. 

[Item: Moth-Eaten Leather Tunic]
[Defense: +2]
[Level Requirement: 1]
[Price: 45 Gold]

"Two defense," Artie whispered. "Fizz, what does two defense actually do?"

"It mitigates incoming physical damage by a flat value of two, before armor penetration calculations!" Fizz supplied cheerfully. "So if a goblin hits you for five damage, you only take three!"

"And I have one HP," Artie said flatly. 

"Correct! So you would still die instantly. But your corpse would be slightly less bruised!"

Artie dropped the tunic back into the bin. "Okay. Fine. This is fine. We just need to find something that gives me a shield. A magical barrier. Something that absorbs the first hit."

He left the armor stall and began weaving through the crowd, his eyes darting from vendor to vendor. He checked a stall selling magical rings. 

[Item: Ring of the Minor Ward]
[Defense: +10 Shield Capacity]
[Level Requirement: 5]
[Price: 800 Gold]

He checked a vendor selling enchanted cloaks.

[Item: Cape of the Zephyr]
[Evasion: +5%]
[Level Requirement: 3]
[Price: 300 Gold]

Every single item that offered even a shred of survivability was locked behind a level requirement he didn't meet, or a price tag that looked like a telephone number. The system was meticulously designed to force players to grind, to take risks, to slowly build their power through incremental upgrades. It was a perfectly balanced progression curve for a normal person. 

For a man with a glitched 1 HP stat, it was a death sentence.

"There has to be something," Artie muttered, pulling at his collar. The market was starting to feel claustrophobic. The clinking of coins, the laughter of high-level players showing off their glowing weapons, the smell of roasted meat—it was all overwhelming. "A starter shield. A wooden buckler. Anything."

"You currently possess exactly zero gold," Fizz reminded him, floating down to land lightly on his shoulder. "Even if you found a level one item with a warding effect, you couldn't afford it. The slime you vaporized dropped the cursed trophy, not currency."

Artie stopped in the middle of the thoroughfare, oblivious to the annoyed players shoving past him. He reached into his right pocket and pulled out his Leather Wallet. He flipped it open. 

Inside was his driver's license, a blocked corporate credit card, three crisp twenty-dollar bills, and a punch card for a sandwich shop that was three stamps away from a free turkey club. 

He stared at the paper money. It was just paper. It had no power here. No system value. 

With a heavy sigh, he shoved the Leather Wallet back into his pocket. Artie realized his useless wallet was exactly that—useless. The standard gear sold by these merchants wasn't going to save him, and their rigid economy offered no solution to his fragility. He couldn't buy his way out of this, and he couldn't grind his way out of it either. 

"Standard merchants are a dead end," Artie said, his voice dropping to a hollow whisper. "If I step outside the safe zone, Vane kills me. If a random mob looks at me funny, I die. I can't buy armor. I can't equip armor."

"Don't despair!" Fizz said, patting his cheek with a tiny, glowing hand. "The system is vast and full of opportunities! We just need to think outside the box."

Artie looked up at the towering spires of Oakhaven, feeling smaller and more fragile than ever. "Outside the box," he repeated. "Right. Because the box is currently trying to bury me."
