PROSE:
The vault doors sealed behind them, offering a momentary reprieve from the collapsing Inner Office. The heavy stone slab ground into place with a terrifying finality, cutting off the deafening roar of falling masonry and the shrieking, glitched alarms of the administrative hub. Inside, the silence was absolute, a stark contrast to the chaos they had just escaped. The only sounds were their own ragged breathing and the erratic, metallic clicking of Unit 734.

Rhea slumped against the cool, polished stone wall, her chest heaving as she fought for oxygen. Every muscle fiber in her body screamed in protest, a chorus of lactic acid and systemic abuse. 

[Stamina: 0/150]
[Status Effect: Exhaustion (Severe) remains active.]
[Warning: Prolonged action at zero stamina will result in permanent Vitality degradation.]

She wiped a thick streak of gray dust and sweat from her forehead, wincing as her singed arm hair brushed against the rough surface of the wall. The physical toll of the exploit was catching up to her. It wasn't just the running and the fighting; it was the sheer mental weight of actively subverting a reality governed by predatory math.

In her right hand, the Administrative Residue Token pulsed with a slow, hypnotic blue light. It was roughly the size of a hockey puck, heavy, cold to the touch, and practically hummed with illicit code. She held it up, letting the system render its true value.

[Item Description: Administrative Residue Token]
[Rarity: High-Tier]
[Type: Illicit System Access Token]
[Details: A concentrated fragment of administrative authority, stripped from a defaulted overseer. Grants the bearer localized bypass privileges and immunity to standard ledger audits. Highly illegal. Possession carries a mandatory deletion penalty. Do not let them catch you with this.]

"Mandatory deletion," Rhea muttered, her lips twitching into a grim smile. "Good to know they take this seriously."

"What is it?" Vance asked. He was leaning heavily on his iron Rebar, his blistered hands trembling as they gripped the makeshift weapon. Blood seeped through his torn clothing, and his face was a mask of pale, strained agony. 

[Vance Harrow HP: 2/100]
[Status Effect: Heavy Blunt-Force Trauma remains active.]

"It's our golden ticket," Rhea said. "Or our death warrant, depending on who checks our pockets next." 

She opened her interface. The blue overlay flickered, struggling against the ambient suppression of the vault, but it held. She didn't trust her physical pockets anymore—not with something this valuable. The blazer pocket had served her well, but physical items could be pickpocketed, dropped, or destroyed by environmental damage. She dragged the glowing token into the projection, watching it dissolve into a stream of digital pixels as it transferred directly into her personal storage.

[System Notification: Item successfully bound to System Inventory.]
[Notice: Illicit item detected. Standard tracking algorithms disabled.]

She glanced at her left hand, where she still clutched the Mercy Cache Coordinates. The mythic-tier data crystal was rapidly degrading now that its purpose had been fulfilled. She quickly shoved it into her left blazer pocket alongside her other exploit items. Her pocket was becoming a very dangerous liability, a localized singularity of system-breaking contraband.

"We don't have time to admire the shiny objects," Vance rasped, coughing up a spatter of gray ash. "If this place is tied to the overseer's ledger, and the overseer is dead..."

"The node crashes," Rhea finished. "And takes the physical architecture with it."

Unit 734 hovered unsteadily nearby, a thick plume of acrid exhaust venting from its smoking chassis. Its optical lens flickered a dull, warning yellow, casting erratic shadows against the vault walls.

[Unit 734 Chassis Integrity: 34%]
[Status: Critical System Failure Imminent.]

The drone let out a series of frantic, synthesized clicks, projecting a narrow beam of light deeper into the vault. 

The interior of the Mercy Cache wasn't a sprawling cavern of gold coins or generic fantasy loot. It looked like a high-end server room fused with a medieval reliquary. Rows of glowing glass cylinders lined the walls, each containing suspended data crystals, rare crafting materials, and sealed vials of liquid. The air smelled of ozone and old paper.

"Look at this," Vance breathed, his voice barely a whisper. He pushed off his Rebar and limped toward a central pedestal. 

Surrounding the pedestal were several heavy, reinforced lockboxes. Most were sealed with red-tier administrative locks, glowing with the same oppressive crimson light as the overseer's compliance warnings. But the ambient system crash had shorted out a few of the secondary containment fields. 

Vance smashed the glass front of an open cabinet with the butt of his Rebar. The iron rod clanged loudly, the impact sending a jarring vibration up his arms, but the glass shattered into a thousand glittering pieces. He reached inside and pulled out a handful of glowing crimson vials. 

"High-tier restorative consumables," Vance said, his eyes wide as he read the invisible system prompts. He didn't wait to analyze them further; he just started shoving the vials into his right cargo pocket. "These aren't the cheap, watered-down health potions from the frontier outposts. These are pure."

"Save one for yourself," Rhea said, pushing off the wall and walking toward him. "If you drop dead before we hit the exit, I'm not carrying you."

Vance grunted, popping the cork off one of the vials with his thumb. The liquid inside was thick, almost viscous, and radiated a faint heat. He downed it in a single gulp. He gagged, his face twisting in disgust as he tossed the empty vial aside. "Tastes like battery acid and copper."

"That's the taste of high-tier alchemy," Rhea said dryly. "If it tasted like strawberries, it wouldn't be healing blunt-force trauma."

The effect was immediate and visceral. The deep, purple bruising on his face began to visibly recede, the broken capillaries knitting themselves back together beneath his skin. The sluggish bleeding from his shoulder stopped as the torn tissue fused. His breathing steadied, losing the wet, rattling edge it had carried since the foyer.

[Vance Harrow HP: 45/100]
[Status Effect: Heavy Blunt-Force Trauma removed.]

"Better," he muttered, adjusting his grip on the Rebar. "Let's grab what we can and—"

A deafening crack echoed through the vault. 

The floor violently lurched, sending Rhea stumbling into the pedestal. Dust rained down from the ceiling in thick, suffocating sheets. The glowing blue lights of the vault flickered, turning a sickly, warning red.

[System Notification: Frontier Zone Purge Initiated. Vault Structural Integrity: Critical.]
[Warning: Total Collapse in 45 Seconds.]

"So much for the momentary reprieve," Rhea said, coughing through the dust.

Unit 734 emitted a high-pitched squeal, its thrusters whining as it spun toward a heavy iron door at the far end of the room. The words FRONTIER ZONE EXIT were stenciled above it in fading yellow paint, partially obscured by a web of structural cracks. 

"Go!" Vance yelled, shoving Rhea forward. 

They bolted across the vault. The floor beneath them began to buckle, massive stone tiles cracking and tilting into the void below. Rhea pushed her exhausted legs to the absolute limit, her lungs burning as she sprinted. Every step was a negotiation with gravity as the architecture of the node actively tried to delete itself. The system wasn't just collapsing the room; it was scrubbing the physical assets from the ledger. Sections of the wall simply vanished into wireframe polygons before winking out of existence.

Another massive crack tore through the ceiling. A support beam the size of a bus detached from the roof, plummeting toward the center of the room. It smashed into the pedestal, obliterating the remaining lockboxes and sending a shockwave of debris outward. 

Rhea dove forward, hitting the ground and rolling as a chunk of masonry shattered exactly where she had been standing. Vance was right behind her, using his Rebar to deflect a falling stone that would have crushed his skull. 

[Durability: Rebar decreased to 55%.]

"The door!" Rhea screamed, scrambling to her feet. 

Unit 734 was already at the exit, its interface cable jammed into the door's control panel. Sparks flew from the drone's chassis as it forced the lock, running a brute-force decryption on a system that was actively disintegrating. The heavy iron door ground open, revealing a long, dark tunnel leading out of the frontier zone. 

"Through!" Vance roared, grabbing Rhea by the shoulder and practically throwing her through the opening. 

Rhea stumbled into the dark tunnel, catching her balance just as Vance dove through behind her. Unit 734 retracted its cable and zipped through the gap, its chassis scraping against the closing metal. 

Behind them, the vault ceiling completely gave way. The heavy iron door slammed shut, sealing off the catastrophic roar of the collapsing Mercy Cache. The resulting shockwave rattled their teeth, but the thick stone of the tunnel held.

They stood in the dark, the ground trembling beneath their feet. They had the loot. They had the evidence. And they had survived the tutorial.
