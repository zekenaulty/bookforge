PROSE:
The steering column of a 2018 sedan was not designed to occupy the same physical space as a human ribcage. 

That was the last coherent, terrestrial thought Rhea managed before the physics of a head-on collision rendered her biology obsolete. There was the deafening, catastrophic crunch of the car crash, the explosive deployment of an airbag a fraction of a second too late, and the distinct, wet sound of her own ending. The pain was absolute. It was the kind of terminal agony that left no room for panic, only the cold, mathematical certainty that her life was over. The ledger was closed. The account was zeroed out.

And then, the steering column was gone. 

The shattered windshield glass vanished from her skin. The choking scent of burning coolant and atomized engine oil was instantly replaced by the rushing, sulfurous stench of a terminal velocity drop. 

Rhea was no longer dying in a crumpled metal box on Interstate 95. She was freefalling. 

Wind screamed past her ears with the force of a physical blow, tearing the breath from her lungs. Her ruined business casual attire—a charcoal blazer and slacks that had been perfectly pressed thirty minutes ago—whipped around her slender frame like a shredded parachute. She snapped her dark brown eyes open, fighting against the blinding pressure of the air, and found herself plummeting through a bruised, violently purple sky. 

There was no up or down, only the terrifying rush of gravity asserting its dominance over a new domain. Rhea flailed, her pale skin stinging as particulate matter pelted her face. It wasn't rain. It was ash. Thick, gray flakes of it swirled in the violent updrafts, coating her messy bob and caking her eyelashes. 

Below her stretched an endless, jagged expanse of an alien landscape. It was a wasteland of shattered black glass and smoldering gray dunes, looking less like a geographical formation and more like the bottom of a colossal, neglected furnace. 

Panic, delayed by the sheer absurdity of the transition, finally clawed its way up her throat. She tried to scream, but the wind shoved the sound back down into her lungs. 

Physics was usually a strict, unimaginative accountant. You fall, you accelerate, you hit the ground, you die. Rhea’s analytical mind, trained by years of corporate contract analysis, immediately began running the numbers. Terminal velocity for a human in a sprawling freefall was roughly one hundred and twenty miles per hour. The jagged obsidian spikes rushing up to meet her offered zero structural give. The math was incredibly straightforward. She was going to die. Again. Within the span of a single minute. 

She braced herself, squeezing her eyes shut, waiting for the final, obliterating impact.

The impact came. It was a localized apocalypse. 

Rhea cratered into the side of a steep, ash-covered dune, her body colliding with a buried shelf of shattered obsidian. The kinetic energy of the drop shattered the rock beneath her, throwing up a massive plume of gray dust and black shrapnel. The sound was deafening—a concussive boom of breaking stone and snapping bone. 

By all known laws of biology, she should have been reduced to a thin, red smear against the glass. 

Instead, the universe paused, checked a hidden ledger, and applied a rounding error.

Rhea lay at the bottom of the smoking crater, her chest heaving, her vision swimming in a sea of gray and black. She couldn't breathe. Every nerve ending in her body was screaming, broadcasting a chorus of catastrophic trauma directly to her brain. Yet, the lights didn't go out. The final curtain refused to drop. 

A sharp, synthetic chime rang cleanly through the ringing in her ears. It was the pleasant, utterly detached sound of a corporate elevator arriving at the executive floor. 

A crisp, translucent blue rectangle snapped into existence directly in her line of sight.

[Status Effect: Tutorial Grace Period activated.]

[Fatal impact damage negated. HP locked at 1.]

Rhea stared at the floating text. It didn't waver in the swirling ash. It was perfectly formatted, utilizing a clean, sans-serif font that felt entirely too polite for the circumstances. 

One hit point. 

The math of surviving on exactly one hit point was a nightmare of biological suspension. It meant her body was broken, hovering at the absolute microscopic edge of death, but explicitly forbidden from crossing over. It was the sensation of bleeding out, permanently paused on the last drop. Her lungs burned. Her ribs felt like they were made of ground glass. 

She tried to sit up, desperate to assess the damage, and a lance of white-hot agony shot up her left leg, pinning her back to the ash. 

Gritting her teeth against the nausea, Rhea forced her head up just enough to look down the length of her body. A massive slab of black rock, roughly the size and weight of an engine block, rested heavily over her left shin. The obsidian debris was jagged, its edges biting into the fabric of her slacks. She was pinned. Completely, immovably trapped.

She placed her hands flat against the glassy rock beneath her, trying to find leverage, but her arms trembled and gave out. Her stamina was completely depleted, her muscles turning to water under the strain. 

[Status Effect: Pinned applied. Immobilized.]

Another blue box. Another polite notification of her absolute helplessness.

Rhea let her head fall back against the ash, her chest heaving with shallow, ragged breaths. "Okay," she rasped, her voice sounding like dry leaves scraping over stone. "Okay. Think. Inventory."

It was a ridiculous thought, a byproduct of too many late-night gaming sessions used to bleed off the stress of her corporate job. But the floating blue boxes had already established the rules of this new reality. If there was a System, there was an inventory. 

She patted her side with her right hand, her fingers trembling. Her slacks were torn, her blazer ruined, but the pockets were still intact. She slipped her hand into her blazer pocket right where she always kept her lifeline. Her fingers brushed against smooth, cracked glass and cold metal. 

She pulled it out. It was her Cracked Smartphone. 

The screen was a spiderweb of shattered glass, the casing dented from the car crash. It was dead, completely unresponsive to her thumb pressing the power button. But it was here. It had made the transition with her, surviving the fatal collision and the atmospheric drop. The tactile sensation of the broken glass against her skin was the only anchor she had to the world she had just violently departed. 

She slipped the Cracked Smartphone back into her blazer pocket, ensuring it was securely stowed. It was a useless brick of lithium and silicon, but it was *hers*. 

Before she could attempt to shift the debris on her leg again, the air in front of her face shimmered. The ambient light of the purple sky was suddenly drowned out by a harsh, glaring luminescence. 

A new system notification manifested. It was larger than the others, bordered in a pulsing, angry red, though the text itself remained that glowing blue. It didn't just hover in the air; it anchored itself to her retinas, tracking her eye movements so she couldn't look away. It obscured the ash, the crater, and the sky, demanding her absolute attention.

[Notice of Arrival: Frontier Node 74-A]

[Subject: Rhea]

[Title Acquired: Indentured Soul]

Rhea blinked, her mind snagging on the terminology. *Indentured.* The word carried a heavy, legal weight. It wasn't a heroic title. It wasn't a designation of a chosen one. It was a liability. 

The text scrolled upward, revealing the next lines of the prompt.

[HP: 1/100]

[MP: 10/10]

[Stamina: 5/50]

[Assessment Complete. Subject possesses insufficient capital to cover the metaphysical costs of resurrection and transit.]

[Soul Debt Assessed: 100,000 Credits.]

The number hung there, stark and uncompromising. One hundred thousand. Rhea’s breath hitched. She didn't know the exchange rate of this nightmare wasteland, but she knew the language of predatory lending when she saw it. The System wasn't a benevolent god granting her a second chance. It was a creditor. And it had just handed her the bill for her own survival.

The bottom of the glowing blue notification coalesced into a final, blinking command, hovering inches from her face.

[Acknowledge Indenture Status to commence mandatory labor cycle.]

[Y / N]

[Warning: Selecting 'N' or failing to acknowledge within the grace period will result in immediate asset liquidation. (Current Asset Value: 1 HP).]

Rhea stared at the prompt. The claustrophobia was absolute. She was physically pinned under hundreds of pounds of obsidian debris, her body broken and held together by a single, arbitrary hit point. And now, she was visually and legally pinned by a glowing blue terms of service agreement that held a gun to her head. 

The ash continued to fall around her, silent and indifferent. The sulfur wind howled across the lip of the crater. Rhea lay trapped in the dirt, the cold light of the indenture prompt washing over her pale face, illuminating the stark reality of her new existence. She wasn't dead. But she definitely wasn't free.
