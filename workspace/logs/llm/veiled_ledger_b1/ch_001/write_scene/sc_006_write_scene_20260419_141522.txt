PROSE:
Rhea stood by the dark pedestal, the howling sulfur wind biting through her thin blazer, holding the Raw Masonry Chunk in her right hand and the Obsidian Shard in her left. The glowing 'Accept' rune of the Starter Cache was gone, banished to the void by her explicit refusal of its predatory terms. The stone kiosk was now just a dead, geometric lump of rock jutting out of the Crater Ridge. 

She stared out over the jagged expanse of the tutorial wasteland. It was a shattered black ocean of obsidian and ash, completely devoid of life, warmth, or safety. 

[HP: 1/100]

The number floated in the upper left corner of her vision, a crisp, unwavering blue metric that defined the absolute fragility of her current existence. One hit point. A stiff breeze, a bad fall, or a particularly aggressive sneeze felt like it could shatter her into polygons. The only thing keeping her upright was the tiny, pulsing shield icon next to her health bar.

[Status: Tutorial Grace Period. Duration Remaining: 00:04:12]

Four minutes. In four minutes, the System would take the training wheels off, and the physical reality of having one percent of her biological integrity remaining would likely assert itself in the form of a massive, fatal coronary. She needed an advantage. She needed a weapon. And she absolutely refused to lease one at a compounding interest rate payable in forced labor.

Rhea lowered herself carefully to the ground, her bruised left leg protesting with a dull, throbbing ache. She sat cross-legged in the cooling ash, placing the heavy, faintly glowing Raw Masonry Chunk in her lap. It was roughly the size of a large brick, but jagged and uneven where she had violently separated it from the pedestal’s base. 

She shifted the Obsidian Shard in her left hand. The edge was molecularly thin, a natural glass scalpel born from the impact crater. 

The System was a bureaucracy. Rhea had spent six years as a forensic auditor, dissecting corporate ledgers and hunting for missing decimal points in offshore accounts. She knew how bureaucracies worked. They were rigid. They were blind to anything that didn't fit neatly into a predefined drop-down menu. The Starter Cache was a standard contract, triggered by proximity and executed via a UI prompt. 

But breaking the pedestal? That was an out-of-bounds physics interaction. The System didn't have a tax bracket for vandalism. 

She gripped the Raw Masonry Chunk with her knees and brought the Obsidian Shard down against its lower third. 

*Scrape.*

The sound was atrocious—a high-pitched, grinding squeal that vibrated straight up her forearms and rattled her teeth. It sounded like someone dragging a fork across a chalk-board made of teeth. Rhea winced, but she didn't stop. She adjusted her grip and pulled the shard across the glowing stone again, bearing down with as much of her body weight as she could safely leverage.

*Scrape. Crunch.*

A fine, glowing blue dust began to fall onto her ruined office slacks. The masonry was tough, but it wasn't invincible. The obsidian was harder. Slowly, agonizingly, she began to whittle away the rough edges of the stone block, attempting to narrow the base into something a human hand could actually wrap around.

[Status: Tutorial Grace Period. Duration Remaining: 00:03:05]

The timer ticked down. Rhea’s breath came in short, ragged gasps. The physical exertion was draining. Her muscles burned, flooded with lactic acid, and the sulfur-tainted air scraped at the back of her throat. 

She was waiting for the blue box to appear. She fully expected a [System Warning: Unauthorized Crafting Attempt] or a prompt demanding she unlock a [Basic Stonemasonry] skill for the low, low price of five hundred soul credits. 

But the air remained empty. 

The System didn't register her actions because she wasn't using a System-approved crafting bench, nor was she combining System-tagged crafting materials. She was just a woman sitting in the dirt, hitting a rock with a sharper rock. The Ledger’s vast, magical architecture simply didn't know how to look at her.

*Scrape. Grind. Snap.*

A large flake of the masonry sheared off, exposing a relatively smooth surface underneath. Rhea wiped the sweat from her forehead with the back of her wrist, leaving a smear of gray ash across her pale skin. 

Her hands were not built for this. They were soft, accustomed to keyboards and touchscreens, not raw stone and volcanic glass. As she dug the obsidian in for another pass, the shard slipped. The razor edge bit into the fleshy part of her left palm, slicing through the top layer of skin before she could jerk her hand away.

Rhea froze. Her heart slammed against her ribs. 

She stared at the bright red line welling up on her palm. Blood pooled, thick and dark, dripping onto the glowing blue stone in her lap. 

She snapped her eyes up to her health bar, bracing for the inevitable drop to zero. The graying out of her vision. The end.

[HP: 1/100]

The number held steady. 

Rhea let out a breath she didn't realize she was holding, a manic, breathless laugh escaping her lips. The System didn't care about cosmetic bleeding. It only tracked structural, lethal damage. A minor laceration didn't cross the mathematical threshold required to deduct a full hit point. 

"You stupid, rigid machine," she whispered, her voice raspy. 

She ignored the stinging pain in her hand and went back to work. Her blood smeared across the masonry handle, mixing with the blue dust to create a sickly, glowing purple paste. She didn't care. She ground the obsidian against the stone with frantic, desperate energy, carving away the bulk of the base until it formed a crude, uneven cylinder.

[Status: Tutorial Grace Period. Duration Remaining: 00:01:12]

"Almost," she muttered, gritting her teeth. 

She gave the handle one final, brutal scrape, smoothing down a jagged spur that would have dug into her palm. She tossed the masonry dust aside and held the finished product up to the dim light of the swirling sky.

It was incredibly ugly. The top was a heavy, asymmetrical block of glowing stone, while the bottom was a blood-stained, roughly carved grip. It was top-heavy, unbalanced, and primitive. 

But it was a weapon. 

Rhea kept the Obsidian Shard gripped tightly in her left hand, and wrapped her bloody right hand around the makeshift handle of the masonry club. She pushed herself up from the ash, her bruised leg trembling as it took her weight. 

She stood before the darkened pedestal, hefting the heavy club. It felt solid. It felt real. 

"Alright, Ledger," Rhea said to the empty wasteland. "Let's see you tax this. Equip."

She mentally pushed the concept of ownership into the System interface, demanding that the game-logic recognize the object in her right hand as her primary weapon.

The air in front of her shimmered. 

Normally, a System notification appeared with a crisp, pleasant chime, the blue holographic box sliding perfectly into the center of her vision. This time, the System hesitated.

The air buzzed with a sound like a blown speaker. A translucent blue rectangle stuttered into existence, but its edges were jagged and tearing. The text inside flickered violently, cycling through unreadable glyphs before finally snapping into English. The pristine blue of the interface bled into a bruised, sickly purple.

[Anomaly Detected. System Overlay Integrity: 88%.]

Rhea grinned, her teeth white against the ash and dirt on her face. She had broken the math.

The System tried again, throwing up a second box that violently overlaid the first.

[Item Discovered: Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon]
[Type: Weapon (Improvised)]
[Rarity: Null/Unregistered]
[Damage: 3-5 Physical (Estimated)]
[Durability: Unrated]
[Status: Equipped]

The box shuddered, dropping a few pixels of visual snow onto the ground before a final, glaring red warning appended itself to the bottom of the readouts.

[Warning: Asset not sourced from standard Ledger tables. Contract status: Null. Tax assessment failed.]

Rhea swung the bludgeon through the air. It was heavy, pulling her shoulder forward, but the momentum was undeniable. If she hit something with this, it was going to break. And more importantly, the System had been forced to acknowledge her ownership without a contract. It had labeled the item as 'Discovered' rather than 'Leased'. 

She had a weapon. She had zero debt attached to it. And the System, in all its vast, bureaucratic power, couldn't do a damn thing about it.

[Status: Tutorial Grace Period. Duration Remaining: 00:00:30]

Rhea looked out at the Crater Ridge, her bloody right hand gripping the unregistered weapon. The training wheels were about to come off, but for the first time since she woke up falling through the sky, she didn't feel entirely like prey. 

She felt like an audit.
