PROSE:
Fleeing the initial ambush, Rhea sprinted toward the gray dunes of the Ash Wastes, desperate to break line of sight with the administrative spire. The transition from the hard-packed dirt of the outpost to the shifting, powdery ash was immediate and punishing. Every step required twice the effort, her boots sinking ankle-deep into the gray ruin of the frontier zone. Behind her, Vance Harrow was a symphony of ragged breathing and clanking metal, his heavy footsteps dragging as he fought to keep pace. 

She didn't make it far before the air itself seemed to curdle. The atmosphere thickened into a localized pressure front, slamming into Rhea's chest like a physical wall just as a new, burning red debuff flared across her peripheral vision.

[Status Effect: Audit Tax applied.]
[Movement Speed reduced by 50%.]
[Environmental Friction Coefficient: +300%]
[Debuff Timer: 180s]

Rhea stumbled, her forward momentum instantly halved. It felt as though she had suddenly walked neck-deep into a swamp of molasses. The System wasn't just throwing monsters at them anymore; it was rewriting the local physics engine to ensure they couldn't escape. 

[Stamina: 3/150]

"Keep moving!" Rhea gasped, her lungs burning as if she were inhaling vaporized glass. She shoved Vance toward the crumbling stone archway of a ruined tollbooth jutting from the ash a few dozen yards ahead. 

Vance groaned, his heavily blistered hands gripping the length of jagged Rebar so tightly his knuckles were white. The burns he’d sustained in the maintenance trenches were raw and angry, the durable marks of his encounter with the superheated cooling vents. "I'm trying," he wheezed, his voice tight with pain. "Legs feel like... lead."

[HP: 10/150]

Vance was a walking ghost. Ten hit points out of a hundred and fifty meant a stiff breeze could probably send him to the respawn screen—if the Ledger even allowed respawns for indentured assets. Rhea wasn't eager to find out what the fine print said about perma-death in an active audit zone. 

"Just make it to the stone," Rhea ordered, grabbing the back of his torn jacket and hauling him forward. 

They breached the threshold of the ruined tollbooth, boots clattering against cracked linoleum that had somehow survived whatever cataclysm had buried this place. The tollbooth was a relic of the old civic layer, a square concrete bunker with reinforced walls and a single, rotting wooden door. 

Rhea grabbed the edge of the heavy timber door and hauled it shut. The hinges screamed in protest, shedding flakes of rust that danced in the dim light. She slammed it into the frame and threw her weight against it, sliding a massive, rusted iron bar into the receiving brackets across the center. 

The heavy thud of the bar dropping into place echoed through the small space, followed immediately by the suffocating silence of the barricaded room. 

"Perimeter secured," Unit 734 announced, though the drone's voice lacked its usual bureaucratic smugness. The construct hovered near the cracked ceiling, its optical lens flickering erratically. Plumes of thick, acrid smoke vented continuously from its metallic body, a durable testament to the severe internal damage it had suffered when the local overseer hijacked its chassis. The smoking chassis filled the top half of the tollbooth with a haze that smelled of melting copper and ozone. 

"Define 'secured,' bucket," Vance spat, leaning his back against the adjacent wall and sliding down until he hit the floor. He rested the Rebar across his knees, his chest heaving. 

"Physical barrier integrity at forty-two percent," Unit 734 replied, dropping its altitude to avoid its own exhaust cloud. "However, Hostile Environment Markers remain active. Early warning telemetry indicates multiple incoming entities. Second wave. Debt Collectors."

[Warning: Debt Collector entities detected.]
[Distance: 15 meters and closing.]

"Of course," Rhea muttered, pressing her forehead against the rough wood of the door. The Audit Tax debuff icon pulsed in the corner of her vision, a relentless, metronomic reminder of their impending doom. 

[Debuff Timer: 145s]

The math was brutal. They were trapped in a box, their movement halved, their stamina practically nonexistent, and Vance sitting at death's door. The System didn't need to overpower them; it just needed to wait them out. The overseer was using administrative debt mechanics to bankrupt their HP and resources without ever having to dirty his own hands. 

A low, grating howl echoed from the ash dunes outside. It didn't sound like an animal. It sounded like tearing sheet metal and grinding gears. 

"They're here," Vance said, his grip tightening on the Rebar. 

The scratching began a second later. Wire-frame claws, rendered in jagged, glitching crimson light, tore at the exterior stone. The hounds didn't bark; they emitted a continuous, low-frequency hum that vibrated in Rhea's teeth. They were constructs of pure administrative malice, designed to extract value from delinquent accounts. 

A glowing blue tether snaked through a narrow vertical crack in the doorframe. It moved with the predatory grace of a viper, sweeping the immediate area for assets to seize. It brushed against the rusted iron bar, found no systemic value, and slithered lower, seeking flesh or inventory. 

It darted toward Vance's shoulder. 

Vance roared, swinging the Rebar in a short, brutal arc. The iron cracked against the stone doorframe, intercepting the glowing line. The impact shattered the tether into a shower of blue sparks, sending a violent shockwave up Vance's arms. 

"Ah, damn it!" Vance hissed, dropping the Rebar to clutch his blistered hands. "I can't take another hit from those things. One touch and I'm out of HP!"

"Don't let them touch you," Rhea said, her voice tight. She pressed her back against the wall next to the door, her singed arm hair brushing against the cold, damp concrete. The burns on her arms were a lingering reminder of the data terminal in the trenches, a physical receipt for the trouble they had caused. 

She instinctively opened her system interface, pulling up her inventory screen. The Heavy Iron Ingot and Crystallized Experience were still stowed safely, but her eyes locked onto the most dangerous item she possessed. 

[Item: Raw Data Packet]
[Status: Stowed]
[Container: System Inventory]
[Tag: Soul-Bound Liability]

The overseer wanted that packet. It contained the unalterable proof of his systemic skim, the mathematical evidence that he had been siphoning quest rewards into a private ledger. Because Rhea had exploited a loophole in their custom party contract to bind the crystal as a 'Soul-Bound Liability,' the local node couldn't simply delete it. It couldn't be dropped, traded, or seized by standard environmental hazards. 

But the Debt Collectors weren't standard hazards. They were administrative repo men. If they managed to zero out Rhea's HP, her indenture contract would default, and the System would claim all her bound assets to cover the resurrection debt. 

[Debuff Timer: 110s]

Another tether whipped through a gap near the floorboards, this one thicker and pulsing with a deeper blue light. It lashed out, wrapping tightly around Rhea's left ankle. 

A cold, bureaucratic suction immediately pulled at her. It wasn't physical pain; it was the sensation of a vacuum being applied to her nervous system. The System was attempting to extract her remaining stamina to pay off the localized tax penalty. 

[Stamina: 2/150]
[Warning: Severe Exhaustion imminent.]

Rhea gasped, her vision narrowing to a tunnel. Her legs buckled under the sudden, catastrophic drain. She kicked out violently, scraping the heel of her boot against the jagged concrete floor to sever the connection. The tether snapped, recoiling back through the gap like a struck snake. 

[Stamina: 1/150]
[Status Effect: Exhaustion (Severe) applied.]

Rhea collapsed onto her hands and knees, her breath coming in ragged, shallow gasps. The Audit Tax debuff felt like a physical weight pressing down on her spine. Every muscle fiber screamed in protest. 

"Rhea!" Vance yelled, scooping up his Rebar and driving it into the gap to pry a snapping, wire-frame jaw away from the wood. "Get up! You can't stay on the floor!"

"I'm... fine," Rhea lied, forcing herself to sit up and lean against the wall. Her stamina was effectively zeroed out. If another tether caught her, it would start eating into her HP, and she didn't have much of that to spare either. 

Unit 734 dropped lower, its optical lens flashing a rapid, anxious yellow. "Structural failure of primary barrier in forty-five seconds. Recommend immediate evasion protocols."

"There are no evasion protocols, you flying toaster!" Vance shouted, swinging the Rebar again to smash a claw that had breached the center crack of the door. "We're in a concrete box!"

"Then I recommend updating your Last Will and Testament algorithms," the drone replied unhelpfully, venting another cloud of acrid smoke. 

[Debuff Timer: 60s]

One minute. Sixty seconds of surviving the math. 

The hounds outside intensified their assault. The rotting wood of the door began to bow inward under the collective weight of the wire-frame constructs. The rusted iron bar groaned, flakes of oxidized metal raining down onto the floor. 

"They're going to break through," Vance said, his voice dropping to a desperate whisper. He looked at his HP bar, hovering in his peripheral vision. Ten hit points. A single bite. A single tether. "Rhea, if they breach..."

"They won't," Rhea said, her voice lacking the conviction she wanted to project. She checked the timer again. 

[Debuff Timer: 45s]

Two more tethers snaked through the widening cracks. Vance smashed one with the Rebar, but the second slipped past his guard, darting toward his chest. 

Rhea lunged, ignoring the screaming protest of her zeroed-out stamina. She grabbed the tether with her bare hands. The raw, administrative code burned against her palms, a freezing, digital fire. 

[Warning: Unauthorized interference with Debt Collection.]
[Penalty assessed.]

The tether didn't drain her stamina—it couldn't. Instead, it tried to seize her inventory. She felt the System reach into her blazer pockets, searching for untaxed value. 

[Item Seized: Scavenger Carapaces]

The tether retracted, satisfied with the extracted value, pulling the physical Scavenger Carapaces right out of her pocket and dragging them through the crack in the door. 

Rhea slumped back, her hands shaking. "Let them take the junk," she panted. "If a tether gets in, let it grab the loose loot. Just keep them off your HP."

Vance nodded, his face pale. He understood the math. They were paying the toll, one piece of garbage at a time. 

[Debuff Timer: 20s]

The door gave a terrifying crack. The upper hinge snapped, the metal shearing completely off. The top corner of the heavy timber door tilted inward, revealing the gray, swirling ash outside and the glowing, glitching red eyes of the Debt Collectors. 

A wire-frame hound shoved its snout through the gap, its jaws snapping wildly. It didn't have teeth; it had jagged lines of code that promised localized deletion. 

Vance didn't hesitate. He thrust the Rebar upward like a spear, driving the iron directly into the hound's open maw. The construct shrieked—a sound like a dial-up modem being fed through a woodchipper—and dissolved into a burst of red pixels, dropping a handful of untaxed copper coins that clattered against the linoleum. 

"Got one," Vance grunted, pulling the Rebar back. 

But three more immediately took its place, their glowing eyes fixed on the gap. 

[Debuff Timer: 10s]

"Hold the line!" Rhea shouted, grabbing a loose chunk of concrete from the floor and hurling it at the gap. It bounced harmlessly off a hound's wire-frame skull, but the distraction was enough to make the construct flinch. 

[Debuff Timer: 5s]
[Debuff Timer: 3s]
[Debuff Timer: 1s]

The heavy, oppressive weight that had been pressing down on Rhea's chest suddenly vanished. The air in the tollbooth cleared, the localized pressure front dissipating as quickly as it had formed. 

[Status Effect: Audit Tax removed.]
[Movement Speed normalized.]
[Environmental Friction Coefficient normalized.]

Outside, the frantic scratching and howling abruptly ceased. The hounds at the gap froze, their red eyes dimming to a dull, lifeless gray. A second later, they dissolved entirely, their code unraveling into the howling wind of the Ash Wastes. 

[Debt Collector Pursuit: Timed Out.]
[Encounter Survived.]

The silence that followed was deafening, broken only by the sound of Vance's ragged breathing and the continuous hum of Unit 734's damaged cooling fans. 

Rhea opened her eyes, staring at the cracked ceiling. They had survived the timer inside the barricaded tollbooth, exhausted but alive. The System had thrown its bureaucratic weight at them, trying to bankrupt them through rigged encounters and predatory taxes. They had survived, but just barely. 

"They're gone," Vance rasped, letting the Rebar slip from his blistered hands. It clattered loudly against the floor. He leaned his head back, staring at his own interface. "Ten HP. I didn't lose a single point."

Unit 734 stabilized its hover, the smoke from its chassis thinning slightly as it processed the environmental data. "Audit wave concluded. Debuff timer expired. Next localized sweep is pending administrative approval."

Rhea pushed herself up, her muscles trembling with the effort. Her stamina was still sitting at a miserable 1/150, but the immediate threat was over. She pulled up her inventory, verifying the Raw Data Packet was still there. It was. The overseer hadn't managed to reclaim his stolen ledger. 

"We can't stay on the defensive," Rhea said, her voice a dry rasp. "The overseer has infinite resources. We have ten hit points and a rusted piece of iron. If we just sit here, the next debuff will kill us."

Vance looked up at her, his scarred face drawn and exhausted. "So what's the play, Mercer? We can't outrun the System."

"We don't outrun it," Rhea said, her eyes narrowing as she looked at the smoking drone. "We audit it back."
