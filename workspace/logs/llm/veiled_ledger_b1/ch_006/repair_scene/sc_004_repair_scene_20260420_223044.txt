PROSE:
As the hounds began to respawn outside, their wire-frame bodies snapping together from the swirling, static-charged ash, Rhea frantically decrypted the hub coordinates while Vance braced his shoulder against the splintering wooden door.

The Ruined Tollbooth had been a sanctuary for exactly three minutes. Built from heavy, system-generated masonry, it was designed to withstand the ambient hostility of the Ash Wastes. The walls were covered in faded, bureaucratic murals depicting the glory of the Ledger, now scored by claw marks and blackened by old fires. Dust motes danced in the red light filtering through the cracks in the door, each particle feeling like a physical weight against Rhea's exhausted frame. 

But the Overseer wasn’t relying on ambient hostility. He was spending node mana to forcibly spawn Debt Collectors—vicious, angular hounds made of glowing red algorithms. They didn’t want meat; they wanted numbers. They wanted HP, stamina, and inventory items, tearing them away with glowing tethers that bypassed physical armor entirely.

Vance Harrow was currently doing the math on his own survival, and the equation was grim. He stood with his boots planted wide on the cracked stone floor, his scarred face pressed against the rough, rotting wood of the heavy door. Every time a hound threw its weight against the exterior, the impact traveled up Vance’s arm, rattling his teeth and threatening to burst the durable blisters covering his hands. He adjusted his grip on the heavy, jagged piece of Rebar in his right hand. It was a crude weapon, salvaged from the collapsed transit tunnel, but it had weight, and right now, weight was the only thing keeping the door closed.

[Vance Harrow HP: 10/150]
[Warning: Critical Vitality. Structural integrity of avatar compromised. Next impact may result in localized deletion.]

"Ten," Vance hissed, his breath fogging the cold air leaking through the door's cracks. "I am sitting at exactly ten hit points, Mercer. If one of those administrative lapdogs so much as sneezes on me through a gap in these boards, I am going to mathematically cease to exist."

Rhea Mercer didn’t look up from the glowing green terminal. She couldn’t afford the physical effort. Her own interface was a flashing nightmare of red warnings and depleted bars.

[Rhea Mercer Stamina: 1/150]
[Status: Exhaustion (Severe) - Movement speed reduced by 90%. Physical actions incur severe pain penalty. Cognitive load increased.]

Every keystroke felt like pushing her fingers through wet concrete. The localized Audit Tax debuff had stripped her down to the barest minimum required to keep breathing. Her lungs burned with a dry, phantom heat. The copper taste of system-induced fatigue coated the back of her throat. She tapped another sequence into the dead terminal's exposed copper wiring, the singed hair on her bare forearms brushing against the rusted console as her hands trembled violently.

"I hear you, Vance," Rhea rasped, her voice barely carrying over the howling wind outside. "Just hold the line. The decryption is processing."

She was using Unit 734's interface cable as a bridge to bypass the local firewall. The drone was not having a good time. Its smoking chassis vibrated violently on the stone floor next to the console, plumes of thick, acrid smoke venting from its internal cooling ports. The local overseer had hijacked its optical lens earlier to project his massive, screaming avatar, and the resulting system override had left the construct in a state of perpetual, agonizing hardware failure.

"Unit 734 is currently experiencing a catastrophic routing error," the drone synthesized, its voice box crackling with static. "Coolant levels depleted. Logic core temperature exceeding safe parameters. Please disconnect. Warranty voided. Existence is pain."

"Hold the connection, Seven," Rhea muttered, her eyes locked on the flickering screen. "I just need the exact origin point of the mana expenditures. The Overseer is hiding behind a proxy, but the ledger doesn't lie. The mana has to flow from somewhere."

"The hounds are getting organized," Vance warned. He slammed his weight forward as another heavy impact rattled the rusted iron hinges. Dust rained down from the ceiling. "They're not just throwing themselves at the wood anymore. They're trying to bite through the crossbeam. I can literally hear them calculating the structural integrity of the oak."

Rhea ignored him, focusing on the interface. She opened her system inventory with a thought. 

[Accessing System Inventory...]
[Retrieving: Raw Data Packet]

She materialized the Raw Data Packet into her left hand. The soul-bound liability pulsed with a sickly, critical-durability amber light, casting long, erratic shadows across the dim tollbooth. It felt heavy, not with physical mass, but with the sheer bureaucratic weight of the evidence it contained. It was the proof of the Overseer's skim—the 14.3% deficit in quest rewards they had uncovered in the Ash Thickets, neatly packaged in a crystallized format.

"The Overseer is funding these Debt Collectors from a finite local budget," Rhea said, her voice gaining a fraction of its usual sharp edge as she held the pulsing data packet near the terminal. She let its localized aura interface with the decryption commands, using the evidence of his own crime as a cryptographic key. "I saw the cascading ledger when we first plugged in. He doesn't have infinite mana. He's pulling it from a centralized administrative hub to spawn these things."

[System Notification: Decryption in Progress... 62%]
[Bypassing Proxy Nodes...]

"Fascinating," Vance grunted. His boots slid an inch backward, squeaking against the dust. He quickly jammed the end of the Rebar against a raised flagstone, using it to lock his leg in place. "Will this finite budget run out before or after my ten hit points become zero hit points? Because if it's the latter, I'd like to formally request a transfer to a less suicidal party."

"It doesn't matter if it runs out," Rhea said, her fingers flying across the cracked glass of the terminal. "We're not going to wait for him to go bankrupt. We're going to audit the hub."

[Mana Ledger: Overseer Node Maintenance Budget]
[Current Draw: -45 Mana/sec]
[Active Constructs: 12]
[Source Coordinate: Encrypted]

"Audit the hub," Vance repeated, his scarred face twisting into a grimace. "Right. Because filing a grievance worked out so beautifully the first time. We got rained on by physical experience points, nearly crushed by flying iron ingots, and then a giant red man told us we were marked for death."

"That was a remote grievance," Rhea corrected, her eyes reflecting the scrolling green text. "We filed it through the Ledger's automated system. The Overseer intercepted it, froze the local assets, and manually overrode the node to target us. He put a wall between himself and the core system so the higher-ups wouldn't see what he was doing."

She tapped the Raw Data Packet against the console. "But if we physically breach the hub... if we take this raw data, format it as a corrupted ledger entry, and inject it directly into his local mainframe?"

[Decryption in Progress... 88%]
[Resolving encrypted spatial tags...]

Unit 734 let out a high-pitched mechanical whine, its optical lens flashing a frantic yellow. "Query: Injecting corrupted data into a localized administrative hub violates Section 4, Paragraph 12 of the System Terms of Service. Penalty: Immediate deletion of all offending entities. Secondary Penalty: Post-deletion debt inheritance assigned to nearest kin."

"Only if the system recognizes us as a foreign body," Rhea said, the logic of the exploit crystallizing in her mind. "But we're marked for Audit Compliance. The Overseer made us part of his internal disciplinary loop. We have administrative access by default, just the wrong kind."

Vance let out a bark of laughter that sounded suspiciously like a death rattle. "You're going to use the death sentence he slapped on us as a VIP pass into his office."

"Exactly," Rhea said. "He categorized us as a liability. We're going to act like one. Think about it, Vance. The System is just a giant accounting firm. It doesn't understand morality; it understands balance. The Overseer has been skimming 14.3% off the top of every transaction in this node. If we take this data packet, wrap it in our Audit Compliance tag, and inject it directly into his central mana ledger, we become a paradox."

She paused to catch her breath, the single point of Stamina making even a long sentence a grueling physical workout. 

"We become a massive, unreconcilable mathematical error," she continued. "The local node's automated defense protocols will look at the ledger, see a catastrophic imbalance, and instantly shut down the Overseer's administrative shields to prevent the corruption from uploading to the core Ledger. It’ll crash his entire local system just to quarantine the math."

Vance strained as a particularly massive Debt Collector slammed into the door. The wood splintered, and a glowing red wire-frame snout pushed through the gap, snapping its jaws. The air instantly smelled of ozone, burning copper, and the distinct, sterile scent of a server room fire.

[Warning: Debt Collector Tether Initiated.]
[Target: Vance Harrow. Resource: HP.]

Vance didn't hesitate. He thrust the Rebar forward, driving the blunt, rusted iron directly into the construct's glowing eye socket, leveraging the weight of the makeshift weapon against the hound's forward momentum.

[Critical Hit! Environmental Damage applied. Target Destroyed.]

The hound shattered into a burst of red pixels, the tether snapping before it could latch onto his remaining HP. But two more immediately shoved their snouts into the gap, their claws tearing at the widening hole in the wood.

"They're through the first layer!" Vance yelled, ripping the Rebar back and bracing for the next impact. "If you're going to crash a system, Mercer, do it now!"

[Decryption Complete.]
[Source Coordinate: LOC_ADMIN_HUB_099]
[Coordinates Acquired.]

The terminal flashed a brilliant, blinding green. A small, physical slot on the console clicked open with a sharp mechanical clack, and a glowing Administrative Coordinate Chip slid out. Rhea snatched it with her left hand, her stamina bar screaming in protest at the sudden, jerky movement. A wave of dizziness washed over her, the Exhaustion debuff threatening to pull her into unconsciousness.

[Item Acquired: Administrative Coordinate Chip]
[Warning: Physical action penalty applied. Pain threshold exceeded.]

She gritted her teeth against the sudden spike of pain in her chest. She stowed the Raw Data Packet back into her system inventory, the pulsing amber light vanishing, and gripped the edge of the console with her free hand to push herself up.

"Got it," Rhea gasped, her legs trembling under her weight. She looked at the back door of the tollbooth, a rusted iron grate that led out into a narrow ravine in the Ash Wastes. It was their only exit that wasn't currently swarming with administrative death-hounds.

"Seven, disconnect and retract," Rhea ordered, her voice authoritative despite the rasp.

Unit 734 ripped its interface cable from the exposed copper wiring, emitting a shower of sparks that illuminated the dim room. "Retracting. Chassis integrity at 34%. Please advise nearest repair vendor. I am leaking vital fluids."

"I'll buy you a whole new chassis if we live through this," Rhea promised, limping toward the rear exit, her blazer hanging off her shoulders like a shroud. 

Vance's boots slipped again. The wood of the door groaned, a massive vertical crack appearing straight down the center. The red light of the hounds spilled into the room, painting the dust in the air.

"Plan's formulated! Coordinates are locked!" Vance shouted, abandoning his brace and backing away from the door, keeping the Rebar raised. "Time to go, Mercer!"

With the coordinates locked into the glowing chip and a plan formed, Rhea prepared to move out before the door gave way entirely.
