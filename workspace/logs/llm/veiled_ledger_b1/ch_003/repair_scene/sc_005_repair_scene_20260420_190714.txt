PROSE:
With the sixteen Copper Credits divided into three uneven but untaxed piles on a slab of fallen concrete, Artie—whose Ledger designation read Vance Harrow—wiped a smear of ash from his scarred face and let out a long, ragged exhale. The collapsed transit tunnel smelled of ozone, crushed insectoid ichor, and the distinct, metallic tang of an exploited physics engine. They had survived the vermin control quest as an ad-hoc group, and more importantly, they had survived it without paying the System’s extortionate stamina and durability taxes. The dust was still settling from the massive chunk of ceiling Rhea had brought down on the scavengers, the particulate matter catching the dim, ambient light of the frontier zone.

Vance leaned heavily against a fractured support pillar, his breath coming in shallow, painful rasps. His health bar, hovering faintly in Rhea's peripheral vision thanks to their temporary proximity link, was a terrifyingly thin sliver of red. He was sitting at exactly four hit points out of a maximum of one hundred. A stiff breeze or a particularly aggressive sneeze might actually kill him. He looked down at the makeshift metal tray they had fashioned from a piece of scrap, eyeing the small pile of currency. 

Vance scooped his share of five Copper Credits from the makeshift metal tray, his bruised arms shaking slightly from the adrenaline crash. He slipped the heavy, warm coins into his torn pocket, the metal clinking softly. Rhea watched him carefully, her own eleven Copper Credits already safely stowed in her right blazer pocket alongside her cracked smartphone. She kept her left hand free, her right hand gripping the heavy, blood-stained Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon that served as her primary weapon and current crutch.

"Alright," Vance said, his voice echoing off the fractured support pillars, sounding thin and exhausted. "That worked. You’re crazy, Mercer, but your math works. The ceiling drop bypassed the combat tax completely. But let’s make this official. The temporary group tag won't protect us from the local node's tariff schedules forever, especially if we have to move back out into the open ashwood. I’m sending a standard System Party invite."

Rhea Mercer, inherently distrustful of default Ledger contracts after her near-death experience with the starter cache, immediately stiffened. The muscles in her back locked up. She leaned harder on her heavy, blood-stained Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon. "Absolutely not," she snapped, shutting the idea down before the prompt even fully rendered in the air between them. "Cancel it."

"It's just a button," Vance argued, his hand hovering over his personal interface, his fingers dancing over translucent blue keys that only he could fully see. He looked at her like she was the one being unreasonable, like she was the one who didn't understand how survival worked in this nightmare. "It’s how things are done here. You group up, you share XP, you don't die alone in a ditch. Standard procedure. Everyone does it."

A glowing blue prompt materialized between them in the gloom of the collapsed transit tunnel, illuminating the dust motes dancing in the dead air. It was crisp, clean, and entirely too cheerful for a world that charged you stamina just to pick up a stick.

[Party Contract: Standard Ledger Alliance]
[Warning: Acceptance binds all participants to local node tariff schedules.]
[Do you accept? Y/N]

"It's a debt trap," Rhea corrected, her voice cold and precise. She didn't blink. She didn't look at the large 'Y' button. She pointed the jagged edge of her Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon at the tiny, pulsing asterisk hidden in the bottom right corner of the holographic parchment. It was barely a pixel wide, designed to be ignored by desperate people bleeding out in dark tunnels. "Look at it, Vance. Really look at it. You think the System just wants us to hold hands and sing campfire songs? Expand the terms."

Vance frowned, his scarred brow furrowing. He reached out and tapped the asterisk. 

The blue window expanded instantly, cascading into a dense wall of legalese that scrolled faster than a human eye could comfortably track. It filled the space between them, casting a harsh, blue light over the crushed scavenger carapaces and the rubble.

"If one of us defaults on a stamina tax," Rhea explained, stepping closer to the glowing text, her eyes tracking the blur of words with the practiced ease of a former forensic accountant. "The System amortizes the deficit across the party. Section four, paragraph nine. It’s a joint-and-several liability clause for biological resources. If you get hit and your HP drops below the threshold to pay the local node's 'breathing tariff,' the System doesn't just let you die. It automatically siphons HP from me to keep your account solvent. I am not subsidizing your bleeding."

Vance blinked, staring at the scrolling text, his mouth slightly open. "Wait, it drains your health to pay my taxes?"

"With a compounding interest rate payable in maximum HP," Rhea confirmed grimly. "And that’s just the health tax. Look at the loot distribution clause. Subsection B, paragraph two. 'All acquired assets are subject to a standard fifteen percent administrative skimming fee before equal distribution.' The local overseer gets a cut of everything we touch just for the privilege of us being in a party. We just spent twenty minutes dropping a ceiling on giant bugs to avoid a two percent gathering tax, and you want to sign a contract that hands over fifteen percent of our gross income before we even see it?"

Vance lowered his hand, the blue prompt hovering untouched. He looked from the text to Rhea, then down at his own battered body. "Okay. Fine. The math is rigged. But we can't run group content without a party tag. The aggro mechanics will tear us apart. If I taunt a mob and we aren't linked, the System treats us as separate hostile entities. It’ll trigger a multi-target penalty. The mobs will enrage, and they'll hit twice as hard. We need the mechanical link to survive the combat algorithms."

"I know," Rhea said, her voice dropping to a dangerous whisper. "Which is why we aren't using the default." 

She turned her attention to the glowing, hovering form of the local clerk. Unit 734, the drone clerk Rhea had nicknamed Vex, had been hovering silently near the tunnel entrance, its rusted chassis humming softly as it processed the transaction logs of their recent kills. Its single glowing optical lens was fixed on the pile of untaxed loot.

"You," Rhea said, pointing her weapon directly at the drone's optical sensor. "Open the backend."

Unit 734 whirred, its internal gears grinding slightly. The glowing optical lens clicked in annoyance as it focused on her. "Negative, User Mercer. Standard users do not possess the administrative rights to alter foundational alliance architecture. Please accept the Standard Ledger Alliance or disband. Loitering in a cleared instance without an active party tag will result in a proximity fine of two Copper Credits per minute."

"I am not a standard user," Rhea said, taking a slow, deliberate step toward the floating machine. "I am an un-indentured anomaly who just figured out how to bypass your node’s primary revenue stream. I possess the right to crush more environmental debris onto more untaxed scavengers and bankrupt your local node's projected ecosystem. I will sit in this tunnel and drop rocks on bugs until your overseer’s ledger bleeds out from the lack of tariff revenue. I will make this sector a black hole of untaxed physics exploits."

The drone clicked again, a sound remarkably like a synthetic sigh. Its internal cooling fans spun up, whining in the damp air. "Threatening a System Clerk is a Class C infraction, punishable by—"

"Punishable by a fine deducted from a linked account," Rhea interrupted, her eyes narrowing, her grip on the Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon tightening. "Which I don't have, because I never signed your starter cache agreement. You have no financial leverage over me, 734. You can't fine an account that doesn't exist in your debt registry. Open the syntax, or I start breaking the local economy in half, starting with every petrified tree in the ashwood."

For a long, tense moment, the only sound in the tunnel was the dripping of condensation from the fractured ceiling and the hum of the drone's repulsors. The machine was calculating. It was weighing the cost of a syntax breach against the catastrophic loss of revenue Rhea represented. 

Then, the blue window shattered. 

It didn't fade; it broke apart like glass, the shards dissolving into the air with a soft, digitized chime. In its place, a cascading wall of jagged, green text materialized, casting a sickly, emerald light over Vance's scarred face and the drone's rusted chassis. The air grew noticeably colder.

[System Syntax: Developer Framework Unlocked]
[Terms and Conditions: Custom Clause Generation Active]
[Warning: Unverified contracts may result in catastrophic metaphysical default.]

"Beautiful," Rhea whispered, stepping up to the scrolling code. Her eyes scanned the variables, her mind shifting gears. Before she had died and woken up in this bureaucratic hellscape, she had been a forensic accountant and a fraud investigator. She had spent her life hunting down hidden assets, unspooling shell corporations, and reading the spaces between the words. She knew how to build a cage out of ink. Now, she just had to do it with glowing green math.

She forced Unit 734 to pull up the raw contract syntax, her fingers moving rapidly through the air as she began drafting a custom party contract. She wasn't just forming an alliance; she was building a legal fortress. She swiped away the default templates, deleting entire paragraphs of predatory Ledger logic with a flick of her wrist.

"First," Rhea muttered, her hands swiping lines of code into place, her eyes reflecting the emerald light. "We disable the amortization of biological debt. Your HP is your HP. My HP is my HP. If you go broke, you die. I am not a health potion. I am inserting a hard firewall between our resource pools. No automatic siphoning. No joint liability."

Vance grimaced, rubbing his broken left arm, but he nodded slowly. "Fair enough. I don't want your health anyway."

"Second," she continued, dragging a massive block of text from the liability section and dropping it into the active compiler. "Friendly fire. The default contract reduces friendly fire damage by eighty percent, but it doesn't eliminate it, and it doesn't penalize the aggressor. That’s a loophole for assassination. If someone wants to kill you, they just have to hit you five times as hard. I’m changing the variable. Any intentional damage dealt by a party member to another party member results in an immediate, unmitigated reflection of that damage multiplied by ten, payable directly to the victim's health pool."

"Jesus, Mercer," Vance muttered, taking a step back from the glowing green wall. "You don't trust anyone, do you? Multiply by ten? That's an instant kill."

"Trust is a statistical anomaly," Rhea replied without looking away from the code. "Math is a guarantee. If you don't plan on stabbing me in the back, the clause will never trigger. Third clause: Loot distribution. We are bypassing the administrative skim entirely by classifying all acquired assets as 'Salvaged Debris' rather than 'System Rewards.' The split is mathematically rigid. Thirty-three point three percent across the board, with the remainder held in a zero-interest escrow account that requires unanimous consent to access. The local overseer gets absolutely nothing."

She worked for another ten minutes, her fingers flying across the holographic interface. She locked down the privacy settings, completely disabling unilateral reporting to the local overseer. If one of them got caught doing something illegal—like, say, exploiting physics to avoid taxes—the System couldn't compel the others to testify or share their telemetry data. She built in clauses for dispute resolution that relied on raw damage output rather than System arbitration, and she explicitly defined the parameters for disbanding the group to prevent sudden, mid-combat betrayals.

Finally, she stepped back, her chest heaving slightly from the mental exertion. The green text solidified, formatting itself into a dense, glowing document that hovered in the center of the tunnel. It was a masterpiece of paranoid legalese, a contract so tight it squeaked. It was a weapon forged from bureaucracy.

"Sign it," Rhea said, gesturing to the glowing line at the bottom of the document. She looked at Vance, her expression hard and unyielding. "Or you can go back to fighting the math on your own."

Vance stared at the contract, the green light reflecting in his eyes. He looked at the clauses, the severe penalties for betrayal, the absolute lack of safety nets. It was a brutal agreement, stripped of all the System's false comforts. But it was honest. And in the Ledger, honesty was the rarest loot of all.
