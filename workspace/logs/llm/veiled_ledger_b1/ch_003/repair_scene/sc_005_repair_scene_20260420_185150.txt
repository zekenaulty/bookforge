PROSE:
With the Copper Credits divided into three uneven but untaxed piles on a slab of fallen concrete, Vance Harrow wiped a smear of ash from his scarred face and let out a long, ragged exhale. The collapsed transit tunnel smelled of ozone, crushed insectoid ichor, and the distinct, metallic tang of an exploited physics engine. They had survived the vermin control quest, and more importantly, they had survived it without paying the System’s extortionate stamina and durability taxes. 

Vance scooped his share of the copper coins from the makeshift metal tray, his bruised arms shaking slightly from the adrenaline crash. He pulled up his standard system interface, his fingers dancing over the translucent blue keys. "Alright," he said, his voice echoing off the fractured support pillars. "That worked. You’re crazy, Mercer, but your math works. Let’s make this official. I’m sending a standard System Party invite."

Rhea Mercer, inherently distrustful of default Ledger contracts after her near-death experience with the starter cache, immediately stiffened. She leaned on her Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon, the heavy, blood-stained block of concrete serving as both a weapon and a crutch. "Absolutely not," she snapped, shutting the idea down before the prompt even fully rendered. "Cancel it."

"It's just a button," Vance argued, his hand hovering over his personal interface. He looked at her like she was the one being unreasonable. "It’s how things are done here. You group up, you share XP, you don't die alone in a ditch. Standard procedure."

A glowing blue prompt materialized between them in the gloom of the collapsed transit tunnel, illuminating the dust motes dancing in the dead air.

[Party Contract: Standard Ledger Alliance]
[Warning: Acceptance binds all participants to local node tariff schedules.]
[Do you accept? Y/N]

"It's a debt trap," Rhea corrected, her voice cold and precise. She pointed the jagged edge of her Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon at the tiny, pulsing asterisk hidden in the bottom right corner of the holographic parchment. "Look at it, Vance. Really look at it. You think the System just wants us to hold hands and sing campfire songs? Expand the terms."

Vance frowned, tapping the asterisk. The blue window expanded, cascading into a dense wall of legalese that scrolled faster than a human eye could comfortably track. 

"If one of us defaults on a stamina tax," Rhea explained, stepping closer to the glowing text, "the System amortizes the deficit across the party. Section four, paragraph nine. It’s a joint-and-several liability clause for biological resources. If you get hit and your HP drops below the threshold to pay the local node's 'breathing tariff,' the System doesn't just let you die. It automatically siphons HP from me to keep your account solvent. I am not subsidizing your bleeding."

Vance blinked, staring at the scrolling text. "Wait, it drains your health to pay my taxes?"

"With a compounding interest rate payable in maximum HP," Rhea confirmed grimly. "And that’s just the health tax. Look at the loot distribution clause. 'All acquired assets are subject to a standard 15% administrative skimming fee before equal distribution.' The local overseer gets a cut of everything we touch just for the privilege of us being in a party. We just spent twenty minutes dropping a ceiling on giant bugs to avoid a 2% gathering tax, and you want to sign a contract that hands over 15% of our gross income?"

Vance lowered his hand, the blue prompt hovering untouched. "Okay. Fine. But we can't run group content without a party tag. The aggro mechanics will tear us apart. If I taunt a mob and we aren't linked, the System treats us as separate hostile entities. It’ll trigger a multi-target penalty."

"I know," Rhea said. "Which is why we aren't using the default." She turned her attention to the glowing, hovering form of the local clerk. Unit 734 had been hovering silently near the tunnel entrance, its rusted drone chassis humming softly as it processed the transaction logs of their recent kills. 

"You," Rhea said, pointing her bludgeon at the drone. "Open the backend."

Unit 734 whirred, its glowing optical lens clicking in annoyance as it focused on her. "Negative, User Mercer. Standard users do not possess the administrative rights to alter foundational alliance architecture. Please accept the Standard Ledger Alliance or disband."

"I am not a standard user," Rhea said, taking a step toward the floating machine. "I am an un-indentured anomaly who just figured out how to bypass your node’s primary revenue stream. I possess the right to crush more environmental debris onto more untaxed scavengers and bankrupt your local node's projected ecosystem. I will sit in this tunnel and drop rocks on bugs until your overseer’s ledger bleeds out from the lack of tariff revenue."

The drone clicked again, a sound remarkably like a synthetic sigh. Its internal cooling fans spun up, whining in the damp air. "Threatening a System Clerk is a Class C infraction, punishable by—"

"Punishable by a fine deducted from a linked account," Rhea interrupted, her eyes narrowing. "Which I don't have, because I never signed your starter cache agreement. You have no financial leverage over me, 734. Open the syntax, or I start breaking the local economy in half."

For a long, tense moment, the only sound in the tunnel was the dripping of condensation and the hum of the drone's repulsors. Then, the blue window shattered. It didn't fade; it broke apart like glass, the shards dissolving into the air. In its place, a cascading wall of jagged, green text materialized, casting a sickly, emerald light over Vance's scarred face and the drone's rusted chassis.

[System Syntax: Developer Framework Unlocked]
[Terms and Conditions: Custom Clause Generation Active]
[Warning: Unverified contracts may result in catastrophic metaphysical default.]

"Beautiful," Rhea whispered, stepping up to the scrolling code. Her eyes scanned the variables, her mind shifting gears. Before she had died and woken up in this bureaucratic hellscape, she had been a forensic accountant and a fraud investigator. She knew how to read the spaces between the words. She knew how to build a cage out of ink. Now, she just had to do it with glowing green math.

She forced Unit 734 to pull up the raw contract syntax, her fingers moving rapidly through the air as she began drafting a custom party contract. She wasn't just forming an alliance; she was building a legal fortress. 

"First," Rhea muttered, her hands swiping lines of code into place, "we disable the amortization of biological debt. Your HP is your HP. My HP is my HP. If you go broke, you die. I am not a health potion."

Vance grimaced but nodded. "Fair enough."

"Second," she continued, dragging a block of text from the liability section. "Friendly fire. The default contract reduces friendly fire damage by 80%, but it doesn't eliminate it, and it doesn't penalize the aggressor. That’s a loophole for assassination. I’m changing the variable. Any intentional damage dealt by a party member to another party member results in an immediate, unmitigated reflection of that damage multiplied by ten, payable directly to the victim's health pool."

"Jesus, Mercer," Vance muttered. "You don't trust anyone, do you?"

"Trust is a statistical anomaly," Rhea replied without looking away from the code. "Math is a guarantee. Third clause: Loot distribution. We are bypassing the administrative skim entirely by classifying all acquired assets as 'Salvaged Debris' rather than 'System Rewards.' The split is mathematically rigid. Thirty-three point three percent across the board, with the remainder held in a zero-interest escrow account that requires unanimous consent to access."

She worked for another ten minutes, her fingers flying across the holographic interface. She locked down the privacy settings, completely disabling unilateral reporting to the local overseer. If one of them got caught doing something illegal—like, say, exploiting physics to avoid taxes—the System couldn't compel the others to testify or share their telemetry data. 

Finally, she stepped back. The green text solidified, formatting itself into a dense, glowing document that hovered in the center of the tunnel. It was a masterpiece of paranoid legalese, a contract so tight it squeaked.

"Sign it," Rhea said, gesturing to the glowing line at the bottom of the document. She looked at Vance, her expression hard and unyielding. "Or you can go back to fighting the math on your own."

Vance stared at the contract, the green light reflecting in his eyes. He looked at the clauses, the severe penalties for betrayal, the absolute lack of safety nets. It was a brutal agreement, stripped of all the System's false comforts. But it was honest. And in the Ledger, honesty was the rarest loot of all.
