PROSE:
Rhea stood at the rusted chassis of the Ashwood Deposit Node, her thumb running over the cool, untaxed surface of her newly acquired Copper Credits, when a harsh crimson light washed over the clearing and an automated flag triggered in her peripheral vision.

The Ashwood Deposit Node looked less like a mystical artifact of a fantasy world and more like a repurposed ATM that had been left out in the acid rain for a decade. Its casing was pitted and scarred, covered in a fine layer of the omnipresent gray ash that choked the tutorial zone. But to Rhea, it was a monument to her first real victory against the rigged mechanics of the afterlife.

The coins in her left hand were heavy. Not physically—they weighed about as much as standard arcade tokens—but metaphysically, they possessed the dense, undeniable gravity of a successfully executed exploit. Four coins. Four tiny, stamped discs of base metal, each etched with a glowing, microscopic System rune that confirmed their validity. Back in the real world, four pennies wouldn't buy a glance from a cashier. Here, in the Veiled Ledger's frontier zone, four Copper Credits felt like a king's ransom. 

The System was designed to strip-mine human souls for value, converting panic, pain, and labor into a neat, easily digestible digital currency. The indenture contract she had barely managed to avoid signing at the Crater Ridge had made that abundantly clear. The house always won. But right now, the house was looking at a discrepancy, and Rhea was holding the proof.

She shifted her weight, wincing as a sharp spike of pain shot up her bruised left leg. Her health pool was sitting at a precarious twenty-five out of a hundred. A stiff breeze, a bad stumble, or a particularly aggressive squirrel could probably put her back in the grave. But her Stamina had stabilized at forty, and more importantly, the suffocating, lung-burning weight of the 'Exhaustion' status effect was gone. 

She looked down at the Copper Credits resting in her palm. The deposit node's cracked screen hummed a satisfied, neutral green. 

[Transaction Complete.]
[Yield: 4 Copper Credits.]
[Tax Applied: 0 Stamina.]
[Garnishment: 0 Copper Credits.]

It was beautiful. It was the most beautiful string of text she had ever read. She had taken untaxed environmental debris—the crushed remains of an ash-crusted scavenger and a splintered branch—and shoved it into the deposit hopper under the classification of 'Environmental Debris Clearance.' The system, lacking the nuance to differentiate between 'clearing a blocked pathway' and 'looting a corpse she had manually crushed with a falling tree,' had processed the transaction without applying the standard gathering tax or garnishing the yield toward her massive Soul Debt. 

She closed her fist around the coins, feeling the hard edges bite into her pale skin. The plan moving forward was simple, repeatable, and beautifully violent. Find more low-level mobs. Drop more petrified trees on them. Process the resulting paste without triggering the refinement interface. Feed the hopper. Bank the capital. Buy a health potion that didn't taste like locker room floor runoff. 

Then, the green light on the deposit node flickered. 

It didn't just turn off; it died with a pathetic, digital wheeze. A second later, the heavy iron chassis violently rebooted. The light shifted from a placid green to a hostile, glaring crimson that painted the surrounding petrified ashwood in the color of an impending headache.

[System Notification]
[Audit Flag: Irregular Transaction Detected.]
[Reviewing Ledger Transaction #884-A...]
[Anomaly Identified: Value Generation without Equivalent Suffering.]

Rhea’s heart rate spiked, her grip tightening instinctively on the heavy wooden haft of her Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon in her right hand. The weapon was crude—just a chunk of glowing masonry lashed to an obsidian-shaved handle—but it had weight, and right now, weight felt like safety. 

"Oh, come on," she muttered, taking a half-step back from the rusted terminal. "It was four coppers. You’re a multi-dimensional ledger governing the afterlife. Round down."

The System did not round down. 

Instead, the air to her left began to smell sharply of ozone and burnt coffee. The crimson light from the terminal fractured, projecting a dense grid of geometric lines that rapidly filled with color and texture. It was like watching a 3D printer construct a disappointment in real-time. 

First came the scuffed loafers. Then the unremarkable, slightly wrinkled slacks. Finally, a pale, glowing blue torso clad in a short-sleeved dress shirt and a crooked tie materialized, topped with a face that radiated pure, unadulterated administrative fatigue. 

He didn't look like a demon or an angel. He looked like an accountant who had died of a heart attack on a Tuesday and had been forced to clock in on Wednesday. The System didn't waste processing power on intimidating avatars for its low-level clerks. It opted for the banal horror of middle management.

The holographic figure let out a long, static-laced sigh. He reached up, pinching the bridge of his nose with two glowing fingers. 

"Do you have any idea," the figure said, his voice a dry, bureaucratic monotone that echoed slightly in the quiet clearing, "how much paperwork is generated when a zero-tier indentured soul bypasses a foundational economic loop?"

Rhea blinked, keeping the bludgeon raised and her left hand securely closed around her credits. "I'm assuming more than four coppers' worth."

"Exponentially more," the clerk replied, dropping his hand and pulling a translucent digital clipboard from thin air. He tapped the screen, his dark eyes scanning the data with the enthusiasm of a man reading a dictionary. "I was in the middle of auditing a localized gravity inversion in Sector Four. A very delicate, very annoying process involving three rogue physics engines and a localized black hole. And then my queue gets pinged because some freshly dropped asset in the tutorial zone decided to classify biological salvage as 'Environmental Debris.' I'm Vex. I'm the local system clerk. And you are a headache."

"I'm Rhea," she said, her tone matching his dryness. "And I read the prompt. The drop-down menu explicitly offered 'Environmental Debris Clearance' as a tax-exempt public service. I provided a public service. That riverbed was a mess. The scavenger was creating a tripping hazard."

Vex lowered the clipboard, fixing her with a stare that managed to be intensely cynical despite his eyes being made of blue light. "You dropped a petrified tree on a scavenger and mashed its corpse into a paste with a rock."

"A Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon," Rhea corrected, hefting the weapon slightly to demonstrate its heft. "And yes. The tree fell. The debris was cleared. I expect a commendation. Or at least a tax rebate."

[Audit Flag: Review Pending.]
[Warning: Administrative Action Imminent.]

"The System doesn't do commendations for tax evasion," Vex said, tapping his clipboard again. "The gathering tax is a core mechanic. It ensures that indentured souls recognize the value of labor and the necessity of their debt. By bypassing the Stamina deduction, you’ve introduced a mathematical discrepancy into the local node. The ledger expects a certain amount of suffering per transaction. You didn't suffer."

"I'm at twenty-five hit points," Rhea snapped, gesturing to her bruised leg with her chin. "I'm suffering plenty. My left leg feels like it was used as a speed bump by a semi-truck."

"Not economically," Vex countered, completely unmoved by her physical plight. "Which is the only kind of suffering the Ledger cares about. I am well within my rights to issue an administrative ban. Or, at the very least, apply a localized gravity multiplier to your immediate vicinity until your internal organs rupture and the debt is settled via asset liquidation."

Rhea's mind raced. An administrative ban sounded bad. Organ rupture sounded definitively worse. She needed leverage. She needed to argue the mechanics of the world, because clearly, appealing to his humanity was a non-starter given he was made of hard-light and bureaucracy. 

"If you ban me, you have to log the reason," Rhea said slowly, piecing the logic together. "Which means you have to admit to the wider System that your local node has a basic logic flaw in its UI. A flaw that a zero-tier arrival found in ten minutes with a broken smartphone in her pocket and zero points in Intelligence."

Vex’s glowing blue fingers froze over his clipboard. The faint hum of his projection stuttered for a fraction of a second. The ambient temperature around him seemed to drop a degree.

Rhea saw the hesitation and leaned in. "Yeah. That’s what I thought. If you flag this as an exploit, it goes up the chain. You get audited. Your boss gets audited. And suddenly, everyone is looking at Ashwood's ledgers to see what else you've misclassified."

Vex narrowed his eyes, the blue light shifting to a slightly darker, more threatening shade. "You are remarkably irritating for someone with a hundred thousand points of Soul Debt."

"I'm highly motivated," Rhea said, offering a thin, humorless smile. 

Before Vex could formulate a rebuttal, a sharp, distinct sound cut through the tense air. 

Crunch.

Rhea spun, her right hand bringing the heavy masonry bludgeon up into a defensive guard, her left hand tucking the Copper Credits tightly against her chest. Vex didn't flinch, his holographic form simply rotating his head toward the noise with a look of profound, world-weary annoyance.

The ash-choked brush to Rhea's right parted. 

A man stepped out of the shadows of the petrified trees. If Rhea looked like she had survived a car crash, this guy looked like he had been dragged behind the car for a few miles over broken glass. His clothes were torn to ribbons, hanging off his frame in jagged strips. His face was smeared with gray ash and dried blood, and his left arm hung limply at his side at a distinctly unnatural angle. He was breathing heavily, his chest heaving with every ragged intake of air, his eyes wide and feverish. 

Artie wasn't just a threat because he was desperate; he was a threat because he was a living, breathing warning. He was what happened when you played by the System's rules. You tried to gather wood to build a fire, the System taxed your stamina, your stamina bottomed out, and the System started eating your health. It was a predatory payday loan with a mortality rate. And Artie was about to default.

But he wasn't looking at the bludgeon. He wasn't looking at the glowing blue system clerk. 

He was staring directly at Rhea's closed left fist.

"How?" the man rasped, his voice sounding like two stones grinding together at the bottom of a dry well. 

Rhea took a slow step back, keeping Vex in her peripheral vision while focusing entirely on the new arrival. "How what?"

"How did you get paid?" The man took a stumbling step forward, his boots dragging through the ash. "I watched you. From the ridge. I watched you drop the tree. I watched you mash the fungus." He coughed, a wet, rattling sound that made Rhea's own ribs ache in sympathy. "I tried to pick up a branch. Just a loose branch on the ground. The System hit me with a ten-point Stamina tax. I didn't have ten points. It took it out of my health." 

He tapped his chest, where a dark, spreading stain of bruised purple indicated severe internal damage. "I'm at four hit points. I'm bleeding out because I tried to pick up a stick. And you... you just walked up to the node and got paid."

[System Notification: Unregistered Asset Proximity.]
[Entity Identification: Artie.]

"Artie, I presume," Vex said, glancing at his clipboard without a shred of empathy. "Indentured Soul #884-B. Currently suffering from Stage Two Systemic Shock and Minor Hemorrhage. You are statistically scheduled to expire in approximately four minutes and twelve seconds. Please ensure you are not blocking a thoroughfare when you collapse."

Artie ignored the hologram entirely, his desperate, bloodshot eyes locked entirely on Rhea. "Show me. Tell me how you bypassed the prompt. Please. I don't want to die here. Not over a stick."

Rhea’s grip on the bludgeon tightened until her knuckles turned white under the grime. She was at twenty-five HP. She was exhausted, bruised, and carrying the only untaxed currency in a ten-mile radius. 

On her left, Vex stood with his digital clipboard, representing the crushing, infinite bureaucracy of the System, ready to audit her into oblivion to save his own paperwork. 

On her right, Artie stood swaying in the ash, representing the desperate, violent reality of the frontier zone, ready to drag her down with him if she didn't hand over her lifeline.

Rhea was trapped between Vex's audit and Artie's desperate approach. The math was catching up, and for the first time since she dropped into the wasteland, she wasn't sure she had the numbers to survive the equation.
