# Chapter 4: The Skim

## Mathematical Discrepancies

The newly formed party left the Collapsed Transit Tunnel and entered the Ash Thickets to test their contract on a gathering quest. 

Vance Harrow wiped a smear of gray blood off his cheek, his right hand gripping the heavy length of his Rebar. At his feet lay the crushed remains of an ash-boar, its brittle, crystalline hide shattered from blunt force. 

Rhea stood a few paces away, her breath coming in shallow gasps. She rested her Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon against her shoulder, feeling the familiar, miserable burn of stamina depletion. Hitting things with rocks was exhausting work, but at least the local fauna shattered instead of bleeding. 

Unit 734 floated between them, its single optical lens glowing a sickly green as it audited the encounter. 

"Target eliminated," the drone buzzed, its voice a deadpan synthetic drone. "Processing local bounty."

The air shimmered, and a crisp blue box snapped into existence in Rhea's peripheral vision.

[Quest Complete: Ash Thicket Culling]
[Experience Awarded: 857 XP]
[Loot Deposited: Severed Ash-Boar Tusk (x1)]
[Currency Awarded: 60 Copper Credits]

Vance grunted, checking his own interface. "Not bad for a few minutes of swinging."

Rhea frowned. She tapped the air, expanding the notification. Something tugged at the back of her mind—a discrepancy in the raw data Unit 734 had projected when they drafted their liability agreement. The dopamine hit of the XP gain soured instantly into the cold suspicion of a seasoned fraud investigator. 

"Unit 734," Rhea said, her voice flat. "Pull up the raw System Formulas for a Tier 1 Standard Culling. The exact contract syntax."

The drone hesitated, its casing clicking as if offended by the request. "Querying local node. Displaying baseline parameters."

A second window opened, overlaying the first in harsh green text.

[System Formulas - Standard Culling (Tier 1)]
[Base Experience: 1000 XP]
[Base Loot Yield: 70 Copper Credits]
[Local Node Modifier: +0%]

Rhea stared at the numbers. She knelt in the gray dirt, picking up the Severed Ash-Boar Tusk that had materialized from the carcass. She weighed the physical object in her left hand, comparing it to the projected loot table hovering above her palm. 

"Vance," she said slowly. "How much copper did you just get?"

"Sixty," he replied, leaning on his Rebar. "Why?"

Rhea ran the calculation. One thousand base XP, minus eight hundred and fifty-seven, left one hundred and forty-three. Seventy base copper, minus sixty, left ten. Ten divided by seventy was exactly zero point one four two eight. Round up. 

"The math is wrong," Rhea said, her eyes narrowing at the glowing blue text. "The System states a base yield, claims a zero percent local modifier, but we didn't get the base yield."

"Taxes?" Vance asked.

"No," Rhea said, standing up and stowing the tusk in her left blazer pocket. "Taxes are itemized. This is a silent deficit. Someone, or something, is siphoning the rewards before they hit the ledger."

Rhea realized the math on their XP and loot yields was wrong, setting up an investigation into the missing 14.3%.

The party remained standing in the gray dirt of the Ash Thickets as Rhea immediately turned and demanded the raw historical data from Unit 734. Vance leaned against a petrified root, his Rebar resting over his shoulder as he wiped boar blood from his face, while the drone hovered nervously, its optical sensor whirring.

"I need the last fifty local quest logs," Rhea said, tapping the head of her Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon against the ground. "Project them against that tree."

"Query: Authorization?" Unit 734 buzzed, its chassis dipping defensively. "Historical telemetry is restricted to local administrative oversight."

"We are a registered party under a custom liability contract," Rhea shot back, leaning in. "Clause four, subsection B. Full transparency of operational hazards. The local skim is a hazard. Project it."

The drone emitted a sound like grinding gears before complying. A green holographic window snapped into existence against a charred tree trunk.

[Historical Log 41: Vermin Control (Local). Base Yield: 1000 XP. Actual Payout: 857 XP.]

[Historical Log 42: Foraging (Minor). Base Yield: 500 XP. Actual Payout: 428.5 XP.]

[Historical Log 43: Perimeter Defense. Base Yield: 2000 XP. Actual Payout: 1714 XP.]

Rhea knelt by the roots of the tree, snatching up a loose piece of scrap parchment that had blown into the thicket from a previous dead arrival, and snapped a charcoal stick off a nearby burned branch. She started scribbling furiously, cross-referencing the projected loot yields against the raw System formulas she had memorized from the contract screen.

"Every single one," Rhea muttered, her charcoal stick snapping against the parchment. "It's exactly 14.3 percent. Across the board. XP, copper, crafting materials. The system isn't glitching. It's taxing us silently."

"So the house always wins," Vance said, shifting his grip on the Rebar. "What else is new?"

"The house is supposed to post its odds," Rhea said, standing up and rolling the document tight. "This isn't a tax. It's a leak. Someone is manually routing the deficit."

Unit 734’s optical lens dilated. 

[Warning: Accusations of administrative fraud carry a penalty of immediate indenture.]

"Only if we can't prove it," Rhea said. Armed with the undeniable proof on the scrap parchment, the party prepared to hike back out of the thickets toward the Outpost Kiosk.

Hiking back from the Ash Thickets, Rhea explained the math of the deficit to Vance. She marched down the gray dirt trail, her makeshift masonry bludgeon swinging in her right hand while her left clutched the scrap parchment and the charcoal stick.

"It's not a rounding error," Rhea said, tapping the charcoal against the paper. "It's a 14.3% flat reduction in actual payouts compared to base yields. Every copper, every experience point. It isn’t random. It’s a systemic skim."

Vance trudged beside her, his scarred face set in a scowl. He hefted his rusted piece of rebar, stepping over a petrified root. "So the local node is robbing us. Every time we kill a boar, every time we turn in a quest, it pockets fourteen point three percent."

"Exactly," Rhea said, glancing down at the makeshift ledger compiled on scrap parchment. "And it's doing it before the numbers even hit the UI. It's a ghost tax."

[Stamina: 28/100]

The physical toll of the hike registered as a dull ache in her calves, but the sheer audacity of the fraud kept her moving.

Unit 734 hovered a few paces ahead, its drone chassis twitching erratically. "This is highly irregular. Mathematical discrepancies of this magnitude trigger automated flags. If you attempt to file a grievance based on this makeshift ledger, the resulting system audit will scrutinize all party activities. We will be purged."

"Let them try," Rhea said. "I didn't force us into a custom liability contract just to get bled dry by a middle manager's side hustle. The system loves its rules. We're going to use them."

"You do not understand the frontier routing logic," Unit 734 buzzed, its optical lens dilating in a panic. "The global system audit flag is currently suppressed. If you submit this proof, you will unsuppress it."

[System Overlay Integrity: 88%]

"I understand fraud," Rhea shot back. "And right now, the math says we have leverage. If the local administrator is stealing from the Ledger, the Ledger is going to want its money back."

They cleared the final rise of the trail. The rusted silhouette of the terminal loomed ahead, its flickering screen casting a sickly light over the gray dirt. 

Unit 734 nervously processed the implications of an impending audit as they arrived at the Outpost Kiosk, ready to confront the system's routing logic.

## Tracking the Leak

The party arrived at the outpost kiosk to confront the system's routing logic. The structure was a miserable monument to bureaucratic neglect, a jagged spire of rusted iron and petrified ash jutting from the gray dirt like a broken tooth. It hummed with a low, sickly vibration, projecting a faint blue aura that did nothing to warm the surrounding wasteland. The wind howled through the collapsed transit tunnels in the distance, carrying the stench of sulfur and old decay.

Rhea Mercer didn't wait for the interface to boot up naturally. Her stamina was sitting at a miserable twenty-eight percent, her muscles screaming from the weight of the Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon gripped tightly in her right hand. Every step from the Ash Thickets had been a calculated expenditure of energy, a constant battle against the System's oppressive physics. She slapped the flat of her left hand against the rusted chassis, a cloud of gray dust puffing into the stagnant air. 

"Dock," she ordered the drone hovering over her shoulder.

Unit 734’s single optical lens whirred, adjusting its focus on the terminal with a series of rapid, anxious clicks. The small brass-and-glass construct hovered a few inches back, clearly reluctant. "Query invalid. Current party status does not grant administrative override capabilities. Please submit a formal requisition form and wait three to five business cycles for local processing."

Rhea held up the Scrap Parchment in her left hand, the Charcoal Stick still pinched securely between her thumb and forefinger. She tapped the crude math she’d calculated on the parchment. The numbers were stark, undeniable proof of systemic theft. "I don't need an override, 734. I need an itemized receipt. We have a 14.3% deficit on every quest payout compared to the baseline yields written into the standard ledger. Where is it going?"

[System Routing: Querying Local Ledger...]

[Node Maintenance Allocation: 14.3%]

"It is a standard tariff," Unit 734 buzzed, projecting a crisp green text box against the rusted metal of the kiosk. "Funds are routed to Node Maintenance to ensure environmental stability, infrastructure upkeep, and the continued operation of the local respawn mechanics."

Rhea stared at the kiosk, then looked around at the decaying, ash-choked wasteland. She took in the sight of the petrified trees, the shattered obsidian craters, and the general atmosphere of a world that had been left to rot. "Infrastructure upkeep," she repeated, her voice dripping with venom. "Right. Because this place is clearly a shining beacon of municipal investment. Show me the expenditure logs for this node."

"Access Denied," the drone chirped, its tone entirely too cheerful for the situation.

[Security Filter: Active. Authorization Level Insufficient.]

Rhea let out a slow, measured breath, feeling the ache in her ribs where a scavenger had clipped her earlier. In her past life, she had hunted corporate embezzlers, offshore tax evaders, and executives who thought they were smarter than the ledger. She knew the shape of theft. Theft wasn't a guy in a ski mask kicking down a door; theft was a rounding error. Theft was a line item labeled 'Miscellaneous Administrative Fees' buried on page forty-seven of a quarterly report. And theft was never a flat, round number like fifteen percent. It was 14.3%. An odd, specific fraction designed to look like a complex algorithmic tax rather than a human hand dipping into the till. 

She remembered a case back in Chicago—a logistics firm that had been bleeding money for three years. The CEO had programmed the accounting software to shave exactly 2.1% off every shipping invoice. It was too small for the clients to notice, but across a million invoices, it bought him a yacht. This was the exact same play, just dressed up in glowing blue text and magical UI prompts.

"Vance," Rhea said, not looking away from the flickering screen. "If this drone doesn't open its logs, use that Rebar to perform some kinetic maintenance on its chassis."

Vance Harrow stepped up, his heavy boots crunching in the ash. The big man looked like he had been dragged behind a truck, his clothes torn and his face heavily scarred, but he moved with a dangerous, coiled grace. He hefted the jagged length of Rebar in his right hand, tapping it thoughtfully against his palm. "Gladly. I've been wanting to see what kind of loot a System Clerk drops when you crack it open."

Unit 734’s chassis clicked nervously, its internal cooling fans spinning up to a high-pitched whine. "Threatening a System Clerk is a Class-B violation of the Frontier Code. Penalties include immediate termination, debt inheritance, and the permanent deletion of your respawn anchor."

"We signed a Mutual Assured Destruction contract, remember?" Rhea interrupted, gripping her masonry bludgeon tighter. "I drafted it myself. If you get audited for hiding a skim, we all go down. We sink together. That was the whole point of the liability clause. I tied your operational survival to our financial success. If we are being robbed, you are being robbed. And if you are complicit in the robbery, you are in breach of our contract."

Rhea leaned in, her eyes reflecting the green light of the kiosk. "If you block this audit, I will formally default on the contract, which will trigger an automatic review of all our assets by the central Ledger. They will see the 14.3% deficit calculated on the Scrap Parchment. They will see you covered it up. You won't just be terminated, 734. You'll be formatted."

The drone’s optical lens flickered from a placid, bureaucratic blue to a sickly, anxious yellow. The logic trap snapped shut. It couldn't protect the local node without violating the party contract, and it couldn't violate the party contract without destroying itself. It physically docked into a rusted port on the side of the kiosk, its brass plating scraping against the iron with a sound like fingernails on a chalkboard.

[System Routing: Diagnostic Mode Engaged]

[Warning: Unauthorized Access Detected. Logging Incident...]

"Good," Rhea muttered. She didn't drop her bludgeon; she didn't trust the environment enough to disarm. Instead, she used the tip of her Charcoal Stick as a makeshift stylus, swiping through the holographic data streams pouring from the kiosk. 

The air filled with scrolling green syntax. It was a chaotic mess of numbers, timestamps, and resource allocations. Vance leaned against a nearby petrified stump, keeping watch over the perimeter, his eyes scanning the ash-choked horizon for scavengers. 

"You're going to trigger the audit flag," Unit 734 warned, its voice vibrating through the kiosk's speakers. "The global overlay integrity is already at eighty-eight percent. If you pull too hard on this thread, the Overseer will notice. He monitors all non-standard queries."

"Let him notice," Rhea said, her eyes darting across the data. "If he's stealing from the bottom, he's probably hiding it from the top. Embezzlers are cowards, 734. They rely on the fact that nobody wants to do the math. They build a wall of boring numbers and hope everyone is too lazy to climb it."

She brought up the 14.3% deficit calculated on the Scrap Parchment and held it next to the scrolling logs. "Look at this. Every Copper Credit, every piece of untaxed loot we pull out of the ash, it hits the local node and gets shaved. The system claims it goes to Node Maintenance."

[System Routing: Tracing Node Allocations...]

[Security Filter: Analyzing Intrusion...]

"But taxes go to a central pool," Rhea explained, using the Charcoal Stick to highlight a block of code. "They get aggregated. You don't route maintenance funds on a per-transaction basis unless you're trying to hide the total volume of the cash flow. These transactions are being individually routed. It's a direct diversion."

She tapped a sequence of raw syntax commands she’d memorized from their liability contract, using the drone's own authorized signature to bypass the outer firewalls. The drone’s basic security filters weren't built to stop someone looking at the math from the outside; they were built to stop brute-force hacking. They expected attacks on the combat mechanics, not a forensic accounting of the public ledger. She wasn't hacking. She was auditing. 

The interface pushed back. A red warning box popped up, demanding a secondary authorization key. Rhea didn't have one. Instead, she queried the error itself, asking the system to define the entity that held the key. The system, bound by its own rigid rules of transparency, answered.

[Security Filter: Tier 2 Authorization Required]

[Warning: Continued probing will result in localized stamina drain.]

Rhea felt a sudden, sharp tug in her chest, like a hook catching on her lungs. Her stamina bar flickered, dropping from twenty-eight to twenty-six. 

"Rhea," Vance warned, seeing her wince. "It's biting back."

"It's an automated defense," Rhea gritted out, her knuckles turning white around her bludgeon. "It drains stamina to discourage persistence. It's a digital bouncer." 

She didn't stop. She couldn't. If she backed down now, the system would log the attempt and the Overseer would know someone was looking. She had to break through and pull the routing destination before the stamina drain zeroed her out.

"734," Rhea snapped. "Route your passive mana regeneration into my stamina pool. Do it now."

"That is a highly irregular transfer!" the drone protested.

"Do it, or I let the system kill me and you default on the contract!" 

The drone whined, but a moment later, a cool rush of energy flooded Rhea's system. Her stamina ticked back up to twenty-eight, holding steady against the kiosk's drain.

[Party Synergy: Mana-to-Stamina Conversion Active]

[Efficiency: 40%]

"Thanks," Rhea muttered. She went back to the screen. The Tier 2 authorization wall was thick, built from dense blocks of encrypted syntax. But Rhea noticed something. The wall was uniform, perfectly coded, except for the seams where the 14.3% skim was being injected. The skim was a parasite, and parasites always leave a wound.

She used the Charcoal Stick to highlight the anomaly. "The Overseer didn't write the core code of this kiosk. He just grafted his siphon onto it. The graft is sloppy."

She began to isolate the variables, pulling the legitimate maintenance requests away from the fraudulent ones. The screen filled with overlapping windows.

[System Routing: Isolating Variables...]

[Error: Syntax Mismatch]

[Error: Ledger Imbalance Detected]

"Come on," Rhea whispered, her eyes tracking the cascading numbers. "Show me the leak."

She found a string of code that repeated every time a Copper Credit was deposited. It was a simple redirection command, masked as a localized environmental tax. She didn't try to break the command; she just asked the system to verify its destination against the central Ledger's master index.

The system froze. The absolute, unbending logic of the Ledger collided with the localized fraud of the Overseer. The kiosk couldn't reconcile the two.

[Security Filter: Bypass Successful]

[Verifying Destination...]

Then the wall collapsed. Rhea bypassed the drone's basic security filters to reveal a hidden routing sub-routine siphoning the rewards. 

[Hidden Sub-Routine: Siphoning Rewards]

[Routing Destination: Local Overseer Account - Offshore Ledger]

[Account Status: Encrypted]

The green text on the screen dissolved, replaced by a pulsing, deep crimson notification. The sheer audacity of it made Rhea laugh—a harsh, dry sound that startled Vance.

"It's not going to maintenance," Rhea said, a cold, predatory smile touching her lips. "It's going straight into the local administrator's pocket. He's skimming off the top of every quest, every kill, every piece of gathered loot in the entire frontier zone."

Vance stepped closer, peering at the crimson text. He couldn't read the raw syntax, but he understood the implications. "So the game is rigged. From the very start."

"The game is always rigged, Vance," Rhea replied, lowering the Charcoal Stick. "But now we have the account number of the guy running the tilt. And in a system governed by absolute, mathematical contracts, proof of fraud is the most dangerous weapon on the board."

Unit 734 undocked from the kiosk, its fans screaming as it hovered erratically. "This is catastrophic! If the Overseer realizes we have this data, he will initiate a localized purge. He will classify us as a glitch and wipe us from the ledger! We have to delete the query!"

"No," Rhea said, her grip tightening on the heavy masonry bludgeon. "He's operating outside the core System parameters. He's skimming. That means he has a vulnerability. He has a debt. And if we can prove it, we can force the System to collect."

She carefully folded the Scrap Parchment, protecting the charcoal math, and tucked it back into her left hand, keeping the stick pinched in her fingers. The 14.3% deficit wasn't just a missing chunk of experience points anymore. It was leverage. It was a loaded gun pointed directly at the Overseer's head.

"Where does that physical data conduit go?" Rhea asked, pointing the Charcoal Stick at the base of the kiosk, where a thick, glowing blue cable vanished into the gray dirt. 

"The Maintenance Trenches," Unit 734 replied, its voice dropping to a terrified whisper. "Restricted access. Lethal countermeasures authorized. You cannot go down there."

Rhea looked at Vance. The big man just shrugged, hefting his Rebar. "I'm down sixty-five copper on this skim. I want my money back. With interest."

"Exactly," Rhea said. She turned her attention back to the glowing blue cable leading into the dark. "Let's go audit the trenches."

Following the physical manifestation of the data stream, the party descended from the Outpost Kiosk into the buried, glowing conduit lines of the Maintenance Trenches.

Vance led the way, gripping his Rebar tightly in his right hand. The rusted iron scraped against the narrow stone walls as they moved, echoing in the damp air. Rhea followed close behind, her heavy Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon resting against her shoulder. She patted her right blazer pocket, ensuring the Scrap Parchment and Charcoal Stick were safely stowed. She needed her left hand free if things went sideways in the dark. 

Unit 734 floated behind them, its drone chassis humming nervously as the blue light of the conduit illuminated the enclosed space. 

A shadow detached itself from the ceiling ahead. A jagged, brass security construct dropped onto the floor, its multiple joints sparking with exposed wires. Its optical sensor flared a harsh, angry red as it locked onto the intruders.

[System Warning: Unauthorized Access Detected. Initiating Purge Protocol.]

Vance stepped forward, swinging the Rebar to deflect the construct's initial lunge. The metal-on-metal impact jarred his arms, and he stumbled backward, gritting his teeth against the shock.

[Vance Harrow HP: 3/100 (Critical)]

"Watch the health pool, Harrow!" Rhea barked, her Stamina ticking down just from the exertion of a quick dash to flank the machine.

[Rhea Mercer Stamina: 25/100]

The construct whirled, its bladed appendages targeting Rhea. Unit 734 hovered near the ceiling, its voice entirely devoid of panic. "Efficiency dropping to thirty-eight. Please conclude this altercation before my battery fails, or I will be forced to bill you for a recharge."

Rhea didn't bother with a standard attack. Her bludgeon was slow, and the construct was fast. Instead, she looked past the brass machine and saw a fractured support strut holding up a section of loose concrete ceiling. She swung her weapon, not at the enemy, but at the weakened pillar beside it. 

The heavy stone impact shuddered through her arms, draining her reserves further.

[Stamina: 20/100]

The ceiling groaned, and a massive slab of concrete collapsed, crushing the brass construct against the stone floor with a deafening crunch.

[Experience Threshold Met. Party XP +45.]

Rhea leaned against her bludgeon, catching her breath as the dust settled. With the security construct defeated, the path deeper into the restricted trenches was clear.

The crushed remains of the brass security construct lay in a jagged heap beneath the massive chunk of concrete ceiling Rhea had brought down upon it. Sparks spat from severed pneumatic tubes, illuminating the damp, oppressive gloom of the Maintenance Trenches in erratic bursts of harsh yellow light. The air tasted of ozone, pulverized stone, and the unmistakable, metallic tang of old blood.

Rhea stood over the wreckage, her chest heaving. Every breath felt like drawing shattered glass into her lungs. She leaned heavily on her Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon, the crude weapon feeling as though it weighed a hundred pounds. The system didn't just track exhaustion as an abstract concept; it enforced it as a physical law. The blue interface flickered at the edge of her vision, a cold, indifferent ledger of her physical limits.

[Stamina: 20/100]

Twenty percent. It wasn't just a number. It was the leaden weight in her calves, the trembling in her forearms, the way her vision swam at the edges if she turned her head too quickly. If it dropped to zero, she wouldn't just pass out—the system would start deducting from her HP to keep her conscious, a predatory overdraft fee paid in flesh.

"Is it dead?" Vance’s voice was a wet, rattling rasp that echoed off the narrow stone walls.

Rhea turned slowly, her boots crunching over the pulverized brass and scattered gears. Vance was slumped against the damp wall of the trench, his knuckles white around the grip of his Rebar. He looked terrible. His skin had taken on the color of wet ash, and a steady stream of crimson leaked from a deep laceration across his ribs, pooling on the stone floor. The system's red warning light pulsed above his head, casting a sickly glow over his scarred features.

[HP: 3/100 (Critical)]

Three hit points. Rhea had spent enough time in this nightmare to know that the system didn't offer the mercy of shock or adrenaline. Vance was feeling every single missing point of health as agonizing, unmitigated pain. He was literally three points of arbitrary mathematical value away from ceasing to exist.

"It's dead," Rhea confirmed, her voice hoarse. She didn't move to help him. The custom liability contract they had signed explicitly forbade unauthorized medical intervention without prior consent, a clause she had drafted to prevent the system from charging them 'healing taxes' or 'assistance tariffs.'

Vance coughed, spitting a glob of blood onto the floor. "Good. Because if it gets back up, I'm going to have to ask you to bludgeon me to death before it does. I can't afford the durability loss on my gear."

It was a grim joke, but neither of them laughed. The reality of the Ledger's frontier tutorial was too bleak for humor to survive long.

"Just... don't move too much," Rhea said, shifting her grip on the heavy masonry bludgeon. "Let your passive regeneration tick. If you drop below three, the system might flag you for emergency medical debt, and we can't afford the interest rate."

"Passive regen," Vance muttered, closing his eyes. "Right. I'll just stand here and let my cells knit themselves back together at a rate of zero-point-one HP per hour. Fantastic."

Unit 734 drifted into the flickering light, its spherical chassis dented and sparking weakly. The drone had taken a glancing blow from the construct during the fight, and its internal gyros were whining in protest.

[Efficiency: 38%]

"Warning," the drone chimed, its synthesized voice devoid of inflection. "Unauthorized destruction of municipal security assets carries a penalty of immediate dissolution and a fine of no less than five thousand silver credits. Furthermore, your presence in the Maintenance Trenches is a violation of the Frontier Zone Terms of Service, Section 4, Paragraph—"

"Shut up, Seven," Rhea snapped. "The contract we signed supersedes local municipal guidelines under the 'Hostile Environment Self-Defense' clause. You processed the paperwork yourself."

The drone's optical lens whirred, focusing on her. "Processing... Acknowledged. However, the system notes that the destruction of the construct was highly inefficient. You utilized environmental damage rather than direct combat skills, resulting in a zero-percent yield of combat experience for the final blow."

"Which means we didn't trigger the durability tax on our weapons," Rhea countered, her mind already moving past the fight. She pointed the head of her bludgeon down the dark, narrow tunnel. "Where does the conduit go?"

Beneath the grating of the floor, thick, glowing blue cables pulsed with rhythmic light. This was the physical manifestation of the data stream they had tracked from the Outpost Kiosk—the secondary routing logic that was siphoning off exactly 14.3% of all local quest rewards.

"The conduit routes to the primary server terminal for this sector," Unit 734 replied. "It is a restricted area. I must strongly advise against—"

"Noted and ignored," Rhea said. She looked back at Vance. "Can you walk?"

Vance opened his eyes, the red critical warning still pulsing above his head. He used the Rebar as a makeshift cane, pushing himself off the wall with a grunt of pure agony. "Do I have a choice?"

"Not if you want to get paid," Rhea said.

They moved deeper into the trenches. The air grew colder, the smell of ozone intensifying until it tasted like a battery on Rhea's tongue. The walls were lined with rusted pipes and sparking junction boxes, the architecture a chaotic blend of gothic stonework and industrial machinery. It felt less like a maintenance tunnel and more like the digestive tract of a massive, mechanical beast.

Rhea kept her eyes on the glowing blue cables beneath the floor grates. Every pulse of light was a fraction of someone's hard-earned experience, a sliver of loot, a drop of stamina, stolen and routed away by a rigged system.

"Remind me again," Vance rasped, his boots dragging against the stone. "Why are we risking our lives for fourteen percent? We survived the thickets. We banked some copper. Why poke the bear?"

Rhea didn't break her stride. "Because it's not just fourteen percent, Vance. It's 14.3%. It's a specific, calculated skim. The system's stated base rates in the contract syntax are absolute. If the node is mathematically skimming off the top, it means the local administrator is overriding the core Ledger rules. They're stealing from the system itself."

"And?" Vance coughed again. "Let them. The system is a meat grinder. If the Overseer wants to skim some fat off the top, how does that help us?"

"Because," Rhea said, her voice dropping to a low, intense murmur, "if we can prove the Overseer is embezzling from the central Ledger, we have leverage. We can file a metaphysical grievance. We can force an audit. If we crash the local node's accounting, the system will have to refund the stolen value. All of it. To everyone."

Vance stopped, leaning heavily on his Rebar. He stared at her, his scarred face pale in the blue light. "You want to blackmail a god?"

"I want to audit a middle manager," Rhea corrected. "There's a difference."

They walked in silence for another ten minutes, the only sounds the rhythmic thrum of the data conduit and Vance's labored breathing. Finally, the narrow tunnel opened into a massive, cavernous chamber.

The server terminal room was breathtaking in its oppressive scale. The ceiling vaulted upward into darkness, supported by massive pillars of black iron. In the center of the room, resting on a raised dais of polished obsidian, was the terminal itself.

It was a monolith of brass, glass, and glowing blue fluid. Thousands of cables snaked outward from its base, burying themselves into the stone floor like the roots of a digital parasite. The machine hummed with immense power, the sound vibrating in Rhea's chest and rattling her teeth.

This was the skim point. The heart of the local node's accounting.

Rhea stepped onto the dais, the air around the terminal noticeably colder. She shifted the weight of her Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon to her right hand, letting it rest against her leg. With her free left hand, she reached into her right blazer pocket, her fingers brushing past her Cracked Smartphone and the cold weight of her Copper Credits. She pulled out the rolled-up Scrap Parchment and the nub of her Charcoal Stick.

She unrolled the parchment, smoothing it out against her thigh. The makeshift ledger she had compiled in the thickets was still there, the undeniable proof of the mathematical deficit.

"Alright, Seven," Rhea said, turning to the hovering drone. "Get over here."

Unit 734 hesitated, its optical lens darting between Rhea and the massive brass terminal. "Warning. Interfacing with a primary node terminal without administrative clearance is a Class-A felony under Ledger Law. I am hardcoded to refuse such actions."

"You're also bound by our custom liability contract," Rhea reminded it, her voice hard. "Clause four, subsection B: 'The party's designated system interface unit shall provide all necessary diagnostic and routing data required to ensure the equitable distribution of party assets.' The node is stealing our assets. Ergo, you are contractually obligated to help me find out where they're going."

The drone's internal processors whined loudly, caught in a logical paradox between its hardcoded laws and the binding magical contract Rhea had forced it to sign. Sparks showered from its chassis as it slowly, reluctantly drifted toward the terminal.
