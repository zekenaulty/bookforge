# Chapter 2: Tariffs and Teeth

## The Cost of Breathing

Rhea leaves the Crater Ridge, descending into a sparse, ash-choked forest in search of sustenance. The landscape was a monochromatic nightmare of jagged obsidian and pale, powdery dust that clung to her clothes and coated the back of her throat. Every step down the steep incline jarred her bruised left leg, sending a jolt of pain that served as a constant, nagging reminder of her fragile mortality. 

She kept her eyes locked on the hovering blue rectangle in her peripheral vision.

[HP: 1/100]

One hit point. It was a statistical rounding error away from a corpse. Assuming the Tutorial Grace Period didn't catch her first, a bad trip over a root could theoretically snap her neck and send her back to whatever bureaucratic hell spawned this place. 

She gripped the Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon tightly in her right hand. The weapon was crude—just a heavy chunk of raw, glowing stone she had carved a handle into—but it possessed a beautiful, glitchy perfection. When she had equipped it, the System interface had flickered purple, failing to apply a tax assessment to the unregistered asset. It was her first debt-free advantage. A tiny, physical middle finger to the Indentured Soul title hovering in her status menu.

But a stone club couldn't hydrate her, and she couldn't eat it. Her stomach gave a hollow, aching rumble. 

She reached the bottom of the incline, her boots crunching on a bed of petrified gray leaves. The ash-choked forest stretched out before her, a graveyard of pale, dead trees that looked like they had been flash-calcified by a volcanic eruption. The air here was stagnant, smelling of sulfur and old dust. 

She needed a free hand to scavenge. Rhea carefully slipped the sharp Obsidian Shard from her left hand, stowing it safely in her left blazer pocket. The Cracked Smartphone rested heavily in her right pocket, a useless brick of dead technology that she refused to discard out of sheer stubbornness. 

She navigated the dead woods, scanning the bases of the petrified trunks. The silence was absolute, broken only by her own ragged breathing and the crunch of her footsteps. 

After ten minutes of wandering, a faint, bioluminescent glow caught her eye. 

Growing from the gray, stone-like bark of a massive petrified oak was a cluster of fruit-like fungus. It pulsed with a soft, sickly-sweet blue light, looking like a cross between a bloated mushroom and a bruised peach. It was the only living thing she had seen since the drop. 

Rhea approached the tree, her eyes narrowing. She didn't trust the System. She didn't trust the glowing Starter Cache on the ridge, and she certainly didn't trust glowing forest food. But her throat was parched, and her stamina bar—currently sitting at a pristine maximum—was the only resource she had left in abundance. 

[Stamina: 100/100]

She reached out, her fingers closing around the spongy cap of the largest Ash-Fungus. It felt wet and cold, like raw meat. Bracing her boots against the ash, she gave it a sharp pull, tearing it free from the petrified bark with a wet, ripping sound. 

Instantly, a crisp blue notification box snapped into existence, accompanied by a sound like a slamming cash register.

[Gathering Action Detected: Ash-Fungus.]
[Assessing Local Node Tariffs...]
[Gathering Tax Applied: -85 Stamina.]

A sudden, violent cramp seized her chest. 

Rhea gasped, her lungs burning as if the air had been instantly sucked out of the forest. Her knees buckled. She collapsed against the petrified bark, clutching the glowing mushroom to her chest while her vision swam with dark spots. The physical backlash was immediate, a heavy, suffocating exhaustion that made her limbs feel like lead. Her muscles screamed in protest, twitching with sudden lactic acid buildup.

[Stamina: 15/100]

She slid down the trunk, hitting the ash-covered ground hard. She couldn't breathe. It felt like she had just sprinted a marathon while breathing through a cocktail straw. 

She stared at the glowing Ash-Fungus in her hand, her breath hissing through clenched teeth as the math clicked into place. The economy here wasn't just rigged; it was actively predatory. It was designed to bankrupt new arrivals before they even understood the rules. If simply picking a mushroom cost eighty-five percent of her stamina, she would literally exhaust herself to death before she could secure enough resources to make it through the night. 

Winded and cautious, Rhea realized the true cost of survival in this system.

Rhea leaned heavily against the petrified bark, her lungs burning as she sucked in the cold, drafty air. She was completely winded. In her left hand, she gripped the single Ash-Fungus she had just pulled from the tree. It pulsed with a faint, mocking bioluminescence. 

Eighty-five stamina. The System had charged her an eighty-five stamina Gathering Tax just to pick a damn mushroom. 

[Stamina: 15/100]

The physical backlash of the tax felt like she had just sprinted up six flights of stairs while breathing through a wet towel. Her bruised left leg throbbed in time with her racing heart. She adjusted her grip on the Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon in her right hand, using its heavy weight as a crude walking stick to keep herself upright. 

The temperature in the Ash-Choked Forest was dropping. If she was going to survive the night, she needed more than a glowing mushroom. She needed basic firewood. 

A few yards away, a scatter of loose, gray branches lay half-buried in the powdery ground. They weren't attached to anything. They were just debris. Rhea dragged her boots through the ash, reaching down with two aching fingers to pinch the end of a small, dead twig. 

[Gathering Tax Assessed: Minor Debris. Stamina -5.]
[Stamina: 10/100]

A fresh wave of exhaustion hit her knees like a physical blow. She stumbled, barely catching herself with the bludgeon. 

"You have got to be kidding me," Rhea rasped, staring at the twig. "It was on the ground. It’s literal garbage."

The System did not care. The System was a ledger, and the ledger demanded its cut. 

Desperate, she reached for a slightly thicker branch, hoping the yield would justify the cost. Her fingers brushed the coarse wood.

[Gathering Tax Assessed: Moderate Debris. Stamina -10.]
[Stamina: 0/100]
[Status Effect: Exhaustion applied.]

Her vision swam. The edges of the pale forest blurred into a sickening, bruised purple. The interface flickered wildly in the center of her vision, expanding from a standard notification into a blaring, crimson alert.

[Insufficient Stamina for Transaction.]
[Stamina Deficit will be drawn from HP pool at a 1:1 ratio.]
[Warning: Current HP is 1/100. Deficit deduction will result in fatal Indenture Default.]

Rhea froze. The math was perfectly, horribly clear. If she picked up the stick, the System would charge her life force. With exactly one hit point to her name, the cost of picking up a piece of firewood was death. The rigged economy wasn't just designed to keep her in debt; it was designed to bankrupt her out of existence before nightfall.

Slowly, agonizingly, she let go of the branch. She let her arm drop to her side. 

Rhea slumped against a jagged boulder by the dry riverbed and slid down until she hit the ash. She sat perfectly motionless, clutching her single mushroom, refusing to interact with the environment, refusing to trigger the fatal taxes that governed every inch of this miserable world.

## Physics vs. The System

Sitting motionless against a jagged boulder to avoid fatal stamina drain, Rhea watched an ash-crusted scavenger mob interact with the petrified environment. 

She didn't dare blink too hard. Blinking felt like it might cost a fraction of a stamina point, and she had exactly zero left to spend.

[Stamina: 0/100]
[Status: Exhaustion. Warning: Further physical exertion will incur a direct HP penalty.]
[HP: 1/100]

One hit point. It wasn't a metaphor. At one hit point, her pulse didn't thud; it vibrated, a thin, frantic buzzing warning her that her structural integrity was failing. Her bruised left leg throbbed with a dull ache. 

In her left hand, her fingers loosely cradled the glowing Ash-Fungus. She had paid eighty-five stamina to pluck it, only for the System to offer a measly five stamina in return—assuming it didn't poison her. It was a payday loan payable in life force. In her right hand, her fingers rested limply against the rough grip of her Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon. If she tried to lift it right now, the exertion would overdraft into her health pool, and she would die.

So, she breathed the sulfur-tinged air in shallow sips and watched the local wildlife.

The scavenger, roughly the size of a badger but covered in a brittle crust of hardened ash, was currently engaged in a violent disagreement with a cluster of pale roots. It dug furiously at the base of a towering, petrified ash tree. 

Frustrated by a stubborn root, the creature reared back and slammed its front claws into the trunk. The impact echoed with a dull thud. High above, something cracked. A thick, gray branch snapped loose, plummeted downward, and slammed directly onto the scavenger’s armored back. The creature let out a startled yelp, scurried away, and resumed foraging in the dry riverbed.

The branch lay in the dirt. 

Rhea waited for the glowing blue box. When she had touched a branch earlier, the System had immediately flagged her with a Gathering Tax. But the scavenger had just knocked a branch loose, and there was no flashing red text. 

Her dark brown eyes narrowed. The System wasn't omnipresent magic; it was a digital ledger plastered on top of a physical reality. The scavenger wasn't a player, and it hadn't "gathered" the branch. It had hit a physical object, and gravity had done the rest. The System didn't tax kinetic energy. Once the branch was knocked loose by a physics collision, it was just loose geometry. 

A slow, sharp smile cracked the dried ash on Rhea’s face. She couldn't hit the trees herself without dying from exhaustion. But she didn't need to. She just needed the scavenger to hit the trees. 

She looked at the dry riverbed. The creature was digging near a steep embankment, directly below a precarious overhang of petrified roots and loose boulders. A natural deadfall trap. If she could startle it toward the overhang without officially attacking it, the physics engine would crush the mob. No combat tax. Clean, untaxed loot.

Gripping her heavy bludgeon just enough to use it as a walking cane, Rhea formulated a plan to exploit the physics engine, preparing to herd the mob.

Rhea carefully rose from her resting spot, fighting through Exhaustion to put her physics exploit into motion. A few yards away, an ash-crusted scavenger was still scraping at the dry riverbed, its brittle claws oblivious to her presence.

A glowing blue box flickered in her periphery, clinically indifferent to her suffering.

[Warning: Stamina Depleted. Further exertion will consume HP.]

She stared at the numbers.

[HP: 1/100]
[Stamina: 0/100]

She couldn't afford a single misstep. At one hit point, a stumble, a heavy cough, or a swung weapon was a fatal event. She waited, her lungs burning, leaning against a petrified root until the merciless System ledger finally ticked over.

[Stamina: 1/100]

With agonizing slowness, she slipped the glowing Ash-Fungus into her left blazer pocket, freeing her hand. She crouched lower behind the thick root, her right hand gripping the rough, heavy handle of her Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon. The weapon was crude, heavy, and delightfully unregistered. 

She picked up a loose stone from the ash. The UI flashed an immediate update.

[Stamina: 0/100]

Rhea froze, her heart hammering against her ribs. She didn't throw it. She couldn't. She held her breath, waiting for the invisible timer to cycle again.

[Stamina: 1/100]

She hurled the stone. It clattered against the jagged rocks near the tree line. The scavenger hissed, its brittle body pivoting toward the sound. It scurried away from the riverbed, moving directly beneath a cluster of glowing fungus and a massive, precariously balanced petrified branch.

Rhea crept forward. Every step was calculated to avoid the System's exertion threshold. She positioned herself next to the petrified tree's trunk. The scavenger chittered right under the deadfall.

She didn't have the stamina to swing the Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon. A full-force attack would trigger a combat exertion penalty, drain her last HP, and kill her instantly. Instead, she lifted the heavy masonry chunk just enough to let gravity do the work.

[Stamina: 0/100]
[Warning: Exertion threshold impending.]

She let go of the tension in her arm, guiding the bludgeon's heavy head and letting its sheer, unbranded mass fall forward like a pendulum. 

The bludgeon slammed into the brittle trunk. 

Physics took over. The petrified wood cracked. The vibration traveled upward, snapping the massive dead branch loose. It plummeted, striking the scavenger with a sickening crunch and pinning the creature to the ash. A cloud of gray dust plumed outward. Splintered wood and chunks of glowing fungus rained down around the crushed mob.

[Scavenger Defeated. +15 Experience Points.]

Rhea squeezed her eyes shut, waiting for the blue flash of a Gathering Tax. 

Nothing happened. 

The System didn't register the fallen fungus and wood as a harvested resource. Because she hadn't actively gathered them, they were just environmental debris, knocked loose by a physical collision.

Panting heavily, Rhea stood over the crushed scavenger, staring down at the pile of glowing fungus and splintered wood that the System registered as untaxed environmental debris.

## Establishing a Baseline

With the scavenger crushed and untaxed loot secured, Rhea turned her attention to the raw materials. The creature’s brittle carapace had shattered under the heavy deadfall, leaving behind splintered wood and several glowing, fruit-like nodes of fungus.

She slumped against a nearby processing rock in the Ash-Choked Forest, the physical toll of her situation screaming in every nerve. Her peripheral vision was dominated by a pulsing, obnoxious red border.

[HP: 1/100]

[Stamina: 0/100]

[Status Effect: Exhaustion (Active)]

Every breath she drew felt like inhaling powdered glass. She eyed the freshly dropped fungus from the dead scavenger, noting it was identical to the raw Ash-Fungus she already had safely stowed in her left blazer pocket. She reached out and pulled one of the scavenger's glowing nodes closer to the flat surface of the rock.

As she stared at the glowing node, a helpful blue box chimed into existence.

[Item Detected: Raw Ash-Fungus]

[Would you like to auto-refine this item into Minor Stamina Paste?]

[Refinement Fee: 5 Stamina. Notice: Insufficient Stamina detected. Overdraft protection active. Refinement Fee will be deducted from HP at a 200% penalty rate (10 HP).]

Rhea let out a dry, rattling laugh. The System wanted ten hit points to mash a mushroom. She only had one. Clicking 'Accept' would be instant suicide.

"Not today, you parasitic spreadsheet," she whispered.

She dismissed the prompt with a weak swipe of her hand. The blue box vanished. If the System treated environmental collisions as untaxed events, then manual labor done entirely outside the UI's automated shortcuts should theoretically bypass the refinement fee entirely. 

Rhea adjusted her grip on the Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon in her right hand. The weapon was crude and heavy, and lifting it took monumental effort. She lined up the heavy, flat end of the bludgeon over the scavenger's dropped fungus and let gravity do the work.

The heavy masonry slammed down onto the rubbery node, bursting it into a puddle of glowing, pale-blue paste. Spore-dust puffed into the air.

She waited for the inevitable blue box demanding a tax for unauthorized crafting. Nothing happened. The red border of her vision didn't flash. Her single hit point remained intact.

Keeping a firm grip on the Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon, she scooped up a handful of the glowing paste with her free hand and shoved the mashed fungus into her mouth. It tasted like battery acid mixed with chalk, but the System's response was glorious.

[Manual Processing Detected. Refinement fee bypassed.]

[Consumable Ingested: Mashed Ash-Fungus (Untaxed)]

[Stamina +40]

[HP +24]

[Status Effect: Exhaustion removed.]

A rush of cool energy flooded her veins. The heavy weight of the Exhaustion debuff evaporated as its icon shattered in her peripheral vision.

[HP: 25/100]

[Stamina: 40/100]

She let out a long exhale, wiping a smear of glowing spore-paste from her chin. She had beaten the rigged math twice in one day. Rhea's stats recover, leaving her ready to bank her surplus.

With her immediate survival secured, Rhea bundled the remaining untaxed materials into the crook of her left arm and located a nearby automated System deposit node. The machine stood at the edge of the dry riverbed, half-buried in a drift of pale gray ash. It looked less like a mystical artifact and more like a rusted ATM that had been violently fused with an industrial trash compactor.

Her left leg throbbed with every step, the deep bruising a constant, dull ache that served as a reminder of her initial fall. But her breathing had finally leveled out. Her Stamina was holding steady at forty. She kept her Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon gripped tightly in her right hand, her knuckles white against the crude, heavy stone handle. The petrified forest around her was dead and quiet, save for the howling sulfur wind, but she wasn't about to trust the silence. The System had already proven it wanted her dead—or worse, endlessly working.

She limped the last few yards to the node. As she stepped within arm's reach, a rectangular slot on the obelisk's rusted face slid open with a screech of un-oiled metal, revealing a glowing blue hopper. The smell of ozone and burning copper wafted from the chute. Without hesitation, she dumped the splintered petrified wood and the cracked scavenger carapace into the grinding maw.

Instantly, a holographic screen snapped into existence, projecting a crisp, bureaucratic blue light into the gloom.

[Deposit Interface: Active]
[Contents Detected: Unregistered Biomass / Petrified Wood]
[Default Classification: Harvested Resources]
[Processing Tax: 5 Stamina]
[Yield: 4 Copper Credits]
[Notice: 100% of Yield auto-garnished toward Soul Debt]
[Accept? Y/N]

Rhea stared at the floating text, her jaw tightening. A bitter laugh scraped its way out of her throat. Five Stamina just to hand over the trash she’d tricked the environment into dropping. At her current capacity, that was over ten percent of her available physical energy. And the payout wouldn't even reach her pocket—it would vanish straight into the gaping, bottomless maw of her hundred-thousand-point deficit.

"Absolutely not," she muttered, jabbing her dirt-caked finger against the glowing red 'N'.

The machine let out a harsh, digitized buzz of disapproval. The hopper locked shut with a heavy metallic clunk, holding her items hostage.

Rhea leaned closer, her dark eyes scanning the glowing blue parchment. The System was a rigid bureaucracy. That was the one truth she had cemented since waking up in this ash-covered hellhole. Bureaucracies didn't cheat; they just hid the rules behind fine print, default settings, and artificial friction. They relied on exhaustion. They banked on the fact that a bleeding, starving arrival would just hit 'Accept' to make the prompt go away.

She ignored the flashing 'Accept' prompt that reappeared and tapped the text that read 'Default Classification'. Nothing happened. She swiped at it. Still nothing. Finally, tracing the edge of the interface frame, she spotted a microscopic, translucent gear icon tucked into the bottom right corner of the [Deposit Interface].

She pressed it.

The window expanded with a soft chime, unrolling a dense, scrolling list of alternative tax brackets and deposit categories. Most of them were worse. 'Hunted Spoils' carried a ten-point Stamina tax. 'Mined Ore' demanded fifteen. 'Salvaged Artifacts' required a flat HP deduction. The list went on, a litany of extortion designed to keep the indentured forever in the red.

She kept scrolling, her eyes tracking the tiny text as her contract_analysis skill hummed at the back of her mind, highlighting the subtle shifts in legal phrasing. Right at the very bottom, buried beneath a dozen obscure sub-clauses, was a category labeled 'Environmental Debris Clearance'.

She tapped it. The screen flickered, recalibrating the math.

[Transaction Log: Reclassified as 'Environmental Debris Clearance'.]
[Warning: This classification is reserved for civic maintenance. No gathering XP will be awarded.]
[Processing Tax: 0 Stamina]
[Yield: 4 Copper Credits]
[Notice: Civic maintenance stipends are exempt from standard Soul Debt garnishment.]
[Accept? Y/N]

Rhea grinned, a sharp, genuine expression that reached her dark brown eyes. She slammed her palm against the 'Y'.

The machine shuddered. Inside the obelisk, heavy gears ground the scavenger carapace and petrified wood into dust with a violent, satisfying crunch. A plume of pale exhaust vented from the top of the node. A moment later, a small drawer popped open at the base.

Inside sat four dull, rectangular metal coins. They didn't glow, and they weren't magical, but to Rhea, they were the most beautiful things she had seen all day.

She scooped them up, feeling their cold, heavy reality against her skin. A final notification chimed in her peripheral vision.

[Soul Debt: 100,000]
[Current Balance: 4 Copper Credits]

The debt was still a crushing, impossible number. But for the first time, she had capital that the System couldn't touch. She slipped the Copper Credits into her right blazer pocket, letting them clink softly against her stowed Cracked Smartphone.

Adjusting her grip on her Makeshift Masonry Bludgeon, Rhea successfully banked her first copper credits without incurring the standard gatherer's debt.
