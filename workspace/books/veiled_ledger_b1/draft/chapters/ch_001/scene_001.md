The steering column of a 2018 sedan was not designed to occupy the same physical space as a human ribcage. 

That was the last coherent, terrestrial thought Rhea managed before the physics of a head-on car crash rendered her biology obsolete. There was the deafening crunch of metal, the explosive deployment of an airbag a fraction of a second too late, and the absolute certainty that her ledger was zeroing out. 

And then, the steering column was gone. 

Rhea transitioned instantly from the crushing pressure of the wreck into a rushing, mid-air freefall. Wind screamed past her ears with the force of a physical blow, tearing the breath from her lungs. Her ruined business casual attire—a charcoal blazer and slacks that had been perfectly pressed thirty minutes ago—whipped around her slender frame like a shredded parachute. She snapped her dark brown eyes open, fighting against the blinding pressure of the air, and found herself plummeting through a bruised, violently purple sky. 

Thick, gray ash pelted her pale skin and tangled in her messy bob. Below her stretched an endless, jagged wasteland of shattered black glass and smoldering dunes. It looked less like a geographical formation and more like the bottom of a colossal, neglected furnace. 

Physics was usually a strict, unimaginative accountant. Terminal velocity for a human was roughly one hundred and twenty miles per hour. The jagged obsidian spikes rushing up to meet her offered zero structural give. The math was incredibly straightforward. 

She braced herself, waiting for the obliterating impact.

It came as a concussive boom of breaking stone and snapping bone. Rhea cratered into the side of a steep dune, colliding with a buried shelf of obsidian. By all known laws of biology, she should have been reduced to a thin, red smear against the rock. 

Instead, the universe paused, checked a hidden ledger, and applied a rounding error.

A sharp, synthetic chime rang cleanly through the ringing in her ears. It was the pleasant, utterly detached sound of a corporate elevator arriving at the executive floor. 

[Status Effect: Tutorial Grace Period activated.]
[Fatal impact damage negated. HP locked at 1.]

Rhea lay at the bottom of the smoking crater, her chest heaving. The math of surviving on exactly one hit point was a nightmare of biological suspension. It meant her body was broken, hovering at the absolute microscopic edge of death, but explicitly forbidden from crossing over. Her lungs burned. Her ribs felt like ground glass. 

She tried to sit up, and a lance of white-hot agony shot up her left leg. A massive slab of jagged debris, roughly the size and weight of an engine block, rested heavily over her shin. She was completely, immovably trapped.

[Status Effect: Pinned applied. Immobilized.]

Another polite notification of her absolute helplessness.

Rhea let her head fall back against the ash. "Okay," she rasped, her voice sounding like dry leaves. "Think. Inventory."

It was a ridiculous, desperate thought, a byproduct of too many late-night gaming sessions. But the floating text had established the rules. If there was a System, there was an inventory. She patted her side with a trembling hand. Her slacks were torn, but she slipped her fingers into her right blazer pocket. They brushed against smooth, shattered glass. 

She pulled it out. Her Cracked Smartphone. 

The screen was a spiderweb of fractures, the casing bent. It was completely dead, unresponsive to her thumb. But it had made the transition. The tactile sensation of the broken glass was her only anchor to the world she had just violently departed. She slipped the Cracked Smartphone back into the blazer pocket, ensuring it was securely stowed. 

Before she could attempt to shift the rock on her leg again, the air in front of her face shimmered. 

A glowing blue system notification manifested. It was bordered in a pulsing, angry red, anchoring itself directly to her retinas so she couldn't look away. It obscured the ash and the sky, demanding her absolute attention.

[Notice of Arrival: Frontier Node 74-A]
[Subject: Rhea]
[Title Acquired: Indentured Soul]

Rhea blinked, her analytical mind snagging on the terminology. *Indentured.* It wasn't a heroic title. It was a liability. The text scrolled upward.

[HP: 1/100]
[MP: 10/10]
[Stamina: 5/50]
[Assessment Complete. Subject possesses insufficient capital to cover the metaphysical costs of resurrection and transit.]
[Soul Debt Assessed: 100,000 Credits.]

The number hung there, stark and uncompromising. One hundred thousand. Rhea didn't know the exchange rate of this nightmare wasteland, but she knew predatory lending when she saw it. The System wasn't a benevolent god granting a second chance. It was a creditor handing her the bill for her own survival.

The bottom of the notification coalesced into a blinking command.

[Acknowledge indenture status to commence mandatory labor cycle. Y / N]
[Warning: Selecting 'N' or failing to acknowledge within the grace period will result in immediate asset liquidation. (Current Asset Value: 1 HP).]

Rhea stared at the prompt. The claustrophobia was absolute. She was physically pinned under hundreds of pounds of obsidian, held together by a single hit point. But far worse, she was legally pinned by a terms of service agreement that held a gun to her head.
