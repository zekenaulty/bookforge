Rhea paused her trembling finger an inch before hitting the glowing blue accept button, her dark brown eyes catching the microscopic asterisk pulsing at the bottom corner of the holographic System prompt. The wind howled across the obsidian ridge, kicking sulfur-laced ash against her bruised left leg, but her focus remained entirely locked on that single, treacherous pixel of punctuation. 

She knew better than to sign anything a glowing rock offered her without reading the fine print. 

With a sharp tap, she pressed the asterisk instead of the main rune. The interface didn't just expand; it unrolled like a digital carpet bombing. Paragraphs of dense, microscopic legalese stacked to the sky, scrolling faster than a human eye could track. 

[Skill Triggered: Contract Analysis Lv.1]
[Highlighting Predatory Clauses...]

Lines of text suddenly flared a sickly, bruised purple. Rhea squinted at the illuminated sections. The 'Starter Cache' wasn't a gift. It was a payday loan from hell. The meager rations, stale water, and poor flint dagger came with a 400% compounding daily interest rate. And because the local economy apparently didn't run on fiat currency, the fine print clearly stated the interest was payable in maximum HP and forced indentured labor.

[Current HP: 1/100]
[Current Soul Debt: 100,000]

"You've got to be kidding me," Rhea muttered, her voice raspy from the dry air. "You want my actual lifeblood for a stale cracker and a rock tied to a stick?"

The System, emotionally dead and perfectly bureaucratic, offered no counter-argument. It just sat there, waiting for her to default on her own existence.

She reached out, swiped aggressively past the mountain of debt obligations, and found the tiny, deliberately obfuscated 'Decline' option buried at the very bottom of the holographic parchment. She jammed her finger into it.

[Offer Declined. Starter Cache Locked.]

The blue light vanished instantly. The stone pedestal went dead, plunging her back into the howling, ash-choked gloom of the Crater Ridge. She was still starving, still sitting at exactly one hit point, and her Cracked Smartphone was still uselessly stowed in her right blazer pocket. But as she stared at the darkened stone, a grim sliver of satisfaction cut through the panic. At least she didn't owe the universe her spleen.
