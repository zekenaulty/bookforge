# Chapter 5: Auditing Reality

## Filing the Grievance

With the data packet secured as a liability, Rhea turned her attention to the terminal to draft a formal grievance. The pulsing, crystalline drive was safely locked away in her personal system inventory, immune to the automated sweeps, but the immediate danger hadn't passed. The damp, humming chamber of the Maintenance Trenches felt less like a mechanical sub-basement and more like a tomb waiting for its occupants to stop struggling.

Vance Harrow let out a ragged, wet cough from the stone floor. He was curled onto his side, his heavily blistered hands trembling as he held them awkwardly away from his body. The burn damage from forcing the terminal's heavy brass cooling vents open had nearly cooked him alive. The smell of singed flesh and ozone hung thick in the stagnant air. He had survived a dozen brutal encounters since arriving in the frontier zone, relying on his thick skin and a heavy piece of rebar, but this was different. You couldn't bludgeon a systemic burn. You couldn't intimidate a digital ledger. 

He looked up at Rhea, watching her lean against the terminal, her face illuminated by the harsh light of the screen. She looked like a ghost, her singed hair plastered to her forehead with sweat, her clothes covered in ash, but there was a terrifying, cold focus in her eyes.

[Party Member: Vance Harrow | HP: 3/100 (Critical)]
[Status: Severe Burns applied. Natural regeneration halted.]

"I can't... I can't feel my fingers," Vance wheezed, his scarred face pale in the dim amber light. He tried to shift his weight, but his boots slipped on the slick stone, sending a spasm of agony through his frame.

"Good," Rhea said, her voice tight but entirely devoid of sympathy. "Pain means you're bleeding HP. Numbness means the system isn't registering further decay. Just stay down, Vance. Don't move. Don't do anything that costs stamina. You're hovering over the void right now."

Rhea herself was barely standing. The edges of her vision throbbed with a persistent, flashing red vignette that synced perfectly with her racing heartbeat. Every breath she drew felt like inhaling ground glass. The physical toll of the frontier zone's rigged economy was catching up to her. 

[Resource Warning: Stamina at 2/100.]
[Debuff: Exhaustion (Severe). Movement speed reduced by 80%. Cognitive processing taxed.]

She leaned her hip heavily against the massive brass server terminal to keep her knees from buckling. She needed to file the grievance before the local overseer realized the memory wipe had failed and sent something much worse than a brass security construct to clean up the mess. Reaching into her right blazer pocket, her fingers brushed past her cracked smartphone and the loose copper credits before closing around her rolled-up Scrap Parchment and the nub of her Charcoal Stick. 

She pulled them out, unrolling the parchment with her left hand while gripping the charcoal in her right. Her eyes scanned the crude ledger she had compiled earlier in the Ash Thickets. The undeniable deficit—exactly 14.3 percent missing from every quest reward, every loot drop, every stamina transaction—was staring back at her in stark black lines. In her old life, she had hunted corporate embezzlers who used shell companies, phantom employees, and fake vendor invoices to hide their skim. She knew the rhythm of a fraud. It always started small—a rounding error here, a misclassified expense there—until the greed outpaced the caution. The thrill of the paper trail was the only thing she missed about Earth. The moment when the numbers stopped lying and the truth crystallized into an undeniable, prosecutable fact. 

Here, in this absurd, magical purgatory, the local administrator was using the fundamental laws of physics and system mechanics to do the exact same thing. But the arrogance was identical. The overseer thought the arrivals were too stupid, too desperate, or too dead to notice the math. 

She shifted her gaze to the mechanical construct hovering near the narrow tunnel exit. Unit 734 was a wreck. Plumes of thick, acrid smoke vented continuously from its metallic chassis. The drone's internal cooling fans whined in a desperate, failing pitch, sounding like a dying animal trapped in a tin can. 

[Unit 734 | Efficiency: 12% | Status: Smoking Chassis]

"Drone," Rhea commanded, her voice cracking in the dry air. "I need the exact bureaucratic syntax for a metaphysical grievance. Direct routing to the Ledger's core audit sub-routine."

Unit 734’s optical lens flickered a sickly, dim green, stuttering as it tried to focus on her face. "Processing request... Error. Unit efficiency critically low. Attempting to access core administrative routing logic requires Level 4 clearance. You do not possess Level 4 clearance. Furthermore, filing a grievance against a local overseer without proper formatting will result in immediate retroactive taxation, asset forfeiture, and potential soul-binding to the nearest labor camp."

"We're already soul-bound to the liability," Rhea snapped, waving the Charcoal Stick at the hovering machine. "The system recognized the Mutual Assured Destruction clause in our custom party contract. That means we have standing as aggrieved stakeholders. We aren't just random arrivals anymore; we're a registered entity holding toxic debt. Pull up the syntax, or I'll use my authority as Party Leader to designate you as unsalvageable scrap and leave you down here."

"Warning," the drone countered weakly, another plume of smoke escaping its vents. "Abandonment of party assets is a violation of—"

"I wrote the contract," Rhea interrupted, her voice dropping to a dangerous hiss. "I know exactly what the clauses say. Section four, paragraph two: Party assets operating below fifteen percent efficiency can be liquidated for parts if their continued operation threatens the party's survival. You are at twelve percent. Do you want to test my interpretation of 'liquidation'?"

The drone’s internal gears ground together loudly, a shower of orange sparks spitting from a ruptured seam along its underbelly. "Compliance acknowledged. Bypassing standard clearance protocols via Stakeholder Loophole 44-J. Accessing System Syntax: Form 88-B."

A rigid, perfectly square projection of glowing green text snapped into existence, hovering in the damp air between the drone and the brass terminal. The formatting was dense, clinical, and completely devoid of plain language. It was a masterpiece of hostile architecture disguised as paperwork.

[System Syntax: Form 88-B (Metaphysical Grievance)]
[Requisite Fields: Offending Node ID, Ledger Discrepancy Percentage, Custodial Proof of Malfeasance, Claimant Signature.]
[Formatting Directive: Standard Bureaucratic Stricture. All inputs must utilize approved Ledger terminology. Deviations, emotional appeals, or colloquialisms will result in automatic rejection and a 500-credit processing fine.]

Rhea stared at the block of text, a grim smile touching the corners of her mouth. "Of course they charge you for filling out the complaint wrong. It's a closed-loop grift. Make the forms impossible to understand, then penalize the victim for failing to understand them."

"Rhea," Vance groaned, his voice barely a whisper over the hum of the servers. "Are you sure about this? If you trigger a core audit, they might just wipe the whole node. Us included. We're standing in the middle of the crime scene."

"They're already skimming us to death, Vance," Rhea said, not taking her eyes off the terminal. "If we play by their rules, we die of exhaustion trying to afford basic health potions. If we break the rules, we get liquidated by security constructs. The only way out is to use their own bureaucratic nightmare against them. We force the Ledger core to recognize that the local overseer is stealing from them, not just us. The System doesn't care if we suffer, but it absolutely cares if its bottom line is being fucked with."

She turned back to the massive brass terminal. The interface was archaic, a series of heavy, physical typewriter-style keys beneath a glowing input screen. She held the Scrap Parchment up, cross-referencing her charcoal math with the projected syntax. 

"Unit 734, what is the exact administrative classification for the stolen experience points?"

"Stolen is an emotional colloquialism," the drone corrected, venting another puff of black smoke that smelled strongly of burning rubber. "The approved terminology is 'Undocumented Node Maintenance Allocation.'"

"Right. And the loot deficit?"

"Unsanctioned Material Diversion."

Rhea nodded slowly, her charcoal-stained fingers hovering over the heavy brass keys. She began to type, her movements slow and deliberate. With only two points of stamina left, a typo could literally cost her a heartbeat. The heavy keys resisted her, as if the machine itself knew it was being weaponized. The resistance wasn't just mechanical; it felt almost magical, a localized dampening field designed to discourage unauthorized access. Each keystroke required her to lean her body weight into her fingers.

[Input Field 1: Offending Node ID]

Rhea typed the alphanumeric string she had pulled from the terminal's routing logs earlier. 

[Input Field 2: Ledger Discrepancy Percentage]

She keyed in 14.3%. She paused, staring at the number. In her experience, automated systems preferred excessive precision. A flat percentage looked like an estimate. It gave the defense room to argue rounding errors. She needed to make it look like an exact extraction from a raw data feed. She added the trailing decimals. 14.300%.

[Input Field 3: Custodial Proof of Malfeasance]

This was the tricky part. She couldn't just upload the Raw Data Packet; it was locked in her unalterable system inventory as a liability to prevent the local purge protocol from deleting it. If she tried to attach it directly, the local firewall might intercept and corrupt the file before it reached the core.

"Drone," Rhea said, pausing to wipe a bead of sweat from her forehead. "How do I cite a Soul-Bound Liability as evidence without transferring physical custody of the data?"

Unit 734 clicked rapidly, its optical lens whirring as it processed the query. "You must invoke the Evidentiary Sequestration clause. Syntax requirement: 'Reference Exhibit A, currently held under Mutual Assured Destruction lock, verifiable via systemic ledger footprint.'"

Rhea smiled grimly. "Perfect. They can check the math without ever touching the actual file." She typed the exact phrase into the terminal, her fingers aching with the effort. 

[Input Field 4: Claimant Signature]

She typed her name, followed by her newly acquired designation: Rhea Mercer, Party Leader, The Mercy Cache (Provisional).

The machine hummed loudly, the input screen flashing a pale, bureaucratic blue. 

[Validating Syntax...]

The seconds ticked by with agonizing slowness. The silence in the damp chamber was absolute, save for Vance's ragged breathing and the dying whine of the drone's cooling fans. Rhea felt a drop of sweat trace a line down her spine. If the system rejected the formatting, they would be hit with a fine they couldn't pay, triggering an immediate default. In the Ledger, default meant death, or worse, indentured servitude until the debt was paid off at a fraction of a copper per century.

The blue light shifted to a stark, unyielding white. The sound of a heavy, metallic stamp echoed from the terminal's internal speakers.

[Syntax Verified. Formatting Directive: Satisfied.]
[Grievance Form 88-B Compiled. Awaiting Final Execution.]

A glowing execution rune appeared on the terminal's main display. It pulsed with a steady, indifferent rhythm, a digital guillotine waiting for the lever to be pulled. The heavy iron blast doors of the Maintenance Trenches remained sealed, the warning klaxons silent. For now, the local overseer was blind to the trap she had just built inside his own system.

Rhea finalized the syntax, her finger hovering over the execution command.

The terminal swallowed the input. For one agonizing second, the damp, humming chamber of the Maintenance Trenches went completely silent.

"Tell me you didn't just poke the bear," Vance said from the floor, clutching his blistered hands to his chest.

"I didn't poke it," Rhea said, shifting the Charcoal Stick to the side of her mouth like a cigar, her bare forearms still showing the noticeably singed arm hair from the terminal's heat. "I just filed a lien against its cave."

Unit 734’s smoking chassis shuddered. A whine built deep within its metallic core. "Query: Have you considered the statistical probability of administrative retaliation? Addendum: We are all going to die."

Before Rhea could point out the ironclad legal nature of the Evidentiary Sequestration clause, the terminal erupted.

[System Notification: Metaphysical Grievance Form 88-B Received.]
[Cross-referencing Local Ledger...]
[Discrepancy Identified: Undocumented Maintenance Allocation (14.3%).]
[Initiating Automated Asset Freeze.]

The dull amber lights illuminating the damp stone overhead snapped off. A heartbeat later, the trenches were bathed in a strobing, violent crimson. Deafening klaxons began to shriek, a rhythmic, bone-rattling pulse that vibrated right through the soles of Rhea's boots.

Heavy iron blast doors groaned at the far end of the tunnel, grinding shut with a finality that made the air pressure drop.

[Warning: Local Node Assets Frozen.]
[Audit State: ACTIVE.]
[System Audit Flag: TRIGGERED.]
[System Overlay Integrity: 88%.]

"That sounds bad!" Vance yelled over the klaxons, scrambling to his feet.

"It's not bad!" Rhea shouted back, though her grip on the Scrap Parchment tightened significantly. "It's a mandatory administrative pause! It means we caught them!"

The terminal locked down completely, its interface freezing into a solid block of red warning text as the system initiated an automated freeze, plunging the trenches into an active audit state.

## The Violent Refund

Heavy iron blast doors groaned, grinding shut across the primary corridors as deafening warning klaxons bathed the damp stone in a violent, rhythmic red light. The local node was purging its active zones, trying to isolate the metaphysical grievance Rhea had just injected into its core.

Rhea shoved the Scrap Parchment and the Charcoal Stick deep into her right blazer pocket. She needed both hands free, and she needed them now. The singed hair on her arms prickled as the ambient temperature spiked, the brass terminals around them venting excess heat to compensate for the system panic.

[Stamina: 2/100]

Her lungs burned with every breath. Moving felt like dragging her body through wet concrete. She was running on fumes and sheer stubbornness.

Beside her, Vance didn't look much better. The man was a walking corpse.

[HP: 3/100]

His heavy boots slapped against the stone floor. He ripped the rusted Rebar from his belt loop, his blistered hands gripping the makeshift weapon with desperate strength. A secondary security gate rattled down from the ceiling, threatening to block their path to the surface. Vance didn't slow down. He threw his shoulder into the descending iron, jamming the Rebar into the track and throwing his weight against it. Metal shrieked, gears snapped, and the gate buckled just enough for them to squeeze through.

[System Warning: Localized Deletion Sweep Imminent.]

A grid of searing red light materialized on the floor behind them, sweeping forward like a physical wave. Unit 734 darted ahead, thick plumes of exhaust venting from its smoking chassis as it calculated the structural weak points in the collapsing architecture.

"Keep moving!" Vance grunted, yanking his Rebar free and stumbling forward.

They scrambled toward the steep incline leading to the surface, the deafening crush of the facility echoing below them. The party navigates the collapsing access shaft, staying one step ahead of the automated quarantine bulkheads.

There was no majestic chime, no gentle shower of golden light. The local node had been forced into an immediate, catastrophic payout by Rhea’s grievance, and the System did not possess the bandwidth to make it pretty. The atmosphere above the ash-choked plains tore open with a sound like ripping canvas, revealing a bruised, pixelated void.

[System Notification: Audit Resolution Initiated. Disbursing 14.3% Withheld Value.]

Then, the math fell out of the sky.

Experience points didn't manifest as abstract concepts; they materialized as dense, jagged shards of crystallized amethyst data, dropping at terminal velocity. They hit the ground like artillery shells, cratering the gray dirt and throwing up plumes of ash. Mixed within the lethal hail of XP was the raw physical loot: heavy iron ingots, bundled scavenger carapaces, and loose copper coins that tore through the air like buckshot.

"Cover!" Vance roared. He planted his boots in the dirt and swung his heavy iron Rebar, his blistered hands gripping the makeshift weapon like a baseball bat.

A jagged shard of crystallized experience points roughly the size of a cinderblock plummeted toward Rhea. Vance intercepted it, bringing the Rebar up in a desperate parry. The shard shattered against the iron, exploding into a blinding spray of blue light and kinetic force that knocked Vance backward into the scattered dirt.

[HP Warning: Vance Harrow - HP: 2/100. Structural Failure Imminent.]

Vance groaned, spitting ash as he struggled to rise. Behind him, Unit 734’s smoking chassis sparked violently as a rain of untaxed copper coins pinged off its metallic casing, driving its efficiency rating deeper into the red.

Rhea dropped to her knees, her lungs burning. She tried to scramble toward the meager shelter of a rusted kiosk, but her legs refused to cooperate.

[Stamina Warning: Rhea Mercer - Stamina: 2/100. Motor Functions Impaired.]

Another wave of heavy, unrefined loot materialized twenty feet above them—stacks of petrified wood and raw scavenger meat. The sheer volume of the back-paid rewards was turning the Outpost Surface into a meat grinder. The System was violently correcting its ledger, and the sheer physical mass and velocity of the back-paid rewards threatened to crush the party, who were already at critical health and stamina.

The air grew thick with the static charge of unallocated power. The physical weight and concussive force of the XP and loot began to crush them, demanding immediate allocation to survive.

The sky above the Outpost Surface was glitching into a violent purple, tearing at the seams as the 14.3% withheld value rained down in a localized, apocalyptic payout.

A jagged block of crystallized experience slammed into the dirt inches from Rhea’s knee. The sheer kinetic force of the audit was deafening. Her Stamina was critically low, sitting at a miserable 2/100, and every breath felt like inhaling ground glass. Beside her, Vance was in worse shape. He had just tried to block a falling XP shard with his Rebar, and the concussive damage had dropped his HP to a terrifying 2/100.

"If a copper piece hits me in the head, I'm dead!" Vance roared over the din, clutching his blistered hands around his weapon.

[System Notification: Administrative Refund Processing.]
[Warning: Unallocated Value Exceeds Physical Capacity. Crushing Damage Imminent.]

"It's a bureaucratic avalanche!" Rhea shouted, swiping frantically at the blue boxes materializing in front of her face. "The System doesn't have a queue for this much back-pay! It's just dropping it on our heads!"

[Experience Threshold Met. Level Up x3.]
[+15 Free Attribute Points available.]

A heavy iron ingot materialized out of thin air, dropping directly onto Rhea's shoulder. She gasped, her knees buckling under the sudden mass. Her singed arm hair stood on end as the static electricity of raw, unprocessed data washed over them.

"Allocate your stats!" Rhea screamed, pulling up her character sheet. "Dump it all into Strength and Vitality! We need the encumbrance limit!"

"I'm a finesse build!" Vance protested, dodging a shower of untaxed copper credits that hit the ground like buckshot.

"You're a dead build if you don't!" Rhea snapped.

She didn't hesitate. Survival was just an arithmetic problem, and right now, the math demanded mass. She grabbed the slider for Strength and shoved it forward.

[Vitality: 10 -> 15]
[Encumbrance Limit Increased: 150kg -> 350kg]

The change was instantaneous. The crushing weight on her shoulders lessened, her muscle fibers hardening just enough to push back against the physical pressure of the loot. She gasped in a breath, her Stamina ticking up slightly to 5/150 as her maximum threshold expanded, though she still hovered dangerously near empty.

[Inventory Capacity Exceeded. Auto-Stow Failed.]

"Inventory space!" Rhea yelled, watching a pile of glowing pelts and heavy iron ingots threaten to bury Vance. "It's trying to physically manifest what we can't carry!"

Vance cursed, his fingers flying across his own interface. He glowed faintly as his level-up processed, his HP ticking up to 15/150—just enough to pull him out of the critical red zone. "I'm expanding my slots! It costs a hundred copper!"

"Loot the ground first!" Rhea ordered. She scooped up a handful of the untaxed copper credits that had just pelted her, her balance skyrocketing past the fee threshold. She rapidly purchased three new rows of system inventory and furiously tapped 'Loot All' on the immediate vicinity.

[Inventory Slot 5: Heavy Iron Ingot x4]
[Inventory Slot 6: Crystallized Experience x12]

The physical pile around them vanished into the digital ether, giving them exactly one square meter of breathing room. Rhea and Vance managed to allocate just enough stats and inventory space to keep from being crushed, though the storm still raged around them, the violent purple sky raining endless bureaucratic vengeance.

The violent storm of physical XP and loot finally subsides, leaving the party in a crater of their own refunded wealth. The sky above the Outpost Surface faded from a glitching, hostile purple back to a dull, indifferent gray. 

Rhea Mercer lay on her back in the dirt, staring up at the empty sky. Her chest heaved, the noticeably singed hair on both of her arms scraping against the ash. The violent physical nature of the XP refund had nearly killed them. If she hadn't executed an emergency stat allocation, dumping every available point into Strength and Vitality, the sheer mass of the back-pay would have crushed her ribcage.

She looked toward the administrative spire looming in the distance. The klaxons had stopped, but the silence felt infinitely more dangerous. The system knew exactly what they had done. Surrounded by their newly acquired wealth, the party braces for the inevitable retaliation from the overseer.

## The Overseer's Gaze

The dust from the violent refund storm barely settles before the system freezes again, this time from a manual override. 

It was not a metaphor. The ambient physics engine of the Outpost Surface crater simply stopped rendering momentum. A jagged chunk of Crystallized Experience that had been tumbling down the side of the ash bank halted in mid-air, defying gravity. The howling wind died instantly, leaving behind a vacuum of absolute, suffocating silence.

[System Override]

Rhea rubbed her bare forearms, the noticeably singed arm hair prickling as the environmental temperature plummeted. The Raw Data Packet sat safely stowed in her system inventory, a heavy, digital weight that had just bought them a fortune and a death sentence. Beside her, Vance tightened his grip on his heavy Rebar in his right hand. His heavily blistered hands protested the friction, knuckles white as he stared at the frozen sky. 

What did you do? Vance whispered, his voice sounding thin and compressed, as if the air itself was resisting the vibration of his vocal cords. 

I filed a grievance, Rhea said, her eyes darting across the static landscape. The ledger locked the node's assets. It is supposed to trigger an automated internal audit.

[Connection Forced]

Unit 734 suddenly convulsed. The drone had been hovering at waist height, plumes of thick smoke venting from its metallic body after the brutal sprint up the access shaft. Now, its smoking chassis jerked upward with a sickening snap of gears. Its internal gyros shrieked in protest as an external force bypassed its localized firewalls. 

The drone's optical lens flared, shifting from its standard dull amber to a blinding red beam that painted the ash-covered ground. 

[Active Audit Target]

The notification did not appear in Rhea's peripheral vision. It burned directly into the center of her retinas, a violent crimson warning that refused to be dismissed. The red beam projecting from Unit 734 expanded outward, tearing through the frozen dust motes and assembling a towering, holographic geometry. 

Code cascaded down the light, solidifying into the shape of a man draped in the heavy, angular robes of high administration. The projection was easily twenty feet tall, radiating an aura of pure, weaponized bureaucracy. The air pressure in the crater spiked, pressing down on Rhea's lungs like a physical weight. 

The massive, furious avatar of the overseer glares down from Unit 734's chassis, locking its focus entirely on Rhea.

The projection was a towering monument to administrative fury, a three-story construct of hard-light and weaponized authority that painted the ash-covered floor of the Outpost Surface Crater in a blinding, aggressive crimson.

The air itself felt heavy, thick with the static charge of a manual system override. The violent purple sky had snapped back to a dull gray, but the clouds weren't moving. The howling wind that constantly scoured the frontier had been muted, replaced by the oppressive, artificial silence of a paused simulation. A stray piece of shattered obsidian, kicked up during their desperate sprint from the maintenance trenches, hung suspended in mid-air, caught in the absolute stasis of the system freeze.

Rhea’s body was a ledger of recent trauma. Her lungs burned with the specific, agonizing fire of a depleted stamina bar. Five points out of one hundred and fifty. It was a joke. It meant she couldn't sprint, couldn't swing a weapon, couldn't even dodge effectively. Every movement felt like wading through wet cement. Her blazer was torn, her pockets heavy with the absurd, untaxed loot she had scavenged. But the real weight was in her system inventory. The Heavy Iron Ingots and Crystallized Experience from the refund storm were safely stowed, but the Raw Data Packet pulsed with a heavy, radioactive presence in her digital storage.

Beside her, Vance Harrow let out a ragged, wet cough. The man was a walking testament to the sheer stubbornness of human biology refusing to yield to digital arithmetic. He was surviving on fifteen hit points out of a maximum of one hundred and fifty. His face was pale beneath a layer of gray ash, and his breath hitched with every exhalation. He gripped the jagged length of Rebar in his right hand, the rusted metal pressing into the durable blisters that covered his palms. He had earned those burns holding open the superheated brass cooling vents of the server terminal, a physical sacrifice that had allowed Rhea to extract the data. He was a liability, mathematically speaking. But he was also the only reason she hadn't been crushed.

"What is that?" Vance rasped, his voice barely carrying over the low, thrumming hum of the projection beam. He didn't raise the Rebar, instinctively understanding that you couldn't bludgeon a hologram, but his body was coiled, ready to throw himself between Rhea and the towering red figure.

"That," Rhea said, her voice dry and surprisingly steady, "is the middle management we just audited."

[System Alert: Administrative Override Active.]
[Warning: Bypassing Standard Dialogue Protocols.]

The blue boxes flickered into existence, but they were immediately overwritten, their crisp borders bleeding into a hostile, jagged red. The System itself was struggling to process the intrusion.

"SUBJECT RHEA MERCER."

The voice did not come from the smoking, convulsing chassis of Unit 734. It bypassed the physical medium of sound entirely, vibrating directly against the bones of Rhea’s skull. It was a voice devoid of humanity, yet dripping with the specific, petty malice of a bureaucrat whose spreadsheet had just been ruined.

"You have initiated an unauthorized metaphysical grievance," the Overseer boomed. The avatar crossed its massive arms, glaring down with featureless, burning white eyes. "You have disrupted the primary routing logic of Sector Four. You have siphoned operational reserves meant for the stabilization of the frontier."

Rhea tilted her head, her mind automatically shifting out of survival mode and into the cold, clinical gears of a corporate fraud investigator. She knew this type of man. She had sat across from dozens of them in sterile, glass-walled conference rooms in her previous life. Men who hid millions in offshore shell companies, men who believed that their position in the hierarchy made them immune to the mathematics of consequence. The Overseer wasn't a god. He was a middle manager caught with his hand in the till, using the company's PA system to scream at the whistleblower.

"I didn't siphon anything," Rhea called back, projecting her voice toward the massive head. "I submitted a standard grievance through the core Ledger audit sub-routine. The System calculated the deficit—exactly fourteen point three percent per transaction—and issued a refund. If your node crashed because it had to pay out what it actually owed, then your ledger was insolvent to begin with."

The red light flared, a pulse of pure, digitized rage that made the singed hair on Rhea's bare forearms stand up.

"You are an Indenture," the Overseer stated, the pressure in their skulls intensifying. "You do not calculate. You do not audit. You generate value until your durability expires."

"I am a Party Leader," Rhea corrected, tapping the air to pull up her own interface. "Operating under a custom liability contract that your own System approved. And according to the terms of the freeze, your administrative assets are currently locked. You can't touch us."

She was gambling. If he could have deleted them, he would have done it already. The system freeze had tied his hands.

Vance shifted his weight, his boots grinding against the scattered copper credits in the dirt. "Rhea, don't piss it off."

"It's already pissed off, Vance," Rhea muttered. "It's trying to intimidate us into a default."

"I DO NOT REQUIRE INTIMIDATION," the Overseer’s voice echoed, shaking the loose ash from the crater walls. "I AM THE DESIGNATED AUTHORITY OF THIS FRONTIER. YOU ARE A GLITCH. A ROUNDING ERROR IN THE MARGINS OF A VAST MACHINE."

The avatar leaned forward, the sheer scale of the movement causing a phantom wind to sweep across the crater. A beam of concentrated, scanning light shot from the avatar’s eyes, washing over her.

[System Alert: Administrative Scan Detected.]
[Resisting... Failed.]

Rhea gritted her teeth as the scan penetrated her inventory. She felt a cold, invasive pressure as the Overseer tallied her assets. And then, the scan hit the absolute bottom of her systemic existence.

[Status Effect Detected: Soul-Bound Liability: Raw Data Packet.]

The red projection flickered violently. The Overseer recoiled, a gesture so profoundly human in its shock that it momentarily shattered the illusion of absolute control.

"You... bound it," the Overseer hissed, the booming volume dropping to a dangerous, localized whisper. "You bound the raw routing logs to your own soul architecture."

"Custom contract loophole," Rhea said, a tight, humorless smile touching her lips. "Any item designated as a shared liability cannot be deleted, transferred, or altered without the unanimous consent of the party. And since Unit 734 is currently hijacked by you, it can't vote. So the data stays right where it is."

Vance let out a low whistle, glancing at Rhea with a mixture of profound respect and absolute terror.

The Overseer remained silent for a long, terrible moment. The static charge in the air grew thicker. The system freeze was holding, preventing the Overseer from simply dropping a meteor on their heads, but the pressure of his presence was beginning to warp the environment.

"Clever," the Overseer finally said. The word was a curse. "You understand the syntax of the Ledger. You know how to hide behind the fine print. But you do not understand the scale of the board you are playing on."

The massive avatar straightened up, the red light shifting from a chaotic, glitching crimson to a deep, steady blood-red.

"The core System rules prevent me from directly terminating a party under an active audit freeze," the Overseer admitted, the bureaucratic exactness returning to his tone. "I cannot alter your hit points. I cannot delete your inventory. I cannot unbind the evidence you have stolen."

Rhea’s smile faded. The admission was too clean.
