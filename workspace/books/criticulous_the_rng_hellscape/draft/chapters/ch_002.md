# Chapter 2: The Glass Cannon’s Guide to Not Dying

## The Coward's Wardrobe

Artie, now safe from Vane behind the shimmering green barrier of the safe zone, began browsing the crowded market stalls with Fizz floating cheerfully by his shoulder. The transition from the low-resolution pixelated trees of the Tutorial Forest to the bustling, high-fidelity cobblestone streets of Oakhaven Market was jarring. The air here smelled heavily of roasted mutton, woodsmoke, and the sharp tang of hot iron. 

Artie leaned against a wooden post, his chest heaving, his pale skin slick with cold sweat. He ran a trembling hand through his messy brown hair, trying to slow his racing heart. His skinny-fat build was entirely unsuited for sprinting for his life, let alone dodging a sword-wielding maniac. 

"You did great!" Fizz chirped. The tiny system entity hovered at his eye level, her neon pink hair glowing softly in the shadows of the market awning. She did a little mid-air loop, her translucent skin leaving a faint trail of magenta light. "Evasion is a perfectly valid early-game strategy! Plus, you made it to the hub city without dying. Statistically, thirty percent of new recruits get eaten by the tutorial wolves."

"I have one hit point, Fizz," Artie wheezed, staring at the glowing blue text that stubbornly hovered in the top left corner of his vision.

[HP: 1/1]

"Just one," he muttered, his voice cracking. "A stiff breeze could kill me. A bad case of heartburn could kill me. If I trip on these cobblestones and scrape my knee, my heart will instantly stop."

"Well, technically, environmental damage is calculated differently," Fizz said, tapping her chin with a tiny finger. Her digital blue eyes sparkled with unhelpful enthusiasm. "A scraped knee is only point-two damage. But since the system rounds up to the nearest whole number... yes, you would die instantly."

Artie squeezed his eyes shut. "I need armor. I need a bubble. I need a medieval tank to crawl inside of."

He pushed off the wooden post, his Employee ID Badge swinging uselessly on its neck lanyard. The plastic card felt like a cruel joke from a past life. Beside it, permanently affixed to his lapel, the cursed Participation Trophy gleamed with an obnoxious, mocking gold sheen. 

The market was a chaotic sprawl of canvas tents, wooden carts, and permanent stone storefronts. Players and NPCs mingled in the thoroughfare, haggling over glowing potions, bundles of herbs, and weapons that looked far too large to be swung by human arms. Artie ignored the potion sellers and the food vendors, his eyes locked on the unmistakable glint of polished metal near the center of the plaza. 

He approached a massive stall draped in red velvet, where suits of armor stood like headless sentinels. The merchant, a burly man with a braided beard and arms the size of tree trunks, was currently polishing a massive tower shield.

"Welcome to Ironclad Defenses!" the merchant boomed, his voice echoing over the din of the market. "You look like a man who values his internal organs staying internal! What can I get you? Plate? Chain? We've got a fresh shipment of mithril-weave tunics in the back!"

"I need the thickest, heaviest, most impenetrable thing you have," Artie said, stepping up to the counter. He pointed at a massive, overlapping suit of steel plate armor that looked like it belonged on a rhinoceros. "That. I want that."

He reached out to touch the cold metal. The moment his finger brushed the steel, a translucent blue window snapped into existence.

[Item: Juggernaut's Fortress Plate]
[Defense: +150]
[Movement Speed: -40%]
[Level Requirement: 25]
[Price: 15,000 Gold]

Artie stared at the floating text. "Level twenty-five?"

"System locks, buddy," the merchant grunted, not looking up from his shield. "Can't have a level one novice walking around in endgame gear. The system would tear your muscles off the bone just trying to lift the pauldron. You gotta earn the right to wear the heavy stuff."

"But I need it now," Artie said, panic edging into his voice. "If I don't have it, I won't survive long enough to reach level two!"

"Not my problem," the merchant said, finally looking up. He took in Artie's cheap, ill-fitting office suit, the plastic ID badge, and the glowing trophy. He snorted. "Look, pal. You want starter gear? Check the bargain bin at the end of the counter. Don't waste my time with the display models."

Artie swallowed his pride and shuffled down to the end of the stall. A large wooden crate sat on the ground, filled with mismatched leather bracers, rusted chainmail coifs, and dented helmets. It smelled like old sweat and wet dog. 

He dug through the pile, pulling out a stiff, cracked leather vest. 

[Item: Moth-Eaten Leather Tunic]
[Defense: +2]
[Level Requirement: 1]
[Price: 45 Gold]

"Two defense," Artie whispered. "Fizz, what does two defense actually do?"

"It mitigates incoming physical damage by a flat value of two, before armor penetration calculations!" Fizz supplied cheerfully. "So if a goblin hits you for five damage, you only take three!"

"And I have one HP," Artie said flatly. 

"Correct! So you would still die instantly. But your corpse would be slightly less bruised!"

Artie dropped the tunic back into the bin. "Okay. Fine. This is fine. We just need to find something that gives me a shield. A magical barrier. Something that absorbs the first hit."

He left the armor stall and began weaving through the crowd, his eyes darting from vendor to vendor. He checked a stall selling magical rings. 

[Item: Ring of the Minor Ward]
[Defense: +10 Shield Capacity]
[Level Requirement: 5]
[Price: 800 Gold]

He checked a vendor selling enchanted cloaks.

[Item: Cape of the Zephyr]
[Evasion: +5%]
[Level Requirement: 3]
[Price: 300 Gold]

Every single item that offered even a shred of survivability was locked behind a level requirement he didn't meet, or a price tag that looked like a telephone number. The system was meticulously designed to force players to grind, to take risks, to slowly build their power through incremental upgrades. It was a perfectly balanced progression curve for a normal person. 

For a man with a glitched 1 HP stat, it was a death sentence.

"There has to be something," Artie muttered, pulling at his collar. The market was starting to feel claustrophobic. The clinking of coins, the laughter of high-level players showing off their glowing weapons, the smell of roasted meat—it was all overwhelming. "A starter shield. A wooden buckler. Anything."

"You currently possess exactly zero gold," Fizz reminded him, floating down to land lightly on his shoulder. "Even if you found a level one item with a warding effect, you couldn't afford it. The slime you vaporized dropped the cursed trophy, not currency."

Artie stopped in the middle of the thoroughfare, oblivious to the annoyed players shoving past him. He reached into his right pocket and pulled out his Leather Wallet. He flipped it open. 

Inside was his driver's license, a blocked corporate credit card, three crisp twenty-dollar bills, and a punch card for a sandwich shop that was three stamps away from a free turkey club. 

He stared at the paper money. It was just paper. It had no power here. No system value. 

With a heavy sigh, he shoved the Leather Wallet back into his pocket. Artie realized his useless wallet was exactly that—useless. The standard gear sold by these merchants wasn't going to save him, and their rigid economy offered no solution to his fragility. He couldn't buy his way out of this, and he couldn't grind his way out of it either. 

"Standard merchants are a dead end," Artie said, his voice dropping to a hollow whisper. "If I step outside the safe zone, Vane kills me. If a random mob looks at me funny, I die. I can't buy armor. I can't equip armor."

"Don't despair!" Fizz said, patting his cheek with a tiny, glowing hand. "The system is vast and full of opportunities! We just need to think outside the box."

Artie looked up at the towering spires of Oakhaven, feeling smaller and more fragile than ever. "Outside the box," he repeated. "Right. Because the box is currently trying to bury me."

Artie and Fizz left the sprawling merchant stalls of the market behind, their footsteps echoing on the cobblestones as they approached the Starter Quest Hub. The bustling plaza was a chaotic mess of novice adventurers waving wooden swords, but Fizz floated ahead, a tiny, ethereal system entity cutting a path toward a quieter corner of the hub. Her glowing neon pink hair and digital blue eyes pulsed with light as she zipped over the crowd.

Artie followed, pushing his messy brown hair out of his muddy brown eyes. His pale, skinny-fat frame looked entirely out of place among the armored warriors, his cheap suit offering zero protection. The cursed Participation Trophy gleamed mockingly on his lapel, right next to his mundane Employee ID Badge.

"There," Fizz said, her digital voice chiming. "If you can't buy armor, we have to exploit the tutorial mechanics."

Artie rubbed his temples. "Exploit how? I have zero gold and the durability of wet tissue paper."

"Look at that," Fizz instructed, pointing a pixelated finger at a massive, iron-banded trunk sitting on a raised wooden platform. A painted warning sign was nailed to the base.

Artie approached the heavy display chest. A blue holographic window hovered just above the reinforced lid.

[Starter Quest: The Unbreakable Lock]
[Objective: Learn the value of specialized skills by attempting to open this chest.]
[Requirement: Lockpicking Lvl 5 or Iron Key]
[Reward: 10 XP, Cloak of the Cowardly Lion]

"It's a lesson in failure," Fizz explained, zipping around the padlock. "The System puts this here to teach newbies that they can't brute-force everything. The lock is functionally unbreakable for anyone under level ten. But the cloak inside? It’s a defensive item meant for squishy rogue builds."

Artie stared at the massive steel padlock. "So, I need to find a rogue?"

"No," Fizz said, her digital eyes narrowing into glowing crescents. "You are a walking system error. You don't need lockpicking."

Artie pulled up his own status screen, the grim reality of his glitched build flashing before his eyes.

[HP: 1/1 | Stamina: 10/10 | Crit Rate: 100%]

He looked from his pathetic single hit point to the guaranteed critical strike. The System said the lock was unbreakable because it had a massive health pool and high defense. But a critical hit bypassed standard damage mitigation. If a simple kick could vaporize a slime and deal ninety-nine thousand damage, what would it do to a piece of tutorial hardware?

It was a terrible idea. If the chest had a damage reflection mechanic, his 1 HP would instantly vanish, sending him back to the void. But standing around in an oversized suit with a cursed golden pin wasn't going to keep him alive either.

He needed that cloak.

Artie glanced around the plaza, noticing a discarded Rusty Iron Dagger lying near a battered training dummy. He scooped it up, the coarse metal heavy in his right hand. It wasn't much, but it was better than breaking his knuckles.

Making his decision, Artie stepped past the painted warning sign. He gripped the hilt tightly, his eyes locked on the heavy iron padlock. Taking a deep breath, Artie prepared to strike the unbreakable chest.

Artie stood before the heavy tutorial chest, the Rusty Iron Dagger gripped tightly in his right hand, ready to test his glitched stats against the game's intended architecture. The chest sat in the center of the Starter Quest Hub, bolted to the cobblestones and heavily reinforced with steel bands. A massive, supposedly indestructible padlock secured the latch. 

"Go on," Fizz urged, hovering near his shoulder as a pulsing orb of neon pink light. "The system says that lock requires a Level 15 Thief with a Mythic lockpick. Let's see what a Level 1 accountant with a broken math engine can do."

Artie swallowed hard. He raised the Rusty Iron Dagger and brought it down in a clumsy, overarching stab. The pitted iron tip struck the center of the heavy padlock.

For a fraction of a second, the universe seemed to pause. Then, the lock detonated. 

[Critical Hit! 99,999 Damage!]

A shockwave of displaced air blasted outward, sending a shower of glowing digital shrapnel across the plaza. The heavy steel bands of the tutorial chest snapped like dry twigs, and the lid blew completely off its hinges.

[System Notification: Warning. Indestructible object destroyed. Error logging failed.]

Artie shielded his face with his free arm, coughing through the cloud of pixelated dust. As the smoke cleared, a single item hovered above the splintered remains of the chest, glowing with a soft, golden light. 

[Loot Acquired: Cloak of the Cowardly Lion]

Reaching out, Artie grabbed the Cloak of the Cowardly Lion. It was heavy, fashioned from a ridiculously oversized, faux-fur mane that immediately draped over his shoulders and fastened itself around his neck. It felt warm, and absurdly conspicuous. 

"Hey! You there! Stop!" 

Artie whipped around to see two heavily armored guild guards sprinting across the plaza, their halberds lowered. 

"Time to go!" Fizz chirped, darting toward a shadowed alleyway behind the guild hall. 

Artie didn't hesitate. Gripping his dagger, he turned and sprinted after the glowing fairy, his new oversized lion cloak billowing wildly behind him as he fled the scene of the crime.

## Loot Pinata Theory

Artie and Fizz evaded the shouting guards, slipping through a rusted iron grate and descending into the damp gloom of the Oakhaven Crypts to test his new gear. The heavy iron grate slammed shut behind them, the metallic clang echoing down the stone tunnel and abruptly cutting off the enraged shouts of the Adventurer's Guild.

Artie leaned against the cold, moss-slicked wall, his chest heaving. His pale, skinny-fat frame was completely unsuited for fleeing through medieval alleyways, let alone exploring subterranean dungeons. He brushed his messy brown hair out of his brown eyes.

[Status Effect Removed: Safe Zone Active]

The floating blue text hovered in Artie’s peripheral vision, a stark, terrifying reminder of his fragile mortality. He shivered, pulling the Cloak of the Cowardly Lion tighter around his shoulders. The garment was absurdly oversized, swallowing him entirely. He adjusted his grip on the Rusty Iron Dagger in his right hand, the metal cold and uncomfortably heavy.

"Are you absolutely certain this is a good idea?" Artie asked, his voice echoing slightly against the damp stone walls. "Because 'Safe Zone Active' just went away, and I am currently sitting at a grand total of one hit point."

[HP: 1/1]
[Stamina: 10/10]

Fizz floated lazily beside him, her neon-pink hair glowing like a tiny, pixelated beacon in the oppressive dark. Her digital-blue eyes sparkled against her translucent skin. "Relax, Artie! It’s a classic RPG exploit. High-level dungeon, high-tier loot, zero risk if you just don't get hit. The cloak provides a stealth modifier. You'll be practically invisible!"

Artie glanced down at himself. The Employee ID Badge still dangled from his neck lanyard, and the cursed Participation Trophy pinned to his lapel felt heavier than usual. "Practically invisible. Right."

He crept deeper into the tunnel. As they rounded a jagged corner, the narrow passage opened into a wide, ruined antechamber. In the center of the room stood a towering skeletal guard clad in rusted chainmail.

[Target: Skeletal Guard (Lvl. 8)]

"Okay," Artie whispered, his heart hammering against his ribs. "It's right there. What now?"

"Stealth time!" Fizz whispered back. "Keep to the shadows. The cloak's enchantment will mask your presence."

Artie took a cautious step forward. A soft, blue notification chimed in his vision.

[Stealth: Active (Flawed)]

Artie paused, his foot hovering an inch above the ground. "Flawed? Fizz, what does 'Flawed' mean?"

"Oh, it's just a minor modifier," Fizz said breezily. "It means the item's stealth is highly effective, but it has a... thematic drawback."

He didn't have to wait for an answer. As he shifted his weight to take his next step, the Cloak of the Cowardly Lion let out a loud, pathetic whimper. It sounded exactly like a frightened dog realizing it was at the vet.

The skeletal guard’s skull snapped up.

Artie panicked, trying to take a hasty step backward, but his heel came down hard on a brittle femur hidden beneath the dust. 

CRACK.

The cloak let out another loud, cowardly yelp, trembling violently against his back.

[Stealth Check: Failed]
[Aggro Gained: Skeletal Guard]

The heavy lion hood of the cloak slipped back, offering no concealment. Artie stood exposed as the skeleton turned its glowing eyes toward him.

Artie stared down the skeletal guard he had just aggroed, his heart hammering against his ribs in a frantic, terrifying rhythm. He scrambled backward across the damp stone of the crypt, his boots slipping on patches of slick, luminescent moss. The Cloak of the Cowardly Lion whimpering around his shoulders didn't help his nerves; the oversized garment trembled like a frightened dog, its magical stealth completely ruined by its own audible anxiety. 

The crypt itself was a masterclass in generic fantasy misery. Water dripped from the arched ceiling, pooling in uneven divots on the floor. The air smelled of ozone, old dust, and the distinct, metallic tang of dried blood. 

[Target: Skeletal Guard (Lvl. 8) – HP: 120/120]

The skeleton didn't care about the whimpering cloak. It raised a chipped, rusty iron sword, its red eyes burning with digital malice inside an empty skull. Every step it took produced a sickening clack-clack-clack of bone against stone. 

Artie gripped the Rusty Iron Dagger in his right hand so tightly his knuckles turned white. The weapon felt entirely useless. To use it, he'd have to get into melee range, and with 1 HP, a single blocked strike would kill him. 

The skeleton lunged, its bony feet clattering loudly as it closed the distance with terrifying speed. It swung the sword in a wide, horizontal arc aimed perfectly at Artie's midsection.

Artie threw himself backward, dodging the blade by a terrifyingly narrow margin. He dropped to one knee, his left hand blindly searching the debris-littered floor. His fingers brushed against broken glass, wet moss, and finally closed around a jagged, loose rock. 

He didn't think about his stance. He just cocked his arm back and hurled the stone with all the desperate, uncoordinated strength of a panicked accountant trying to survive a Monday morning audit. 

The rock sailed through the dank air and clocked the skeletal guard right between its glowing red eyes with a hollow, resonant thwack.

[Critical Hit! -99,999 HP]

The massive, glowing red numbers exploded into the air, illuminating the gloomy crypt like a distress flare. The skeletal guard froze mid-swing. Then, with a sound like a collapsing glass chandelier, the monster completely shattered into a fine mist of white dust and tiny, pulverized bone splinters. 

[Target Defeated.]

[Experience Gained: 150 XP]

Artie stayed on his knees, panting heavily. He slowly lowered the Rusty Iron Dagger, his chest heaving as the adrenaline began to ebb.

Fizz zipped down from the ceiling, her digital blue eyes wide and her neon pink hair flaring with static electricity. She hovered over the small pile of dust.

"Uh, Artie?" Fizz said, her voice tinged with systemic concern. "I think you broke its math."

"I threw a rock," Artie said, his voice trembling slightly as he stared at his empty left hand. "I didn't even throw it that hard."

"You have a one hundred percent critical hit rate," Fizz reminded him. "And your crit multiplier is currently returning a null value, which the system defaults to the maximum integer limit."

Before he could process the absurdity of that statement, the air above the remains began to violently glitch. A wireframe box materialized out of nowhere, rapidly cycling through item icons while emitting a high-pitched screech.

[System Error: Overkill threshold exceeded by 99,900%. Entity loot table rupture detected.]

[Warning: Physics engine stress test failed.]

[Dumping complete loot table...]

Artie scrambled backward again as a geyser of items erupted from the floor. It started with a fountain of gold coins, spraying outward like a busted fire hydrant and clattering loudly against the stone walls. But it didn't stop there. Glowing bone fragments, dozens of rusty swords, tattered leather scraps, minor health potions, and random pieces of low-level armor rained down in a chaotic, absurd cascade. 

"Take cover!" Fizz yelled, darting behind Artie's head. 

Artie threw his arms over his head, the Cloak of the Cowardly Lion whimpering loudly as it tried to wrap itself around his face for protection. The clatter of falling loot echoed through the crypt for a full ten seconds before finally tapering off.

When the dust finally settled, Artie slowly stood up. The crypt floor was completely unrecognizable. Where there had once been damp stone and a single pile of bone dust, there was now a mound of treasure that reached Artie's waist. 

He stepped closer, his boots crunching on a thick carpet of gold coins. He reached out and picked up a glowing blue bone fragment from the top of the pile. 

[Item: Flawless Femur of the Forgotten. Drop Rate: 0.01%.]

He dropped it back onto the pile. 

"Fizz," Artie muttered, his eyes wide as his accounting brain automatically began to calculate the gross value of the hoard. "Did it just drop everything?"

"Everything," Fizz confirmed, floating out from behind him. "Every item on its loot table. The overkill damage was so high that the system's loot generation formula experienced a buffer overflow. It couldn't calculate the probability, so it just gave you everything to prevent a fatal crash."

Artie stared at the absurd pile of loot left by the shattered skeleton. He wasn't just a glass cannon. He was a walking exploit. He could turn any low-level enemy into a localized economic crisis with a single tap. 

Artie chuckled, slipping the Rusty Iron Dagger into his belt. "Fizz, how many more skeletons are in this place?"

"According to the map? Hundreds," Fizz replied, narrowing her eyes. "Why?"

"Because I think I just found my new career path. We're going to need a bigger wallet."

Artie stared at the massive loot pile burying the shattered remains of the first skeleton, a slow, greedy realization dawning on him. If a single pebble could do that, he had just stumbled onto a highly repeatable exploit. He scooped up a handful of gravel from the damp floor, eyeing the other shambling skeletons deeper in the antechamber.

"Did you catch the math on that?" Artie asked, adjusting the oversized Cloak of the Cowardly Lion on his shoulders so it wouldn't snag his throwing arm. 

Fizz zipped over the mountain of gold coins and glowing bone fragments, her digital eyes wide. "It's a cascading math failure. The rock triggered a 99,999 damage critical hit, instantly shattering the skeleton. The engine couldn't handle it."

"So it panicked?"

"Exactly," Fizz said, dropping her voice to a conspiratorial whisper. "The massive overkill damage caused a system error, forcing the skeleton to drop its entire loot table at once."

Artie grinned. His 1/1 HP was a terrifying liability, but right now? It was the key to the kingdom. He checked that his Rusty Iron Dagger was securely stowed at his belt. He wouldn't need the blade. He had rocks. He wound up and lobbed a jagged piece of crypt masonry at a skeleton limping toward him from the shadows. The rock pinged off the monster's skull with a pathetic clack.

[Critical Hit! -99,999 HP]
[Target Defeated.]

The skeleton exploded into a shower of white light. A second later, the air filled with the heavy clinking of currency and the clatter of useless low-level gear.

[Experience Gained: 150 XP]
[Loot Drop: 42 Gold Coins, 14 Bone Fragments, 1 Rusty Iron Helm, 2 Minor Stamina Potions, 1 Skeletal Femur, 1 Tattered Tunic...]

"Oh, this is beautiful," Artie laughed, side-stepping a rolling skull. He threw another rock. Then another.

[Critical Hit! -99,999 HP]
[Target Defeated.]
[Experience Gained: 150 XP]

He developed a rhythm. Chuck a pebble, watch the skeleton vaporize, dodge the incoming avalanche of loot. The antechamber quickly transformed from a gloomy, damp crypt into a glittering hazard zone. He had to be careful not to trip over the knee-deep piles of copper and silver, or the sheer volume of rusty iron swords threatening to impale him. 

[Experience Gained: 150 XP]
[Experience Gained: 150 XP]
[Level Up! You are now Level 2.]
[Level Up! You are now Level 3.]

Artie paused, panting slightly as the golden glow of leveling washed over him. He opened his stat sheet.

[HP: 1/1 (Locked)]
[Stamina: 12/12]
[Stat Points Available: 10]

"Still locked at one HP," Artie sighed, dismissing the window. "But the stamina bump is nice."

"You're destabilizing the local instance," Fizz warned, hovering above a particularly tall stack of iron shields. "The physics engine is struggling to render all these individual coins."

"Just one more," Artie said, spotting a final Skeletal Guard cowering in the corner. Artie flicked a tiny chip of gravel.

[Critical Hit! -99,999 HP]
[Target Defeated.]
[Experience Gained: 150 XP]

A fresh geyser of loot erupted, cascading across the floor and burying the last patch of visible cobblestone. Artie stood amidst a ridiculous mountain of loot, having cleared the room of every last threat in the antechamber.
