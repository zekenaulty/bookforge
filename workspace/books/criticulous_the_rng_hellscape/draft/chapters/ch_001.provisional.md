# Chapter 1: Welcome to the RNG-Hellscape

## Death by Snacking

Artie’s stomach growled, a hollow, echoing rumble that cut through the sterile quiet of the office breakroom. He was aggressively hungry, the kind of mid-afternoon starving that made the humming fluorescent lights overhead seem to pulse in time with his mounting headache. It was exactly three-forty-two in the afternoon on a Tuesday, the absolute nadir of the corporate workweek. The spreadsheets he had been staring at for the last six hours had blurred into a meaningless matrix of black numbers on a white background, a digital purgatory from which there was no escape. Dragging his feet across the scuffed linoleum, his muddy brown eyes locked onto the glowing, humming monolith in the corner: the vending machine. It was a beacon of high-sodium salvation.

The breakroom of Miller, Stanton, and Associates was a depressing testament to mid-sized accounting firm culture. It smelled perpetually of burnt coffee and stale microwave popcorn. The walls were painted a color that could only be described as "despair beige," and the single window looked out onto a brick wall of the adjacent building. But Artie didn't care about the aesthetics. He cared about the calories. He needed fuel. His skinny-fat physique required sustenance to maintain its delicate balance of lethargy and anxiety. 

He ran a hand through his messy brown hair, adjusting the collar of his cheap, ill-fitting suit. The polyester blend was itchy against his pale skin, a constant reminder of his meager salary. Around his neck, his Employee ID Badge swung on its lanyard, a plastic pendulum marking the slow death of his late twenties. He reached into his right pocket and pulled out his Leather Wallet, his thumb flicking it open to extract a slightly crumpled dollar bill. It was his last physical dollar, a relic in an increasingly cashless society, saved specifically for this moment. He shoved the Leather Wallet back into his pocket, keeping his right hand free to smooth out the currency.

"Come on," Artie muttered, feeding the dollar into the machine's blinking slot. The bill validator whirred, a mechanical grinding sound that sounded like a robot clearing its throat. It spat the bill out with a dismissive buzz, demanding a flatter sacrifice. 

Artie sighed. He rubbed the bill against the sharp edge of the machine, trying to iron out the wrinkles with friction. He tried again. The machine swallowed it, thought about it for three agonizing seconds, and spat it out again. 

"Don't do this to me," Artie whispered, his voice cracking. He flipped the bill over, smoothing it against his thigh. He fed it in upside down. The machine whirred. It clicked. It swallowed the cash with a satisfying mechanical clunk. The digital display lit up with a glorious $1.00.

He punched in the coordinates for the barbecue potato chips. E-4. The alpha-numeric code of champions. It was the only acceptable snack in the entire machine. The pretzels were too dry, the chocolate was always slightly chalky, and the generic cheese crackers tasted like salted cardboard. But the barbecue chips? They were a symphony of artificial flavoring and grease. 

Inside the glass case, the metal coil began its slow, agonizing rotation. The silver packaging of the chips shimmied forward, reaching the precipice of the shelf. Artie leaned in, his pale face reflecting in the glass, anticipating the dull thud of delivered processed carbs. He could almost taste the smoky, salty dust on his fingers. 

The bag tipped. It slid. And then, defying all laws of physics and basic human decency, it caught on the lip of the shelf below. The coil stopped turning. The bag of chips hung suspended at a forty-five-degree angle, mocking him. 

Artie blinked. He tapped the glass with his knuckles. The stuck snack did not budge. He pressed his forehead against the cool pane, staring at the foil bag trapped just inches from freedom. His ten out of ten maximum stamina felt entirely drained by the sheer injustice of it all. He wasn't about to walk away and let the next guy get a two-for-one drop. 

He stepped back, rolling his shoulders. He glared at the stuck snack, weighing his options. Banging on the glass hadn't worked. Shaking the machine was risky; there were warning labels about tipping hazards, and he didn't want to be the guy who died for a bag of chips. But leaving was out of the question. He had invested his last dollar. He had invested his emotional well-being. 

He made his decision. If the machine wouldn't yield its treasure willingly, it was time for manual retrieval. He rolled up the sleeves of his cheap suit jacket, his eyes narrowed with determination. The dispenser flap at the bottom looked wide enough to accommodate his arm. It was a risk, sure. But it was a calculated risk. And Artie was, after all, an accountant.

Artie rolls up his sleeve and shoves his arm through the anti-theft flap of the vending machine. The heavy plastic door bites into his bicep, but the promise of barbecue chips demands sacrifice. He presses the side of his pale face against the cool glass, squinting through the glare of the humming fluorescent lights. His fingers stretch upward, blindly navigating the metallic landscape of the drop chute. 

His Employee ID Badge swings from its neck lanyard, clinking uselessly against the machine’s exterior as he leans harder into the narrow opening. The heavy weight of his Leather Wallet in his right pocket throws him slightly off balance, forcing him to plant his feet wider on the cheap linoleum floor. He grunts, straining his shoulder. Just a few more inches. 

His fingertips brush the crinkly foil of the chip bag. A small victory. 

Artie twists his wrist, attempting to pinch the bottom corner of the snack. Instead, his elbow bumps against a secondary internal guard—a rigid, spring-loaded piece of metal designed specifically to thwart this exact maneuver. He tries to quickly pull back, but the sudden movement triggers the mechanism. The metal plate snaps downward, pinning his forearm against the side of the drop chute. 

Panic spikes. He yanks his arm backward, a sharp jerk that only tightens the machine's mechanical grip. His cheap suit jacket bunches uncomfortably at the shoulder. He tries twisting his wrist to slide free, but his hand slips off the snack bag entirely and plunges deeper into the dark recess beside the dispensing coils. 

Something sharp scrapes across his knuckles. He pushes against the side chassis to find leverage, but there is no solid surface—only a gap in the interior paneling. His hand wedges tightly against a bundle of exposed wires, trapping him completely. A faint, ozone-scented hum vibrates against his bare skin.

Artie yanked his arm, but he was firmly trapped inside the vending machine, his knuckles wedged against a bundle of frayed, exposed wires. The compressor in the back of the rusted metal chassis kicked on with a heavy, mechanical shudder. 

A bright blue spark arced from the bare copper directly to his index finger.

[System Notification: Environmental Hazard Detected – Lethal Voltage.]

Every muscle in Artie's body locked up instantly. A massive surge of 120 volts of breakroom justice shot up his arm, bypassing his dangling Employee ID Badge and slamming straight into his chest. It felt like swallowing a lit firework. His teeth clamped together so hard he tasted copper, and his Leather Wallet felt heavy in his pocket as his legs seized.

[-10 HP]

[HP: 0/10]

The humming fluorescent lights above flickered, popped, and died. The stale smell of old coffee was instantly overwhelmed by the sharp, bitter stench of ozone and singed fabric. Artie couldn't breathe. He couldn't scream. 

[System Notification: Critical Failure. Subject is dead.]

His knees buckled. The dark breakroom faded into a blinding, featureless white. The agonizing heat vanished, abruptly replaced by a weightless, floating sensation that left him entirely numb. 

Artie blinked, staring into an endless, digital void. There was no breakroom. No vending machine. No unyielding bag of chips. Just an infinite expanse of glowing nothingness stretching in every direction. 

A crisp, translucent blue system prompt materialized in the empty air, hovering inches from his face.

[Initializing Character Creation...]

[Welcome to the RNG-Hellscape, Anomaly.]

## The Grease-Fire Build

Artie floats in the void, recovering from his electrocution, as a glowing pixelated fairy appears. The infinite white expanse stretched in every direction, devoid of horizon, floor, or ceiling. His limbs drifted weightlessly. The smell of ozone and burning breakroom plastic still lingered in his nostrils, a visceral reminder of the vending machine's lethal anti-theft defense. He patted his right pocket. The familiar rectangular lump of his Leather Wallet was still there, stowed safely away. Around his neck, his Employee ID Badge drifted on its cheap corporate lanyard, the plastic casing un-melted. 

[System Notification: Welcome to the RNG-Hellscape.]

The blue text hovered in the empty air, crisp and glowing. 

"Okay, so this is a coma," Artie muttered to the empty white void. "A stress-induced, snack-deprivation coma. I'm lying on the breakroom linoleum, and Linda from HR is probably giving me terrible CPR."

"Actually, Linda from HR is currently screaming because you voided your bowels when the voltage hit your heart," a voice chirped. 

Artie twisted in the non-gravity. A sphere of pink light pulsed a few feet away. It shimmered, the resolution dropping sharply until it coalesced into a glowing pixelated fairy. She had neon pink hair, digital blue eyes, and the aggressive cheerfulness of an unskippable tutorial NPC. 

"Hi there!" the fairy squeaked, doing a little mid-air loop. "I'm Fizz, your assigned system guide. Congratulations on your recruitment!"

"Recruitment?" Artie grabbed his Employee ID Badge, holding it like a tiny, useless shield before letting it drop back against his chest. "I'm an accountant. I don't get recruited. I get audited. Where am I?"

Fizz giggled, a sound like an 8-bit coin drop. "You're in the Character Creation Void! It's the standard holding area for Anomalies before they're injected into the server."

"Server? No. No, no, no." Artie squeezed his eyes shut. "Wake up. Wake up, Artie. The chips weren't worth it."

[HP: 0/10]
[Stamina: 10/10]
[Status: Dead]

The floating blue windows snapped into his field of vision, hovering aggressively just inches from his nose. 

"You can't wake up, silly," Fizz said, floating closer to tap the HP bar with a tiny, translucent finger. "You're dead. Super dead. Like, crispy-fried-nervous-system dead. The vending machine won."

Artie stared at the zero. Zero out of ten. It was a mathematically perfect failure. "I died over a bag of sour cream and onion chips."

"It was a very tragic electrocution," Fizz offered sympathetically. "But the good news is, your sudden and highly pathetic demise triggered an Anomaly flag! The System snatched your soul right out of the recycling bin. You get to play!"

"Play what?"

"The game!" Fizz threw her tiny arms wide. "A brand new life of adventure, stats, loot, and highly probable dismemberment! All you have to do is build your avatar."

Artie let out a long breath. The denial was fading, replaced by the cold, hard logic of an accountant looking at a hopelessly unbalanced ledger. He was dead. The firm wasn't going to pay out his life insurance if he died committing petty vending machine theft. There was no going back to his cubicle.

"Fine," Artie sighed, the last of his fight draining away into the infinite white. "If I'm dead, and this is hell, I might as well see the paperwork."

Fizz beamed. She snapped her fingers, and a massive, semi-transparent interface materialized in front of him. Sliders, point pools, and empty class slots blinked invitingly. 

Artie stares at the stat allocation screen, ready to build his character.

Artie examines the stat allocation menu Fizz just opened for him. The glowing blue interface hovered in the absolute blankness of the Character Creation Void, casting a cool light over his pale skin and messy brown hair. 

[Stat Allocation]
[Available Points: 10]
[Vitality: 1 (Max HP: 10)]
[Strength: 1]
[Agility: 1]
[Luck: 1]

"So, I just dump everything into Vitality, right?" Artie asked, reaching up to tap the floating screen. His plastic Employee ID Badge dangled from its neck lanyard, clinking softly against his chest. He shifted his weight, feeling the familiar, mundane lump of his Leather Wallet stowed in his right pocket—small comforts in an otherwise terrifying digital afterlife. "If I have more HP, I don't die again. Simple accounting."

Fizz zipped around his head, her neon_pink hair leaving a faint, pixelated light trail. "I mean, usually people balance it out, but considering you just got one-shot by a snack dispenser, I highly recommend beefing up your health pool. Go ahead, tap the plus sign."

Artie pressed his index finger against the glowing symbol next to Vitality. 

[Vitality: 2 (Max HP: 20)]
[Available Points: 9]

"See? Easy," Artie muttered. He tapped it again. And again.

On the fourth tap, the blue screen stuttered. A harsh, grinding static noise echoed through the void. The interface inverted its colors, flashing a violent, blinding red that reflected in Fizz's wide, digital_blue eyes.

[ERROR 404: Anomaly Integrity Breach]
[Unrecognized Soul Signature. Stat Allocation Overridden.]

"Whoa, hey!" Fizz shouted, her ethereal build flickering rapidly as she darted backward. "What did you do? The slider bar is jamming!"

"I didn't do anything!" Artie yelled, jamming his finger against the minus sign in a panic. "It's stuck!"

The red text flickered wildly, the numbers spinning like a broken slot machine. 

[Correcting Anomaly...]
[Vitality locked to 0.1]
[Max HP: 1]
[Compensating Build Deficit...]
[Critical Hit Rate set to 100%]

"One HP?" Artie stared at the flickering red text. "What kind of build has one HP? A stiff breeze is going to kill me!"

"I can't stop it!" Fizz yelled, pulling up rapidly scrolling logs that vanished as quickly as they appeared. A flashing warning icon pulsed above her head. "The system is forcing the character confirmation despite the errors!"

[Build Locked.]
[Initialization Complete.]

"Wait, no, cancel!" Artie shouted, waving his hands at the frozen screen. 

The system finalizes the glitched build, preparing to teleport Artie.

The glitch locks Artie's stats to a maximum of 1 HP and a 100% Critical Hit Rate, finalizing his build against his will. The infinite white expanse of the void echoed with a harsh, metallic chime as the system slammed the metaphorical door on his character sheet.

[System Notification: Anomaly Integrity Breach. Build Finalized.]
[HP: 1/1 (Locked)]
[Critical Hit Rate: 100% (Locked)]

"Cancel!" Fizz shrieked, her tiny, translucent hands flying across a holographic keyboard that materialized out of thin air. Her neon pink hair flared as she hammered at the glowing keys. "Abort! Ctrl-Z! Undo!"

[Error 404: Administrator Override Denied.]

Artie stared at the floating blue text. He was an accountant; he knew a catastrophic deficit when he saw one. "Fizz," he said, his voice terrifyingly calm. "What does one HP mean in this context?"

The pixelated fairy stopped typing. She slowly turned to face him, her digital blue eyes wide with panic. "It means if you trip on a root, you die. If a mildly aggressive bee stings you, you die."

Artie swallowed hard. He looked down at his chest. His Employee ID Badge still hung from the cheap nylon neck lanyard. He reached a trembling hand into his right pocket, his fingers brushing against the worn leather of his Leather Wallet. They were the only two things that had crossed over with him. 

"I'm going to die in a cheap suit," Artie whispered.

[Warning: Teleportation Sequence Initiated.]

The pristine void fractured, black gridlines appearing across the space. 

"Hold on to something!" Fizz yelled, diving toward him and grabbing onto the edge of his lanyard.

[Destination: Tutorial Forest.]
[Transferring User: Artie (Anomaly)]

The void collapsed. Artie felt like he was being shoved through a dial-up modem. His vision inverted, colors bleeding into a blinding spectrum of neon static. For three agonizing seconds, his entire existence was reduced to a stream of raw data.

Then, gravity returned with a vengeance.

Artie slammed hard into a patch of damp, spongy earth. He squeezed his eyes shut, fully expecting a system prompt to announce his immediate demise from fall damage. Nothing happened. He was still breathing. 

[Teleportation Complete.]

He cracked one eye open. The blinding white of the void was gone, replaced by the deep, shadowed greens of a dense woodland. The air smelled of pine needles and damp soil. Fizz floated upward, shaking her head to clear a halo of tiny, glitching stars. 

Artie pushed himself up onto his hands and knees. The dirt beneath his palms felt real. He patted his right pocket. The Leather Wallet was still there. He climbed to his feet, brushing a layer of pixelated moss off his slacks. 

Artie spawns in the tutorial forest with his glitched stats locked.

## The Participation Trophy

Artie and Fizz hit the grass of the Tutorial Forest spawn point, immediately stumbling into a gelatinous blue slime that squelched forward to initiate an encounter. The digital canopy above filtered low-resolution sunlight over the aggressive dessert as it jiggled menacingly. 

"Welcome to the combat tutorial!" Fizz cheered, her tiny ethereal form doing a loop in the air. Her neon pink hair flared brightly. "Please engage the hostile fauna to proceed."

Artie backed away rapidly, his cheap leather loafers slipping on the pixelated dirt. His pale, skinny-fat frame was rigid with absolute terror. He clutched his Employee ID Badge dangling from its neck lanyard like a protective talisman, his right hand hovering near the pocket holding his Leather Wallet. 

[Target: Basic Slime (Lvl. 1) - HP: 10/10]

"Fizz, I have one hit point," Artie hissed, his muddy brown eyes wide with panic. 

[Artie - HP: 1/1]

"It's a basic slime," Fizz sighed, her digital blue eyes rolling. "It does one point of damage. Just kick it. You literally can't miss."

"One point of damage is my entire life bar!" Artie shrieked. The slime pulsed, a red telegraph rectangle appearing on the ground directly at his feet. 

With nowhere left to retreat, Artie squeezed his eyes shut, let out a pathetic battle cry, and swung his right foot in a frantic, uncoordinated punt. His scuffed shoe connected with the jiggling mass.

[Critical Hit!]

The sound of the impact was deafening—a digital thunderclap that rattled Artie's teeth and shook the surrounding pine trees. A massive shockwave of distorted kinetic energy erupted from the point of contact.

[Damage: 99,999]

The massive red numbers clipped through the tree branches, hanging in the sky like an apocalyptic warning. 

[Target Defeated.]

[Experience Gained: 5 XP]

Artie opened his eyes. The slime was completely vaporized. The physics engine had simply deleted it from reality, unable to process the math of the absurd critical hit multiplier. Where the slime had been, a smoking crater remained, and hovering in the center was a small, golden loot drop spinning slowly on an invisible axis. 

Fizz floated down, her jaw hanging open. "Well. That's one way to clear a tutorial."

Artie cautiously approached the golden loot drop left in the crater of the vaporized slime. The air still smelled heavily of ozone and whatever chemical compound made up a gelatinous monster. At the center of the scorched earth, a small, glowing sphere hovered just inches above the dirt, pulsing with an inviting, warm light.

"Ooh, shiny!" Fizz did a mid-air loop, her neon-pink glowing hair trailing like a comet's tail. "That's a high-tier drop aura! You must have triggered an overkill multiplier when you did ninety-nine thousand damage to a level one mob. Touch it! Touch it!"

Artie hesitated, glancing down at his hands. He was still reeling from the fact that a single, panicked kick had practically erased a biological entity from existence. But the glowing orb was mesmerizing. It looked like a reward. He reached out, his fingers brushing the radiant surface. 

The golden light shattered like glass, dissolving into the air to reveal the item hidden within. It dropped into his waiting right hand with a pathetic, hollow clack.

Artie stared at it. It was a cheap, wooden Participation Trophy, complete with a peeling, gold-painted plastic star on top and a tattered red ribbon tied around the base.

[Item Looted: Participation Trophy]

"A trophy?" Artie muttered, turning the pathetic object over in his right hand. "I vaporized a monster with the power of a tactical nuke, and the system gives me a hunk of plastic?"

"Wait, let me scan it," Fizz said, floating closer, her blue pixel eyes narrowing. "Sometimes joke items have hidden stats—oh. Oh no."

Before Artie could ask what she meant, the Participation Trophy vanished from his right hand. 

A sharp weight suddenly dragged at the left side of his chest. Artie looked down. The Participation Trophy was now securely fastened to the lapel of his cheap office suit, pinning the fabric together. 

[Equip Notification: Participation Trophy has automatically equipped!]

"Hey, what the—" Artie grabbed the wooden base with his left hand and pulled. It didn't budge. He pulled harder, planting his feet and yanking with both hands. The fabric of his suit jacket stretched, but the Participation Trophy remained perfectly, immovably anchored to his chest.

[Item Stats: Participation Trophy]
[Rarity: Trash]
[Modifiers: +0 All Stats. At least you tried.]

"Get it off," Artie grunted, straining against the item. "Fizz, how do I unequip this?"

The fairy winced, her translucent form shrinking back slightly. "You... kind of can't. It's a binding drop."

A pulsing red system box materialized directly in front of Artie's face, flashing with an aggressive warning chime.

[Cursed Status: Participation Trophy cannot be unequipped, dropped, or sold. You are bound to your mediocrity.]

Artie released the Participation Trophy, his shoulders slumping. He had one hit point, a glitch that made him a walking glass cannon, and now he was permanently branded with an award for showing up. "This," he sighed, the plastic star catching the sunlight, "is going to be a very long afterlife."

## Oakhaven's Welcome

Artie reached the edge of the Tutorial Forest, the pixelated pine needles giving way to a worn dirt path, only to be intercepted by Vane. A shadow blotted out the digital sun as the massive figure stepped into the clearing. Vane was an overly designed fantasy warlord bristling with unnecessary armor spikes. A dark Guild Master Tattoo marked his left cheek, standing out starkly against his tanned skin and spiky silver hair.

"Well, well," Vane rumbled, his crimson eyes locking onto Artie's chest. "A golden-tier drop from a tutorial mob? You must have some insane RNG, noob."

Artie glanced down at his lapel. The cursed Participation Trophy gleamed with an obnoxious, unearned luster. "This? It's literally just a piece of plastic. It says 'You Tried' on the bottom."

"Don't play dumb," Vane sneered, crossing his thick, muscular arms. The massive Obsidian Greatsword strapped to his back scabbard shifted with the movement, the dark, glass-like blade humming with latent energy. "I've been farming this zone for three days. Hand it over, or I take it the hard way."

Before Artie could explain that the cursed item was permanently glued to his cheap suit, a red holographic window shattered the air between them.

[System Notification: Duel Request Received!]

[Challenger: Vane (Lvl. 15) – HP: 500/500]

[Condition: Deathmatch]

[Wager: Equipped Item (Participation Trophy)]

Artie stared at the floating red text, his heart hammering against his ribs. Level fifteen? Five hundred health? 

"Accept the duel," Vane demanded, his hand dropping to the hilt of his greatsword. "Or I flag up and player-kill you right here. Your choice."

Artie pulled up his own status bar.

[HP: 1/1]

Right. A single tap from that oversized butter knife, a stiff breeze, or even a particularly aggressive sneeze from the warlord would shatter Artie's single hit point into digital dust. His one-hundred-percent critical hit rate wouldn't matter at all if he was sliced in half before he could even throw a punch. 

Artie's brown eyes darted past Vane's spiked shoulder pads, scanning the fortified stone walls in the distance. The heavy iron gates of Oakhaven were just down the path, bustling with the noise of a passing merchant caravan. 

He swallowed hard. Artie realized he could not win a fair fight and looked for an escape.

Realizing he cannot win a fair fight against the towering swordsman, Artie looks for an escape, his cheap suit shoes slipping against the dirt path. Vane stood directly between him and the fortified gates of Oakhaven, a massive Obsidian Greatsword resting casually over one armored shoulder. 

[System Alert: Duel Request from Vane – Deathmatch]

The prompt hovered in the air like a glowing red stop sign. Artie swallowed hard. He didn't need to check his stats, but his peripheral vision helpfully flashed his most terrifying metric anyway.

[HP: 1/1]

One hit. A glancing blow, a stiff breeze, or a particularly aggressive sneeze from the armored giant would send Artie straight back to the void. He patted his chest, feeling the heavy lump of his Leather Wallet safely stowed in his right pocket and the plastic edge of his Employee ID Badge dangling from his neck lanyard. Then, he glanced down at his lapel. The cursed Participation Trophy pulsed with a faint, mocking golden light, drawing Vane's greedy gaze.

"Just accept the duel, Anomaly," Vane sneered, taking a heavy, deliberate step forward. "I'll make it quick. I just want the drop."

"I'd love to, really," Artie said, backing up, "but my schedule is packed. Lots of synergizing to do. Quarterly reviews."

Before Vane could swing, a cacophony of snorts, creaking wood, and shouting erupted from the main road. A massive merchant caravan—comprised of six oversized wagons pulled by six-legged, scaly beasts of burden—barreled toward the iron gates. Dust kicked up in thick, choking clouds.

"Make way!" a merchant screamed from the lead wagon, cracking a glowing whip. 

It was the only chance. Artie threw himself forward, not at Vane, but toward the side of the passing caravan. He squeezed between a massive, scaly flank and the spinning wooden wheels of the second wagon, instantly breaking Vane's line of sight.

"Hey! Get back here!" Vane roared, the heavy thud of his boots momentarily drowned out by the bellowing draft beasts.

Artie didn't look back. He kept his head down, using the massive wagons as a moving wall. He matched their frantic pace as they rolled over the cobblestone threshold, slipping right past the heavily armed city guards. The heavy iron portcullis loomed overhead, casting a brief shadow before Artie burst out into the bustling, sunlit expanse of the central square.

A soft blue chime rang in his ears, instantly shattering the blaring red of the duel challenge.

[Area Discovered: Oakhaven City Proper]
[Safe Zone Active. PvP Combat Disabled.]

Artie collapsed against a wooden fruit stall, gasping for breath. He checked his lapel. The Participation Trophy was still there, but more importantly, he was still breathing. He had made it into the city.
