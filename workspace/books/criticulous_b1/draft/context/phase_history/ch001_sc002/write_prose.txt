Arthur 'Artie' Penhaligon stood in the blinding white expanse, still clutching his Bag of Salt-and-Vinegar Chips, as a flickering blue light coalesced before him. The void was absolute, a featureless desert of unrendered data that made his eyes ache and his stomach do a slow, nauseous somersault. He looked down at the bag in his right hand. The crinkle of the plastic was the only sound in the silence, a mundane, terrestrial noise that felt entirely out of place in the afterlife.

"Is that... salt and vinegar?" 

The voice was high-pitched, vibrating with a digital filter that sounded like a speaker with a loose wire. The flickering blue light snapped into a solid form—a tiny, glowing sprite with a neon blue bob cut and wings that left trails of pixelated dust in the air. She hovered at eye level, her translucent blue arms crossed over a chest that barely existed.

Artie blinked, his crooked glasses sliding down the bridge of his nose. "It was the last thing I bought. Well, technically I died for them. I’m not sharing."

"Oh, believe me, I don't want your grease-packets, Arthur," the sprite said, her glowing white eyes rolling with practiced theatricality. "I'm Fizz. Your System Guide. Or, as the Admin likes to put it, your 'Mandatory Orientation Interface for the Computationally Challenged.' Though, looking at your cause of death, I think 'Defective' is a better label for both of us."

A translucent blue window popped into existence between them, humming with a low-frequency buzz.

[Welcome, User: PENHALIGON, ARTHUR]
[Status: DECEASED (Pending Rehydration)]
[Initialization Phase: Character Creation]

"Rehydration?" Artie asked, peering at the screen. "That sounds like something you do to dried mango. Can we skip to the part where I’m not a ghost?"

"You're not a ghost, you're a packet of unsaved data," Fizz corrected, hovering closer to the screen. "And if you want to be 'rehydrated' into a world that isn't just a white box, you need to assign your stats. Since you managed to get yourself electrocuted by a snack machine, the System has granted you a 'Pity Bonus' of one hundred points. Try not to spend them all on 'Bad Decision Making,' if that's even a stat."

Artie ignored her, his fingers hovering over the glowing text. The interface was sluggish, trailing ghost-images as he moved his hand.

[Available Stat Points: 100]
[HP: 1/1] (Warning: Critical Low)
[Strength: 0]
[Dexterity: 0]
[Intelligence: 0]
[Luck: 0]
[Critical Hit Rate: 0%]

"Wait, why is my HP one?" Artie pointed at the digit. "One? A stiff breeze would delete me. I’ve seen office plants with more constitution than that."

"Well, you did arrive in a state of 'Total Biological Failure,'" Fizz said, examining her pixelated tattoo on her left forearm. "The System is generous, but it's not a miracle worker. You want more health? Put points into it. Or don't. I personally think the 'One-Hit Wonder' look suits your aesthetic."

Artie grumbled, shifting the Bag of Salt-and-Vinegar Chips to his left armpit so he could use both hands. His fingers were slick with a fine coating of salt and potato-grease—the spoils of his final victory over the vending machine. He reached for the 'HP' bar, intending to dump at least fifty points into not-dying.

"Just a tip," Fizz added, her voice dripping with mock-helpfulness. "The 'Luck' stat is usually where people like you fail. It’s hard to calculate 'Pathetic' into a winning formula."

"I'm not putting points into Luck," Artie snapped. "I'm putting them into—"

As his finger touched the 'HP' icon, a sudden, sharp glitch rippled through the screen. The blue light flared, and the grease on Artie's fingertip acted like a conductive bridge across the sensitive UI. The 'Critical Hit Rate' bar, located directly beneath the HP icon, began to skyrocket.

[Critical Hit Rate: 10%]
[Critical Hit Rate: 45%]
[Critical Hit Rate: 88%]

"Whoa! Hey! Stop!" Artie poked at the screen, but the grease smear was wreaking havoc on the digital sensors. The numbers blurred in a rapid-fire ticker. 

"What are you doing?" Fizz leaned in, her eyes wide. "Are you speed-running your own extinction?"

"The screen is sticking! It’s the salt!" Artie tried to wipe the interface with his sleeve, but the friction only seemed to confirm the selection. 

[DING!]
[System Notification: Stat Allocation Confirmed]
[Available Stat Points: 0]

Artie froze. The screen settled, the blue glow pulsing with a finality that made his heart—or whatever was currently simulating his heart—sink.

[HP: 1/1]
[Strength: 0]
[Dexterity: 0]
[Intelligence: 0]
[Luck: 0]
[Critical Hit Rate: 100%]

[New Trait Acquired: Crit or Quit]
[Description: You have achieved the statistical impossibility of a 100% Critical Hit Rate at Level 1. In exchange, your HP is locked at 1. You are the ultimate glass cannon. Don't trip over a pebble.]

The silence that followed was broken only by a single, sharp laugh from Fizz. She doubled over in mid-air, her wings buzzing so fast they became a blur. "One hundred percent? You absolute madman. You’re a walking math error! You literally cannot land a normal hit, but a sneeze will turn you into confetti!"

"It was an accident!" Artie shouted, waving his chips at the screen. "Reset it! Give me a 'Back' button!"

"No can do, Arthur," Fizz gasped, wiping a digital tear from her eye. "The System doesn't do 'backsies.' You've locked in the build. You’re the first-ever 'Crit or Quit' pioneer. I give you three minutes in the tutorial before a butterfly kills you."

"I have a hundred percent crit rate," Artie muttered, trying to find the silver lining in a cloud made of razor wire. "That means I hit for maximum damage every time, right?"

"Sure. If you actually manage to hit anything before it looks at you funny," Fizz said, her sarcasm returning to its baseline. "But hey, look on the bright side. You’ll have the most spectacular death in the history of the server. I’ll make sure to recording it for the highlight reel."

[Character Creation Complete]
[World: Oakhaven (Tutorial Zone)]
[Spawning in 3... 2... 1...]

"Wait, I'm not ready!" Artie gripped his chips tighter. "I need a weapon! Or a suit of armor! Or a very thick bubble-wrap suit!"

"Good luck, Crit-boy," Fizz chimed, her form beginning to flicker as the void around them shuddered. "You're going to need it. Or actually, you won't, because you have zero points in Luck. This is going to be hilarious."

With a final, sarcastic chime from Fizz, the white void began to pixelate and dissolve into the green of a forest. The blinding light was replaced by the dappled shade of oak trees, and the smell of ozone vanished, replaced by the scent of damp earth and looming, low-level danger.
