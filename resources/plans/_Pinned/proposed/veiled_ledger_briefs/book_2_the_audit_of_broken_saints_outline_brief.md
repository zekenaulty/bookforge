# Outline Brief: Book 2 - The Audit of Broken Saints

Book ID: `veiled_ledger_b2`  
Title: `The Audit of Broken Saints`  
Author Ref: `eldrik-vale/v2`  
Series ID: `veiled-ledger`  
Genre: `litrpg, urban_fantasy, political_mystery`

## High-Level Role
Middle book. Shift the series from frontier exploitation to civic and institutional corruption without losing Eldrik's clarity, humor, and mechanical credibility.

## One-Sentence Pitch
In a city powered by miracle accounting and holy tariffs, Rhea uncovers evidence that the church, guilds, and civil bureaucracy are all laundering power through the same divine-seeming system they claim merely to administer.

## Core Story Direction
This book should prove the series can survive outside pure combat loops. The action comes from audits, negotiations, infiltration, leverage, records, faction conflict, and civic pressure. The System must still matter, but it should not dominate every page with visible UI. The city should feel orderly on the surface and predatory in the ledger underneath.

## What Must Happen
- Rhea arrives in a major city and immediately feels the difference between frontier chaos and institutional control.
- She discovers that resurrection fees, healing access, guild certifications, and civic privilege all tie back to controlled system records.
- Multiple factions compete for her skills or want her silenced.
- Evidence emerges that corruption is structural, not just personal greed.
- A meaningful cast relationship is strained by secrecy, leverage, or ideological difference.
- Rhea gains access to hidden records or restricted system infrastructure.
- The book ends with proof that the Ledger is patched, failing, manipulated, or all three.

## What Must Not Happen
- Do not revert to frontier pacing just because it is easier.
- Do not make the city corruption generic “evil church” shorthand.
- Do not spray blue-box UI into scenes that should stay social or procedural.
- Do not flatten factions into simple good/evil camps.

## Tone Mix
- 25% progression/comedy
- 40% systems mystery
- 25% political pressure
- 10% dread

## Expected Structural Shape
Target around 8 chapters. Reasonable shape:
1. arrival, orientation, city pressure
2. first audit trail, first institutional wall
3. faction split: guild / church / civic / underground
4. public pressure, private proof
5. records breach, raid, or hidden archive access
6. trust fracture within the core cast
7. confrontation with incomplete victory
8. reveal of deeper infrastructure and next-book setup

## Key Tensions
- truth vs survival inside institutions
- reform vs capture
- procedural legality vs moral legitimacy
- system faith vs system exploitation
- competence vs isolation

## Setting Priorities
- the city must feel administratively alive
- tariffs, queues, permits, holy accounting, district differences, and guild politics should matter
- miracles should have visible cost structures

## Character Priorities
- Rhea should stay sharp and dry, but carry residue from Book 1
- supporting cast should not reset emotionally at book start
- factions should be populated by people with coherent motives

## Mechanical Priorities
- UI should appear only when system-active context justifies it
- mechanics should influence law, class, debt, and institutional access
- progression can slow slightly, but it cannot vanish

## Ending Target
Rhea leaves the book with hard proof that the Ledger's corruption is tied to hidden infrastructure rather than isolated bad actors, setting up the world-engine conflict of Book 3.
