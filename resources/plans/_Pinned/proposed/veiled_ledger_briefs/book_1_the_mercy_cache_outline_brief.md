# Outline Brief: Book 1 - The Mercy Cache

Book ID: `veiled_ledger_b1`  
Title: `The Mercy Cache`  
Author Ref: `eldrik-vale/v2`  
Series ID: `veiled-ledger`  
Genre: `litrpg, progression_fantasy, dark_comedy`

## High-Level Role
Opening book. Establish the protagonist, the Ledger, the progression loop, the exploit loop, and the fact that the frontier onboarding system is predatory by design.

## One-Sentence Pitch
Rhea Mercer dies in a municipal records fire and wakes in a predatory tutorial frontier where every starter system is built to skim value from new arrivals, forcing her to survive by auditing reality faster than reality can kill her.

## Core Story Direction
This is a progression-survival book with real humor and real pressure. The protagonist wins by reading rules, tariffs, cooldowns, inventory wording, quest conditions, and payout structures like fraud instruments. Every exploit should feel clever, specific, and costly enough to stay believable. The frontier should not feel like a generic RPG starter zone. It should feel like a rigged administrative environment pretending to be merciful.

## What Must Happen
- Rhea dies and is transferred into the Ledger-run frontier onboarding environment.
- She learns the starter systems are exploitative, not merely difficult.
- She earns early survival through careful reading of rules rather than brute power.
- A small supporting cast begins to form around shared incentives, necessity, or mutual damage.
- At least one familiar / clerk / helper figure exposes administrative residue that should not exist at Rhea's access level.
- Rhea's victories scale from petty survival gains to meaningful structural interference.
- By the end of the book, she proves the exploit pattern is systemic, not local.
- The ending hands off cleanly to a city / civic / institutional layer for Book 2.

## What Must Not Happen
- Do not make Rhea a chosen one by fiat.
- Do not solve problems with vague system favor or destiny language.
- Do not replace local stakes with cosmic exposition too early.
- Do not make the humor cancel the danger.
- Do not let progression come free of social or economic consequence.

## Tone Mix
- 50% progression/comedy
- 30% survival/tactical pressure
- 20% mystery/dread

## Expected Structural Shape
Target around 8 chapters. Reasonable shape:
1. death, transfer, first exploit opportunity
2. first survival loop, food/shelter/currency friction
3. ally/rival/familiar entanglement
4. discovery that standard rewards are being skimmed or distorted
5. mid-book escalation where exploit use becomes visible to local power
6. authority pressure, debt logic, and administrative cruelty
7. frontier climax with clear systemic leak or access breach
8. transition out of the frontier into the wider civic world

## Key Tensions
- survival vs dignity
- optimization vs trust
- local exploit wins vs institutional retaliation
- humor vs accumulating damage
- mercy framing vs extraction reality

## Setting Priorities
- tactile, procedural, and materially specific
- frontier systems should have tariffs, queues, inventory limits, and bad incentives
- locations should matter tactically and economically, not just visually

## Character Priorities
- Rhea is competent, observant, irritated, and not romantically naive about institutions
- supporting cast should each have clear incentives, not generic loyalty
- humor should emerge from character logic under pressure

## Mechanical Priorities
- explicit system logic when the System is active
- clear stat / skill / reward relevance
- exploits must follow established rules
- loot and inventory should matter materially

## Ending Target
Rhea leaves the frontier with enough proof, power, and social consequence to justify entering a city where the real abuse of the Ledger is institutional rather than local.
